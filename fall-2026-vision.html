North star — locked 2026-07-18 · working language, not student-facing

A student builds a repeatable process for finding — and continually updating — the place where something they genuinely care about, something they can get good at, and something the world needs overlap, then follows through on what that turns up, past comfort, past friction, for real.

Honest bar: one real rep of surface → deeper → sustained pursuit — a reusable method for life. The first answer is a door, never a wrong answer. Genuine care is demonstrated through going past friction, not asserted.
Open question (Sathya, 7/20): what understanding, attitude, mindset, or aptitude does a student need before the Ikigai questions are even answerable — or is attempting them the best starting point? Current design bet: never ask the questions cold — build each circle from evidence (Layer 2). The Sprint-0 baseline is how Fall answers this empirically.

Layer 2 · Design principlesThe few moves that produce the outcomes — 4 shared + 3 per course. Term "principles" locked 7/18 (was "guards").

Locked 7/18

Layer rule

Every principle cashes out as a repeatable process the student learns to rerun — adaptability builds on repeatable processes.

Shared (both courses)

  • 1 · Build caring from evidence, via divergence. Three moments (not one) where they lost track of time or got annoyed enough to act, spanning their life; light scaffolding (memory-jog categories); AI-as-mirror names the thread across the three. Protects against: the comfort default — the first quick answer.
  • 2 · Widen to the overlap, then develop the skill — don't test for it. Two beats, in order: care surfaces first, world-needs widens it second — never one combined "find what you care about that the world needs" prompt. Student lands on 1–3 candidate pursuits at the overlap; the course helps them build the skill. Protects against: usefulness-filtering up front; up-front skill bars.
  • 3 · Follow-through past friction is the graded spine. Demonstrations of follow-through ARE the evidence of actual care — and where "good at it" gets answered, since skill develops and shows itself in the same place. Protects against: performed sincerity; asserted passion.
  • 4 · Revisit, not just start. "Continually updating" needs a real recurring checkpoint — CST349's start/mid/end Goal Plan revision is the shared candidate mechanism. Protects against: one-and-done discovery.

Architecture (7/18)

The design layer is modality-independent — the asynchronous content layer. Synchronous sessions (in-person or online) are built around conversations and engagement with the digging, at session-design time in Build.

CST286

  • 1 · Physics is an instrument, not a destination. Success = depth reached and what it did for the pursuit — never physics coverage.
  • 2 · Close the loop. Every dig lands back in the pursuit: understanding the mechanism visibly changes what the student does, makes, or chooses.
  • 3 · Transfer is designed, not hoped for. The dig process reruns on a second interest — depth as a discipline is experienced, not asserted.
  • Guardrail: interest selection stays genuinely open — never steer toward "topics that tie to CS/your career."

CST349

  • 1 · The pursuit is the student's own professional becoming — declared openly. (Contrast by design: the professional lens is a trap in 286, the subject in 349. Neither course smuggles it.)
  • 2 · Growth is defined comprehensively before it's chased. Multiple dimensions, a metric per dimension — SDL-dimensions material is the scaffold.
  • 3 · The weekly milestone loop is the follow-through spine — and the student runs it. The directing is the graded skill; instructor, AI, peers are mirrors, not judges.

Layer 3 · EvidenceOne deepening arc, student-defined evidence, verified in prepared face-to-face walkthroughs.

Locked 7/18–20

Shared commitments (E1–E3)

  • E1 · One deepening arc is the evidence. Fewer, connected demonstrations, each showing "further than last time" — not points accumulated across many assignments.
  • E2 · Students define their own evidence, within a scaffold. Each student proposes what will convince a skeptic they went deeper and got better; refined against mirrors and exemplars; instructor approves the plan, never dictates it.
  • E3 · Conversations are evidence-collection moments. 2–4 one-on-one or small-group face-to-face conversations (Zoom with video on counts) — doubling as principle 4's revisit checkpoints.

Sprint macro-arc — both courses (7/18)

Sprint 0 find + evidence plan + readiness baseline → Sprints 1–2 going deeper (the single-pursuit arc) → Sprint 3 testing transfer (the process rerun on a second interest/dimension) → wrap synthesis & final demonstration.

Cadence — both courses (7/20)

One course assignment per 1–2 weeks. A course assignment may span 2–3 Canvas assignments as cumulative installments — continuity, one evolving piece of work. The check-in goes deep on one drawn assignment.

Verification — the anti-fake mechanism (7/18–20)

  • 50/50: half the points on submission; the other half earned in the live meeting, walking through 3–5 past evidences. Nothing can be submitted and forgotten.
  • Per-window scoring: the meeting covers everything since the last one; sampled artifacts are the probe; the score applies to the window (kills the volume bet).
  • Sticky losses: a shaky walkthrough loses points and at minimum half the loss is permanent — come prepared. (Full vs half-recoverable = open parameter.)
  • Bandwidth rule: class time preferred, by appointment; full points via office hours if present but unreached — nobody loses points to the instructor's calendar.
  • Framing: "walk me through your dig," never an interrogation. Heavy AI use is not a violation; the question is whether the understanding stuck.
  • This mechanism is itself a Fall learning question — "something along those lines needs to be tested for the age of AI."

Grading calls

  • Grade the delta, not the altitude (286): "deeper" is measured from each student's own Sprint-0 starting point — no absolute physics bar.
  • Grade the loop, not the line (349): the outcome is a working feedback loop — an honest miss plus adaptation counts, sometimes more than an easy win.

Per-course evidence standards

  • CST286: Mechanism ("I can show how it really works" — probed live with what-ifs) · Payoff ("better at it because of the depth" — before/after with the causal trace) · Process ("I could run this dig again" — documented; Sprint 3 is the proof).
  • CST349: Definition ("I know what growth means for me" — dimensions, metrics, baselines) · Movement ("the metrics moved and I can show why") · Direction ("I ran the loop myself" — Goal Plan revisions; Sprint 3 reruns the loop).
