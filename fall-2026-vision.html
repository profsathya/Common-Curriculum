Fall 2026 — Course Vision

CST286 · Physics of Computing (new build)  ·  CST349 · Professional Seminar (re-run refresh)  ·  CST499 · Capstone (support only, Paige leads). A living map: what we want for students, how the design gets there, what we measure, and the tasks each piece implies.
North star — above all the scaffolding

Students find something they genuinely care about — deep, not the comfort default — and follow through on it, past their comfort zone.

Honest bar: one real rep of surface → deeper → sustained pursuit — a reusable method for life, not "find your calling in 15 weeks." The shallow first answer is a door, never a wrong answer.

Level 1 · Identity & growth

Who the student becomes — the layer everything else serves.
▶

Caring built from evidence, proven by follow-throughThe north star, made teachable — three design guards raise the floor without dictating content.

In design

What we're trying to achieve

Move each student from a surface interest ("I like video games" / "the cookie") to a specific question or stake they own, then through sustained pursuit past friction. Identity question ("what does the evidence say I care about, and who would I become chasing it?"), not a preference question.

How

  • Build caring from evidence — surface moments they lost track of time or got annoyed enough to act; AI as first mirror, peer/instructor as second; what-questions, not why-questions.
  • Force specificity until a real stake appears — "video games" → for whom, why, to what end — where shallow either deepens or reveals itself.
  • Follow-through past friction is the graded spine — shallow caring doesn't survive the stretch, so sincerity is never policed up front.
  • Validate the door — the first answer is a fine start; "let's find what's underneath," never "pick something deeper." Protects first-gen / transfer students who carry identity threat (~56% first-gen, ~45% transfer, 60+ feeder institutions).

What we measure

  • Movement across Goal Plan revisions (start → mid → final): vague interest → specific, staked, tractable pursuit.
  • Follow-through visible in artifacts: measurable weekly milestones set, measured, and adapted (missed milestone = data, not failure).
  • Self-check deltas — beginning vs. end of course.

Tasks

  • North-star framing + three guards + "door" refinement captured (6/30)
  • Pressure-test the design once more against both course arcs
  • Define the milestone loop as a student-facing structure (Goal Plan + sprint reflections)

CF-3 · queued

What we're trying to achieve

Students internalize self-directed learning, working with AI as a thinking partner (creating vs. consuming), and disciplined problem-solving — as judgment they own, not checklists they fill in. Scaffold fades by compression: fewer, more general principles replace templates as students progress.

How

  • One shared concepts reference both courses point at (single source, no drift).
  • Sequencing fix: master 5 Whys / UMPIRE before meeting 3Cs — never every framework in Sprint 1 (GAP-5: conceptual overload is documented).
  • UMPIRE with design pressure on Map (students jump to doing) and Review/Evaluate (where quality lives; students skip them).

What we measure

  • Artifact-embedded pressure tests: the artifact itself asks "what's missing in this evidence?"
  • Rubrics score the student's contribution — claims, evidence, connections — never the AI's conversation arc (4.3 vs 2.1 lesson from spring).
  • Declining scaffold reliance across the three Goal Plan revisions.

Tasks

  • Build shared concepts reference (CF-3)
  • Design the framework sequence per course (what appears in which sprint)
  • Shallow-vs-deep exemplar pairs, starting with 5 Whys (CF-5, used in Sprint 1 of both)

CF-4 · queued

What we're trying to achieve

Every student starts with working AI habits: AI as partner, not answer machine. Struggle lands on judgment (what to claim, cut, count as evidence) — never on mechanics or English (for ESL / first-gen students, fighting their English is a barrier, not a struggle).

How

  • Shared Sprint 0 module: AI-free phases as architecture, Dojo norms with a self-check, hand-written reflection policy (prevents AI bypass).
  • Intake gated on completed work, run in Canvas (not open forms).

What we measure

  • Sprint 0 self-check completion + quality.
  • Reflection authenticity (hand-written policy trade-off accepted: ~60% unanalyzable by script — humans read them).

Tasks

  • Build shared Sprint 0 module (CF-4)
  • Add intake questions: optional phone + explicit consent to text from TA + other courses this term (decided 7/2 — ask students, don't scrape SIS; transparency that we may reach out there)
  • Adapt for CST286 (named Sprint 0 exists) and CST349 (needs the equivalent)

Level 4 · Operations & the human safety net

Don't lose anyone as the semester progresses.
▶

Daily playbook loop: automate mechanics, keep judgment humanScheduled Cowork task reads the playbook daily — nudges run alone; grades and outreach wait for Sathya.

Needs Sathya

What we're trying to achieve

Superagency + human value in our own process: AI drafts, human judges. Every student who drifts gets noticed early — by a person, not just a system. The same struggle-on-judgment principle the courses teach, applied to how we run them.

How

  • Daily scheduled task driven by daily-playbook-v0.md (the playbook, not code, is the source of truth; Sathya edits it anytime).
  • A-series (fully automated): asset-framed template nudges + reminders, rate-capped, all logged.
  • H-series (human-in-the-loop): H1 grading drafts → Sathya reviews → post + verify; H2 weekly attention list; H3 TA action items — the human connection layer: drafted personal text/Slack/email, 1-on-1 invites with questions tailored to the student's Goal Plan, catch-in-class plans. TA outcomes feed next week's list.
  • Review is async between runs; approved items advance on the next run.

What we measure

  • Recovery rate: students who respond / resubmit after nudge or TA contact.
  • Nobody unreached: every 2+-missing student has a logged human touch within a week.
  • Grading turnaround time with judgment intact.

Tasks

  • v0 playbook drafted: daily loop, A1/A2/A3, H1/H2/H3, guards (7/2)
  • Sathya: data-boundary decision (packets hold names + phones — sync across machines or local-only?)
  • Sathya: bless / rewrite A1 + A2 message templates
  • Decide TA delivery channel (email / Slack)
  • Archive or delete Common-Curriculum-Data; disable nightly-grading.yml + analyze-submissions.yml
  • Schedule the daily task in Cowork (only after the above)

In design

What we're trying to achieve

A student who has climbed every level of abstraction — physics → logic → architecture — stops seeing computers (and by extension, technical systems) as magic.   • What students choose in the open stretch — evidence of caring beyond the requirement.

Tasks

GAP-1/3 · Students describe instead of analyze~70% of 5 Whys chains circle or rephrase; no quality reference point exists.

Refresh target

Achieve

Students recognize deeper-vs-sideways before producing — analysis, not description.

How

  • Bad-chain vs good-chain exemplars students diagnose first (recognition before production; feeds CF-5).
  • Teach the "level change" move: "what would have to be structurally different for this problem to disappear?"
  • Pre-submission revision check embedded in the assignment.

Measure

% of chains that deepen (spring baseline: ~30% deepen). Target: clear movement, measured by the contribution-scoring rubric.

Tasks

  • Build 5 Whys exemplar pairs (CF-5)
  • Add diagnose-first activity to Sprint 1
  • Embed revision check in the assignment page

GAP-4 · Evidence read as feelings"I feel more confident" isn't evidence of change.

Refresh target

Achieve

Students point to a specific behavioral moment as evidence ("last Tuesday I did X where I would have done Y").

How

Reframe the evidence-of-change prompt to demand a moment, not a feeling; exemplar pair shows the difference.

Measure

Share of reflections citing a concrete moment.

Tasks

  • Rewrite the prompt + add exemplar
