Learn and Check

Learning goal

Apply the core idea from Learn and Check in a focused Canvas submission.

Purpose

This is the part where you actually go learn the thing. Everything before this was setting it up.

Part 1. Do the Learning

Work through your path. Use AI freely here. Ask it to explain, to quiz you, to give you examples, to go slower when you are stuck, to try a different angle when the first explanation did not land. That is what it is good at, and there is no penalty for leaning on it.

Keep two things as you go: the sources you actually used, and two or three prompts that moved your understanding forward. Not every prompt. The ones where you steered.

Your path may turn out to be wrong. Steps take longer than you thought, or a step turns out to be unnecessary once you understand the first one. Note what you changed and why. A path you revised on contact with the material is a better answer than a path you followed because you wrote it down.

Part 2. Run Your Two Checks

For each check, fill in five fields.

Field What goes here
The claim Stated so that it could be false.
What told you it Where the belief came from.
What would make it wrong Copied from your Dojo Lab, written before you looked.
What you checked it against The actual source, and when. Note if it changed from your plan.
What came back Confirmed, contradicted, or complicated.
  • Confirmed. The check held.
  • Contradicted. The claim was wrong.
  • Complicated. Yes, but. Write the “but” down rather than rounding it to confirmed. It is usually the finding.

Worked example:

Claim: The nine day wait is real. Ticket age does not restart when a ticket is reassigned.

What told me: The number on the service desk dashboard.

What would make it wrong: If the counter resets on reassignment, the true wait is longer than nine days.

Checked against: Our own admin console, plus the service desk lead, August 14.

What came back: Complicated. The counter does not reset, but tickets that go to the hardware queue are closed and reopened as new tickets, which does reset it. So nine days is right for software access and wrong for anything involving a laptop.

If a check cannot be run, because the person is unavailable or you do not have access to the source, say so. Describe what you tried, mark the claim inferred, and move on. That is an honest outcome and it is graded as one.

Part 3. The Write-Up

Cover:

  • The gap and the decision that was waiting on it.
  • The path as you actually ran it, not as you planned it, with the sources you used by name.
  • Two or three prompts that moved your understanding, and what you did with what came back.
  • Time against estimate. What you guessed, what it took.
  • What you now know, with every item marked confirmed (you checked it against something independent of the claim, and it held) or inferred (you believe it and you have not checked it). A clear AI explanation you never verified is inferred.
  • What is still uncertain.
  • What AI did not decide for you. Where your own judgment made the call.

Part 4. Update Your Goal Plan

Open your Goal Plan and revise whichever part the learning touched. Paste the before and the after.

If your learning confirmed what you already believed and nothing needs changing, say that, and say what result would have made you change it. A confirmed assumption is a real finding. Do not invent a change so that this section has something in it.

What to Submit

All four parts, as a text entry.
