1. Compare these four statements about account handovers. Which one goes furthest toward a candidate problem rather than an indicator, a proposed solution, or a broad goal?

Guidance and self-check
  • Distinguish an indicator, a gap, a proposed solution, and a desired outcome.
  • Look for a current state, a possible state, and a specific cost to people.

2. The volunteer example starts with “New volunteers sign up, come twice, and stop.” Which follow-up best makes the next move: describing how it actually works now?

Guidance and self-check
  • Look for observable steps and who does what.
  • Distinguish current handling from a diagnosis, a desired state, or a proposed fix.

3. In the new-hire example, “We need an app to track where new hires are” leads to the goal that new hires should contribute to their teams as quickly as possible. What does reaching that goal statement allow you to do next?

Guidance and self-check
  • Keep the goal separate from both candidate problems and solutions.
  • Consider what the goal reveals and what it cannot decide for you.

4. You have one candidate that feels promising and two rough indicators you have not examined. What is the strongest next step for the Candidate List submission?

Guidance and self-check
  • Consider whether the next step creates meaningful alternatives from your own situation.
  • Keep gathering and describing separate from defending a choice.

5. In the handover example, “It has not been fixed because each individual handover feels small enough to handle informally” is a proposed explanation. If you have not checked that explanation, how should you include it in your Candidate List?

Guidance and self-check
  • Distinguish a plausible explanation from an established fact.
  • Keep the five-field gap complete without hiding what you do not know.
