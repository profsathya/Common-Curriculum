The goal of this activity is for you to explore your assumptions to understand how they might impact your Problem Frame.

Start with Part 6: your assumptions

Let’s start by looking at Part 6: your assumptions. Every line there is something you wrote as if it were true, but you do not really know yet.

Example: All four managers are affected by account handovers not happening, not just the one I sat next to.

You don’t know if this is true. You’re assuming it. Behind that assumption are questions, whose answers could change the shape of your problem, or show you that it is not a problem worth working on at all. For example, if none of the other managers are impacted, you may not have a problem at all. If two of them are and two of them aren’t, you may have more digging to do. Or if they all agree, you are on to something. You also want to consider how likely each answer is to shut down your problem. For example, if you know that clients are complaining about this, it might not matter what the managers think. You would deem this “not risky.”

Step 1

Walk your assumptions, one table each

With that in mind, take each assumption from Part 6 of your frame and ask three things:

  • What are the questions behind this assumption?
  • What are the different answers that could change my problem?
  • How risky is this to shutting down my problem?

The table below will help you think through each assumption. Fill one table per assumption.
