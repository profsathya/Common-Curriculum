Overview

Welcome. Over the next ten weeks you will take a goal you actually care about, from your work or a personal or professional goal, and turn it into a clear, well-defined problem worth solving. That is the whole game: not rushing to answers, but getting the problem right.

Two questions sit under everything you do here:

  • Superagency: what problems are now within your reach that you would not have attempted before?
  • Human value: what would be worse about the result if you had simply handed it to AI?

You work with AI as a thinking partner, pushing back on it instead of just accepting what it says. You stay in charge. AI helps you think; it does not think for you. This course calls that habit Symbiotic Thinking, and you will go deeper on it later.

How the course is structured

The course runs as five two-week sprints over ten weeks. Each sprint produces one artifact that builds on the last, so the work compounds instead of resetting.

The five-artifact arc, with your current sprint highlighted:

  1. Sprint 1 (you are here): Frame the problem produces your first framing artifact.
  2. Sprint 2: an AI Exploration and 3Cs Application Log.
  3. Sprint 3: a Stakeholder Validation Report.
  4. Sprint 4: a Self-Learning Journey and Learning Plan.
  5. Sprint 5: a Final Integrated Problem Document.

Sprint 1 moves you from your first description of the problem to its underlying causes: the real problem beneath the goal.

How you will work: pacing and rhythm

This course is self-paced, but plan on steady weekly progress. Each sprint spans two weeks, so aim to complete about half a sprint each week rather than saving it for the end. Because everything is asynchronous, set yourself a fixed weekly time to build a routine.

  • Progress means measurable milestones toward your goal each week, not finishing the goal itself.

Choosing your goal

A good goal is a real problem from your own work, or a personal or professional goal you genuinely care about (a career change, a community project, a personal objective; it does not have to be work). It should be big enough to explore for ten weeks but specific enough to act on. You carry this same goal through all five sprints, so pick something that matters to you.

Quick test: what would you need to learn to make progress on it? If the honest answer is “nothing,” the goal is framed too shallow. Reframe it.

How you will use AI here

AI is your thinking partner in this course, not a vending machine for answers. The habit to build: think first, then bring AI in. Write your own take before you prompt, then use AI to challenge and deepen it. You lead, and you decide what is credible and what to keep. Where an activity asks you to think without AI first, that is on purpose.

Meet the Dojo

Some activities use the Symbiotic Thinking Dojo, a space to think with AI rather than get answers from it. It plays a guided role: it challenges your framing instead of solving things for you, which is why it is different from a normal chatbot.

Set up your access first, before your first Dojo activity, so you do not hit an API rate limit mid-activity. The Dojo Setup and How-To guide on the Tutorials and Resources page walks you through it.
