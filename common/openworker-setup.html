Run your own AI agent — free

OpenWorker + a free Gemini key · about 20 minutes, no credit card anywhere
By the end of this page, an AI agent will be running on your own computer — reading files, drafting documents, organizing folders for you — on a free allowance big enough for a full working day.

What an agent is, and why this is worth 20 minutes

You have used a chatbot: you ask, it answers, and doing something with the answer is on you. An AI agentWhat it means. A program that uses an AI model to work toward a goal in steps — reading files, drafting, checking its own results — instead of answering one message and stopping.Why it matters. More and more real work with AI happens this way. Watching one plan and act teaches you more about these tools than any description can. is different. You give it a goal, and it takes the steps itself, then shows you the result. It is the difference between asking a friend how to renew a library book and having them go renew it.

Until recently, trying one on your own computer usually meant a paid subscription. Two things changed that. OpenWorker is a free, open-source agent from Andrew Ng's team that you install like any other app. And Google gives every account a free allowance for its Gemini models — enough to run an agent all day. This page connects the two.

One phrase ahead may sound like developer territory — “API key.” It is not programming: a long password you copy once and paste once, and that is the entire skill.

What you need

A Mac (Apple Silicon, macOS 12 or newer) or a Windows 10/11 computer; a Google account; and about 20 minutes. No credit card — at no point in this setup will anything ask you for payment.

Step 1 — Get your free key

We start with the key because it is the first thing the app will ask for. The key is what lets OpenWorker use your free allowance — you bring your own access, which is why the whole setup can be free.

About 5 minutes
Open aistudio.google.com and sign in
Google AI Studio is where Google hands out free access to its Gemini models. If your campus Google account will not open it — some campus accounts block this tool — sign in with a personal Google account instead.
Click Get API key, then Create API key
Or go straight to aistudio.google.com/apikey. A few seconds later you have a long string of letters and numbers. That string is your API keyWhat it means. A long string that an app sends along with every request so the AI provider knows whose allowance to use. Yours points at your free Gemini allowance.Why it matters. It works like a password: anyone who has the string can spend your allowance as you. Paste it into apps you trust, and nowhere public..
Copy the key and treat it like a password
Paste it somewhere private for the next few minutes. It never goes in a shared doc, a screenshot, a group chat, or anything you post — anyone holding the string can use your allowance.

Step 2 — Install OpenWorker

About 5 minutes
Download it from github.com/andrewyng/openworker
The download links are near the top of that page — pick macOS (Apple Silicon) or Windows 10/11. Install it the way you would any app.
On Windows: expect a SmartScreen warning, this once
Windows warns because OpenWorker's builds are not yet code-signed; the project is open source and its code is public. Click More info, then Run anyway — and keep respecting that warning everywhere else. Here you know exactly what you downloaded and from where.
Open the app
First launch takes a moment. When it asks how you want to connect a model, you are ready for step 3.

Step 3 — Connect the two

About 2 minutes
In OpenWorker's model settings, choose Google Gemini and paste your key
The key is stored on your computer, in the app's own secure store.
Pick Gemini 3 Flash as the model
Not the biggest model on the list — the one whose free allowance fits agent work. An agent spends one request on nearly every step it takes, and Flash's free allowance is about 1,500 requests a day, against about 50 a day for the Pro model. Fifty disappears inside a single task; 1,500 is a full working day.

Step 4 — Give it something real. Do this now

Ask for a real task, and watch how it worksThe point of this page
Try: “Look through my Downloads folder and write me a short summary of what's in it, grouped by type.” Then watch: it makes a plan, takes a step, checks what it found, adjusts — and stops to ask you before anything consequential, like sending or deleting. That plan–act–check loop is the Agentic workflowWhat it means. Work done as a loop — plan, act, check the result, adjust — rather than as one question and one answer.Why it matters. This loop is what lets AI carry a task instead of just commenting on it, and seeing it run once is the fastest way to understand what these tools are and are not. this page exists to let you feel.
If it pauses with a “rate limit” message, wait a minute
Free allowances have a per-minute ceiling too (around 10 requests). The agent is not broken — it is waiting its turn. And if it stops late in the day, the daily allowance resets tomorrow.

Two things to know before you rely on it

Free has a price: your prompts. Google may use what you send on the free tier to improve its models. So keep private things out — no personal documents, nothing about other people, nothing you would not say in public. Point the agent at folders you are comfortable sharing.
The key stays yours. If it ever leaks — pasted somewhere public by accident — go back to aistudio.google.com/apikey, delete it, and create a new one. Two clicks, no harm done.
Where this leaves you

You now have, on your own machine, the kind of agent setup people pay real money for — a current-generation model, a working agent, and an allowance that resets every morning.

So use it on something from your actual week: organizing a messy folder, turning notes into a draft, pulling the dates out of a syllabus. And bring what surprises you — good or bad — to class or Slack. What the agent gets wrong is usually the best conversation in the room.
