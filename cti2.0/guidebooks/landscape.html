What we are doing: testing the CTI 2.0 framework — in its entirety or one component at a time — through small 4–8 week pilots, and collating what we learn into a comprehensive picture that informs how human development adapts in the age of AI.

The student's journey — the highest-level view

Every CTI program, seen as one experience. Click any step for what actually happens in it.

1 · Arrive A student joins any CTI program — course, cohort, or pathway.
more

One account follows them across all CTI programs, so nothing is repeated and growth accumulates. First contact includes a short piece of real work — never an entry test.

→
2 · Reflect A first reflection-survey: finding the qualities already in them.
more

Our stance: everyone has curiosity, agency, and drive — the environment just hasn't sparked them yet. The reflection-survey helps a student see where they're starting, in their own words plus a few short scales. It is never used to select or gate anyone.

→
3 · Learn & build The program runs the full capability loop with AI as a partner.
more

Self-Directed Learner → Integrative Solver → Adaptive Builder, integrated by Symbiotic Thinking. Practice happens in the Dojo (an AI coach that pushes back) and, soon, the Commons (learning together). Students produce real work: goal plans, reflections, projects.

→
4 · Demonstrate Students present and defend real work. Their card grows.
more

Growth shows in the work itself — defended projects, revised goal plans, judgment visible in what they made. Each program adds to a personal "certificate card" of demonstrated qualities: not a formal credential yet, but built from real artifacts and human validation, aiming to become one.

→
5 · Return Next CTI program: reflect again, and the growth is visible.
more

A returning student's reflection-survey takes ~3 minutes, not 15 — the core was done once. Comparing reflections across programs is where the student sees their own arc, and where CTI sees what no single program can show (like habits actually forming, which takes months).

⟲ And underneath it all: what CTI learns from every cycle flows back into the foundational questions below.

The three questions above everything

Every pilot asks a specific form of F1 — some also test F2. F3 is why we do it.

F1 · Framework Do the CTI 2.0 capabilities — Self-Directed Learner → Integrative Solver → Adaptive Builder, integrated by Symbiotic Thinking — describe what AI-age work actually requires? And does developing them produce human-valuable performance in real work?
F2 · Diffusion Can colleges, community partners, and industry adopt the framework in forms that keep it working — spreading with the substance intact, not as fashion?
F3 · North Star Does the middle of society participate and thrive in the AI age? Downstream of F1 and F2, on a longer clock than any pilot. It justifies the work; no single pilot is measured by it.

How the learning flows back up

Each pilot runs in short loops and captures what it learns in the team repo. The formal close of each run is the EDFG Phase 4 retrospective — hypotheses assessed against the criteria set before launch. Findings flow up the layers: pilot evidence accumulates on F1 and F2, cross-pilot patterns get promoted into the framework and methodology, and the foundational questions themselves get sharpened — or falsified. This page is the picture those learnings assemble.

Reflection-surveys — our stance on measurement

We call them reflection-surveys, and the name is the design. Our working hypothesis: people don't lack curiosity or agency — everyone has these qualities, and the environment just hasn't sparked them to grow. So the reflection-survey is not a test of who someone is. It helps a student see where they're starting, and each return shows them their own growth. It is never used to select or gate anyone.

How this stays rigorous (click)

Two layers, kept separate on purpose. The student-facing layer is the reflection experience and the growing certificate card — built from real artifacts and human validation, never from scores. The research layer sits quietly inside: a few short validated question sets, asked with their exact original wording, analyzed only at cohort level. Students never see scale scores to perform against, and growth claims come from the work students produce — goal plans, reflections, defended projects — not from surveys alone.

Who arrives — the dimensions we reflect on

Every "can we cultivate X?" question is really "can we cultivate X in whoever shows up" — so growth only counts when we know the starting point. Click each dimension for how we see it.

Agency The belief and initiative to direct your own path.
how we see it

A short validated question set at first contact, plus what a student attempts unprompted in week one. Superagency, precisely: growth in agency times extended reach with AI — what a student attempts and completes beyond their starting scope.

Curiosity The drive to explore past the comfort default — inside Self-Directed Learner: learning something because you care about it.
how we see it

A 10-question validated set, plus behavior: the questions a student asks without being prompted. We don't claim to teach curiosity — we design the environment to spark it (real questions before answers, prediction before feedback, autonomy) and watch whether interest deepens: surface → deeper → sustained.

AI fluency The current working relationship with AI — consumer or director.
how we see it

A short self-check plus a few objective questions with confidence ratings attached — because self-rated AI skill runs high, and the confidence ratings show us something more valuable: whether a student knows what they know.

Self-knowledge practice Whether they examine evidence about themselves.
how we see it

No questionnaire at all — this one only shows in the work: the quality of a first written reflection, and how specific the first Goal Plan is.

Under consideration Research-backed candidates awaiting team adoption: self-regulation / follow-through · belonging · abstraction · comfort with uncertainty, plus a 2-minute motivation check in weeks 2–3 (a validated early signal that someone is disengaging — so we can reach them, not rank them).
The goal, precisely: before its next run, every pilot states who it expects to arrive on these dimensions, runs the shared reflection-survey at entry, and states its learning goals as growth of the whole cohort from that starting point, shown in the work students produce. Pilot leads own their pilot's version; the question sets are shared so results can combine.

The common structure — what every pilot does

The key points of our current thinking, held in common across every pilot. More stable than the details — but nothing is set in stone; it changes when we decide together to change it.

  1. Runs a common intake. One short reflection-survey plus a first sample of student work, the same in every pilot — completed once at a student's first CTI contact, with a ~3-minute refresh at each later program.
  2. Collects the same kinds of student work all the way through. Goal plans, written reflections, and the final work students present and defend — scored with the same shared scoring guides. This student work is our main evidence of growth.
  3. Designs for the full SDL → IS → AB loop. No pilot tests one capability without the others; what a pilot chooses is where the stress lands (table below).
  4. Completes its experiment design (EDFG) before launch. The learning question is the pilot's EDFG Section 2 — a core question plus 2–4 hypotheses, each with criteria that would prove it wrong; Sections 3–4 name the measures and data sources.
  5. Never uses intake results to select students. Measurement is for design and understanding, never admission.
  6. Captures findings to the team repo after every run, so evidence rolls up into the foundational questions.

Everything below this line — exact question sets, scoring guides, the final dimension list — is still being worked out, and pilot leads own those calls.

If you lead a pilot

  1. Check your card and your row. On the "What we run" tab: is your pilot's learning question right? On "How we measure": is your loop-stress row right? Correct them — say it to Athena or edit the repo (the guidebook shows how in five steps).
  2. Complete or update your EDFG before your next run — Section 1 (where you stress the loop), Section 2 (core question + hypotheses with fail criteria), Sections 3–4 (measures and data). Athena facilitates it; say "let's work on my experiment design."
  3. Plan the common intake into your next run: the shared reflection-survey at entry plus a first sample of student work. The question sets are being finalized — your job now is just leaving room for it in your week-one design.
  4. Collect the three core kinds of work as your run goes: goal plans, written reflections, and the final defended work. Keep them where the team can score them later.
  5. Close your run in the repo: what you learned, against the hypotheses you set. That's what flows up to the foundational questions.
