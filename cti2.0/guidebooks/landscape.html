The CTI Landscape

The 2000-foot view on one page: the foundational questions we are asking, the pilots asking them in specific forms, and how what we learn flows back up. Refreshed monthly. For the how-to of daily work, see the Team Guidebook.

What we are doing: testing the CTI 2.0 framework — in its entirety or one component at a time — through small 4–8 week pilots, and collating what we learn into a comprehensive picture that informs how human development adapts in the age of AI.

The common structure — what every pilot does

The stable part (Sathya). The deeper into detail, the more is unknown and the more freedom pilot leads have — but this structure holds across every pilot.

  1. Runs a common intake. One short pre-survey plus baseline artifacts, identical across pilots, measuring students on the key dimensions. The exact dimensions are work in progress — the current list is in the Inputs section below.
  2. Collects the same core artifacts throughout. Goal plans, reflections, defended work — scored with shared rubrics. The work is the growth evidence; surveys calibrate but never carry a claim alone.
  3. Designs for the full SDL → IS → AB loop. No pilot tests one capability without the others; what a pilot chooses is where the stress lands (table below).
  4. Completes its experiment design (EDFG) before launch. The learning question is the pilot's EDFG Section 2 — a core question plus 2–4 hypotheses, each with falsification criteria; Sections 3–4 name the outcomes, measures, and data sources that count as movement.
  5. Never uses intake scores to select students. Measurement is for design and attribution only.
  6. Captures findings to the team repo after every run, so evidence rolls up into the foundational questions instead of scattering.

Stable ends here. Instruments, rubric anchors, and the final dimension list are work in progress, owned by pilot leads.

The foundational questions

Three questions sit above everything. Every pilot below asks a specific, testable form of F1 — and some also test F2. What a pilot learns flows back up as evidence on these.

F1 · Framework Do the CTI 2.0 capabilities — Self-Directed Learner → Integrative Solver → Adaptive Builder, integrated by Symbiotic Thinking — describe what AI-age work actually requires? And does developing them produce human-valuable performance in real work?
F2 · Diffusion Can colleges, community partners, and industry adopt the framework in forms that keep it working — spreading with the substance intact, not as fashion?
F3 · North Star Does the middle of society participate and thrive in the AI age? Downstream of F1 and F2, on a longer clock than any pilot. It justifies the work; no single pilot is measured by it.

Inputs — who arrives

The layer we are defining now. Every "can we cultivate X?" question is really "can we cultivate X in whoever shows up" — so development claims only count as evidence on F1 when measured against who arrived. Four entry dimensions, each with a research-anchored measure (draft; instruments to be confirmed in the working paper):

Agency The belief and initiative to direct your own path. Intake: agency measures from hope/self-efficacy research + a first-week behavioral baseline (what they attempt unprompted). Superagency, precisely: growth in agency times extended reach with AI — what a student attempts and completes beyond their measured baseline scope.
Curiosity The drive to explore past the comfort default. Intake: short validated curiosity scale + behavior (questions asked and threads pursued without prompting).
AI fluency The current working relationship with AI — consumer or director. Intake: entry-point self-check (the CI fluency assumptions doc is the start).
Self-knowledge practice Whether they examine evidence about themselves. Intake: reflection quality at entry + specificity of the first Goal Plan.
Under consideration From the measurement working papers (team repo, methodology/, 2026-07-14) — research-backed candidates awaiting team adoption: self-regulation / follow-through · belonging · abstraction · comfort with uncertainty, plus a brief motivation pulse in weeks 2–3 as a validated early-warning signal — a natural first instrument for the EDFG's Phase 3 experiment pulse, which is designed but not yet built.
The goal, precisely: before its next run, every pilot states its assumed entry profile on these dimensions, measures it at intake (brief validated instrument + first-week behavior — never a questionnaire ritual), and states its learning goals as cohort-level growth from that baseline, evidenced in the work. Each pilot lead owns their pilot's input definition; the instruments menu is shared. The research-grounded working papers are in the team repo for leads to react to.

How the learning flows back up

Each pilot runs in short loops and captures what it learns in the team repo (conversations → decisions → the pilot's status file). The formal close of each run is the EDFG Phase 4 retrospective — hypotheses assessed against their falsification criteria, story revisited. Those findings flow up the layers: pilot evidence accumulates on F1 and F2, cross-pilot patterns get promoted into the framework and methodology, and the foundational questions themselves get sharpened — or falsified. F3 stays overhead as the reason for all of it. This page is the picture those promoted learnings assemble.
