Learning Plan

Week 6 · due Monday 5 October, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

Purpose

Identify what you individually must learn to complete your project, and plan how you will learn it and how you will prove that you did.

Tasks

“You” means you personally, also in a team project: what you must learn, not what the team must.

Paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it.

Each + opens guidance, one example, a reflection question and a box to write in. Nothing under a + is required.

  1. 1.Identify the two things you individually must learn to complete the project: the most critical technical skill and the most critical non-technical skill. Name each one.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI

    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Name the one technical skill and the one non-technical skill that your project depends on most and that you do not have yet. Tasks 2 to 5 come back to these two skills one at a time. The choice is yours even on a team project: what you must learn, not what a teammate already knows.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Technical skill — a form of accessible search on a static site.
    Non-technical skill — how the offices that own campus information actually publish and update it (interviews, and one office’s process end to end).

    Reflection

    Which other skill did you leave out to choose these two?

  2. 2.For each skill, explain why the build needs it: which part of the work cannot be done, or cannot be done well, without it.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI

    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    For each of your two skills, name the part of the work that cannot be done, or cannot be done well, without it. One or two sentences per skill is enough; the test is that a reader could point at the part of the project you mean.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Technical — without it, a student has to scroll the whole list to find the tutoring hours.
    Non-technical — without it, the resource list is wrong the first time an office changes its hours.

    Reflection

    If your answer here is weak for one skill, is there a more critical skill that should replace it?

  3. 3.For each skill, name the resources you will learn from — a course, documentation, a book, a person, a tool — with links where they exist.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI

    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    For each skill, name what you will learn from: a course, documentation, a book, a person, a tool. Link each one where a link exists. A person counts as a resource — name them, and say how you will reach them.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Technical — the Pagefind docs (link).
    Non-technical — a 20-minute conversation with the Tutoring Center coordinator (booked for 8 Oct), and Arlene’s administrator feedback from Cabrillo and Gavilan.

    Reflection

    Which resource is a person, and have you asked them yet?

  4. 4.For each skill, describe the action steps you will take to learn it: what you will do, in what order, and when.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI

    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    For each skill, write what you will do, in what order, and when. Small steps with dates beat one big one: a step you can finish in a sitting, with the day you will do it, is one you can check off.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Non-technical — 8 Oct: the conversation with the coordinator. 9 Oct: write up the office’s current process in one page. 12 Oct: show it back to the coordinator and correct it.

    Reflection

    Which step could you do today?

  5. 5.For each skill, state how you will verify you learned it: what you will be able to show, what it will demonstrate, and what external verification step, if any, you will use to confirm it — a review by someone who knows the area, a test with a real user, a result others can check.

Someone or something outside you confirming that what you show is real: a review by a person who knows the area, a test with a real user, a result others can check for themselves.Why it matters. A hiring manager can only take your word for what you learned; they can act on a coordinator’s email or a timed test with three students. Criteria

Checklist — one line per task. Run it on your own draft; your reviewer runs the same list.
  1. Name the most critical technical skill and the most critical non-technical skill you must learn.
  2. For each skill, say which part of the work cannot be done, or done well, without it.
  3. For each skill, name the resources, with links where links exist.
  4. For each skill, give the action steps: what, in what order, and when.
  5. For each skill, say what you will show, what it demonstrates, and whether an external verification step is planned.
  6. Optional: link or screenshot what exists and runs today.
