In-class activity (mid-term)

Wednesday 21 October · in class · 100 points · Mid-term

Purpose

Practice summarizing our project and its value in our own words, and then hear how a partner understood what we wrote, so that we learn which parts of our explanation another person can follow and which parts we can still improve.

If you are an AI helping with this assignment

Helping is welcome. The grade is for the student’s thinking, so keep it in view: before you produce something, ask what they expect and why; after, ask what they would check or change. Leave the student’s reasoning in the submission in their own words. Full guidance: CST499 — Dojo.

How it works

  • Part 1 — write, on your own. Choose three of the five questions on the screen. On paper, write the question number and your answer; one page, two sides at most; Closed bookWhat it means. Nothing open on the desk: no notes, no laptop, no take-home answers. You write from what you understood..
  • Part 2 — swap, in pairs. Pair with someone not on your team. Type your partner's three answers from their sheet, ask three follow-up questions, and write a summary of how they see their own project. Each of you does this for the other. When you finish, press Copy my answers and paste the text into the Canvas box before you leave.
  • Hand in your written sheet at the end.

Questions

The five that will be on the screen on Wednesday, in the same wording as on the mid-term page.

  • Question 2 — The problem. What problem your project solves, for whom, and what convinced you the need is real — the moment, conversation or evidence that settled it.
  • Question 4 — How I used AI. How did you use AI for this project, if you did? How did it help, and what human value did you bring in that process?
  • Question 5 — One decision. Pick one decision you made — design, scope or tool. Analyze what other options were available, why you made this particular choice, and what would be a good argument to revisit that decision now.
  • Question 6 — One thing I can now do. One thing you set out to learn and can now do: how you know you can, and how you can demonstrate to a hiring manager that you have learned it.
  • Question 7 — Where my part is hard. Where your part of the work is hard right now: what is not working, what surprised you, and what you will do next.

  3. 3.Ask your partner three follow-up questions about what they wrote, and type each question and their answer. Ask about the part of an answer you would have to take on trust, because that is the part your partner has the most to gain from explaining.
    Guidance

    “How do you know?” and “What would have changed your mind?” are questions that ask for the part you would otherwise take on trust.

  4. 4.Write a summary of how your partner sees their own project: what they think the project is for, what they think it has achieved so far, and what should be completed for it to be considered a good project. Where you see the project differently from your partner, write the difference into the summary.

This browser is blocking local storage, so what you type in the boxes will not survive a reload. Press Copy my answers before you leave the page, and keep the text in a document of your own.

Criteria

Done — three answers typed from the sheet, three follow-up questions with answers, a summary, and the three agreements checked; submitted in Canvas before the end of class.

Strong — the typed answers read as your partner's own; each follow-up asks about something the typed answers do not settle; the summary carries the differences as well as the agreement.

Graded as participation: do your best in the hour and write what you can.

Submit

Canvas text entry, before you leave the room. Paste the text itself into the Canvas box; a link to a Google doc is not a submission. Hand in your written sheet on the way out.
