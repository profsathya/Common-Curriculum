Know What Makes Me Tick

Due Friday 4 September, 11:59 p.m. · about 30 minutes

Where this fits. This week connects what the market is asking for, work that holds your attention, and the evidence you want to build next — and next week you turn all three into a short professional introduction (the full picture is on Read the Market). Today is the middle one.

Purpose

Work out which kind of work reliably holds your attention, and describe one strength and one weakness precisely enough to act on.

Steps

Learning outcomes

1. Identify one kind of work that Flow stateWhat it is. Those stretches where you kept going without deciding to, or looked up and found an hour gone. Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi named it flow: full absorption in work that stretches you a little past what you can already do easily.Why it matters here. Flow is a signal about the kind of work, not about having had a good day. What more than one of those times share is something you can aim at. And it matters more in the age of AI, not less — finishing something without going deep on it has never been easier, so the work that goes deep is what sets yours apart., described precisely enough that I could recognise it in a job posting.

Two worked examplesBoth end on the recognition test — what the outcome looks like in a posting.

Example 1. More than one of my moments were the same shape: I was chasing something that was not behaving the way it should. The sensor loop at 1 a.m., the CSV import that dropped every hundredth row, my brother’s bike gears. What holds me is a system misbehaving in a way that leaves a trail I can follow. In a posting it looks like Chewy’s “Monitor data quality and pipeline performance; assist in triaging and resolving basic issues” — triage is the word for it.

Example 2. Two of mine were about making something legible to someone else: rewriting our group’s README until my roommate could run the project without asking me anything, and explaining recursion to a classmate until it clicked. What holds me is taking something tangled and making it usable by a person who was not in the room. In a posting it shows up as Airtable’s “Create reusable and maintainable systems and abstractions that help us scale”, and in anything that asks for documentation.

2. Describe one of my strengths and one of my weaknesses precisely enough that I can act on them.

Two worked examplesIn both, the weakness is the shadow of the strength rather than a separate deficiency.

Example 1. Strength: I get things to a finished, working state under a deadline — in my data structures class I was the one who got the group’s project submitted and running. That is more precise than “hard worker”, and it is the version I can put in front of someone as a shipped thing. Weakness: the same thing taken too far — I get the work done by myself instead of bringing the team along. What I need is to get better at setting team norms and holding them gently, so that everyone contributes and grows instead of watching me finish it.

Example 2. Strength: I notice when something does not add up. In our networks lab I was the one who found the rule that quietly let traffic through. The precise version is that I am good at finding the case a design did not consider — which is most of what a security assessment is. Weakness: I go quiet when I disagree with a group’s decision. The precise version is not that I have no opinion; it is that I have no practised way to raise a concern so that it lands as a question rather than an accusation. Three of the five postings name communication as a qualification.

What you are building on

Your market read from Wednesday. You need it for box 4 only — leave it closed for the first three, so your answers are not shaped by what would be convenient. Nothing else is assumed.

Why this sits in a market week

The market has shifted toward work that takes sustained attention. The first pass at most technical tasks is quick now; the value is in what comes after — the problem someone keeps working on, the detail they follow further than they were asked to.

Knowing which kinds of work do that for you is what lets you aim at the openings worth aiming at. It is also most of what makes a professional introduction sound like a person rather than a profile.

So this is not a detour from the market work. Wednesday told you where the openings are. Today tells you which of them are worth your effort.

Reflection

  • For the benefit, name something you would be able to do afterwards that you cannot do now.
  • Read yours next to the wording above. Which phrase is closest to a copy? Rewrite that one.

  • Write the before and the after: what you took it to mean, and what you take it to mean now.

2 · Your moments

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Are these specific occasions, rather than descriptions of the kind of work you like?
  • Does each one cover all four columns?
  • Does the fourth column say what kept you engaged, rather than why the work mattered in general?

Reflection

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Put a number on each side — assigned, and started yourself.
  • Is what you notice a claim about the moments, or about you in general?
  • If they all fell one way, say what that rules in and what it rules out.
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Say which of the two, and what they asked.
  • Write the detail you added, in the words you added it in.
  • Would that detail have come out if they had not asked?

3 · What more than one of them share, and a second reading

Pick out more than one of your moments that share something, say which ones, and say what they share — in one sentence. First reading, first answer; box 3 is where you test it.
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Did you say which moments — by number — the pattern is drawn from?
  • Is it one sentence about what they share, rather than a description of each one?
  • Is it written down, so box 3 has something to work from?
Read your moments again, slowly. Then do one of three things to what you wrote in box 2: revise it, confirm it with the evidence that supports it, or qualify it — say where it holds and where it does not. Confirming is a real outcome; a second reading does not have to disagree with the first.
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Did you say which of the three you did?
  • Which specific moments support the pattern, and are there any it does not fit?
  • If two moments sit a long way apart, what does having both of them tell you?

Reflection

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Fill the blank with something a job posting could contain.
  • Read it against a moment it does not fit — does the sentence survive?
  • Could a classmate use your sentence to sort their own moments?
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Write their objection as they put it, not as you would put it.
  • Say which of your moments their objection lands on.
  • Do you agree? Either answer is complete, with a reason.

4 · Explore it against the market, and identify a strength and a weakness

Now open Wednesday’s work. Look at the two things employers are asking for that you found. Does the pattern from box 3 sit inside them, outside them, or partly both? All three are usable answers, and Tuesday is where you act on whichever it is.
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Is the placement drawn from your actual answers in both activities?
  • If it sits outside: is that a part of the market you have not looked at yet, or work you would keep alongside a different aim?
One strength and one weakness, each described precisely enough to act on. Precise means a reader could tell what you would do differently tomorrow: “I get things to a working state under a deadline” rather than “hard worker”, and a weakness written as the practice you would take up rather than the thing you lack.
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Is the strength something you did on a particular occasion, or a quality you are claiming?
  • Would a reader know what to do differently tomorrow from your weakness?
  • Does the weakness say what you would practise, rather than what you lack?

Reflection

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Name the thing specifically enough to go and find it.
  • Say what result would count as an overlap, and what result would not.
  • Could you do it this week?
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • For each, write the thing a reader would do differently tomorrow.
  • Say which was harder, and what made it harder.
  • Read the weakness again — is it a practice you would take up, or a lack you are reporting?
Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Say which of their two, and what it tells you about working with them.
  • Write your remaining question as a question.
  • Would your answer change if you had read only the other one?

5 · Final reflection

Reflection
