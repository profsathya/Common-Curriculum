CST499 · Fall 2026 · Sprint 1

Project Proposal

Week 4 · due Monday 21 September, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

Purpose

Articulate in detail your understanding about the market, the project you will build this semester, the rubric you would use to assess the quality of your work, and why this project will be useful to its users and to your career.

Tasks

This is an individual submission. If you are working with a partner or a team, parts of the proposal may be shared across members; your personal motivation, and how this project benefits you, are yours alone and cannot match anyone else's.

Paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it.

Each + opens guidance, one example, a reflection question and a box to write in. Nothing under a + is required.

  1. 1.Identify one or more roles you are aiming for. For each, link two or three postings you are using as the bar.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Choose roles you would apply to next year, and use the postings as the bar you are building toward. Two or three postings per role is enough to see what repeats.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Front-end software engineer — three linked postings (Company A, Company B, Company C).
    Product engineer — three linked postings (Company D, Company E, Company F).
    The phrase that repeats, in five of the six: “ship features end to end and keep them working for real users”.

    Reflection

    What repeats across the postings that you did not expect?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Each role you would apply to next year is listed.
    • Each role has two or three postings, and each posting is a link someone could open.
  2. 2.State the gap in your preparation briefly: what those postings ask for that you cannot show yet.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    The The gap in your preparationWhat it means. The distance between what the postings ask for and what you can show today.Why it matters. Your project is the answer to this one sentence. A specific gap gives you a specific thing to build evidence for.What a good one looks like. “I have never shipped code another engineer reviewed” is a gap. “I need more experience” is not. is the distance between what the postings ask for and what you can show today. Write it in your own words, briefly.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    “Every posting asks for work that shipped to real users; everything I can show is coursework.”

    Reflection

    Which posting phrase is the gap closest to?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It says what the postings ask for, in words close to the postings’ own.
    • It says what you cannot show yet, as one specific thing rather than a general level.
    • It is brief.
  3. 3.Describe the project.
    1. What you will build or make.
    2. Who it is for — a named person, team or kind of user.
    3. The evidence that this is a real need for that person, team or user: what you observed, who you asked and what they said, or the data you found — each with its source.
    4. What they will do with it that they cannot do now.
    If you do not have a project in mind yet, open the guidance under this task.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Finding a project. Look at the problem spaces curated for this course as a starting point. Which of them are interesting to you? Within those, which would give you the opportunity to build the skill you need to address your gap? Who do you need to talk to, or what do you need to investigate, to define a project goal that is meaningful for you?

    Answer 3a, 3b and 3d in a sentence or two each. Spend the most time on 3c. Evidence of need is something you observed, someone you asked and what they said, or data you found — each with its source. “I read somewhere” and “it is a common problem” are claims; a claim with its source is evidence.

    An example of 3b and 3c, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    3b. New and transfer students at CSUMB looking for one kind of campus resource — tutoring hours — and the office that owns that information. (From the Campus Compass problem space.)

    3c. Asked — Arlene Resendiz Lopez, who offered the problem space, shared her 22 student survey responses; 16 say they missed a resource in their first month because they could not find it. Observed — I sat with two transfer students while they looked for tutoring hours: 4 minutes and 7 minutes, and one gave up. Asked further — I emailed orientation staff at three other campuses to ask whether their students have the same problem; two have replied that they do, and I plan to reach more over the coming weeks.

    Reflection

    If the person in 3b read your 3c, would they recognise their own need in it?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • 3a says what you will build or make.
    • 3b identifies a person, a team or a kind of user.
    • Every item in 3c is something you observed, someone you asked and what they said, or data you found — and each item has its source.
    • 3d says what they will do with it that they cannot do now.
  4. 4.Test what AI can build of this today.
    1. What you tried, and with what (ai.studio or another tool).
    2. What came out.
    3. What it could not do, or got wrong.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Open ai.studioWhat it is. Google AI Studio, at ai.studio — free with a Google account, and it can generate code and a running preview from a description.Why it is the default here. Everyone in the class can open it today at no cost. If you already use another tool, use that one and say which. (or the tool you have), describe the project, and see what comes out. The point is to know where the tool’s contribution ends before you decide where yours begins. Record what you tried, what came out, and what it could not do or got wrong.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Tried: gave ai.studio two lines — “a page that lists campus resources by category with hours and a contact, with a search box; no login.”
    Came out: a working page with a search box over a list of resources.
    Got wrong: every resource and every hour on it was invented, and nothing in it would change when an office changes its hours.

    Reflection

    What did the test change about how you see the project?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • 4a identifies the tool and says what you gave it.
    • 4b says what came out.
    • 4c includes at least one specific thing it could not do or got wrong.
  5. 5.Identify what you will bring that the tool does not — judgment, domain knowledge, testing with real users, the part that needs a person. This is a goal you aim at and can adjust; the mid-term and the final report ask what you brought.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    This is a goal, not a promise; the The mid-termWhat it is. Week 8, Wednesday 14 October: written answers about your own project and the market evidence behind it — part online before class, part in class, closed book.Why it matters here. One of its questions asks how you used AI and what you brought. What you write in task 5 now is the goal you will be checking against then. and the final report ask what you brought, and you can adjust. Judgment, domain knowledge, testing with real users, and the parts that need a person are the usual answers; yours should be specific to this project.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    “The tool can generate the page and the search; I bring Arlene’s survey, the students I watched, the conversations with the offices about keeping their information current, and a test of each version with students who have not seen it before.”

    Reflection

    Which of these would a hiring manager care about?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Each thing you bring is specific to this project, and could not be pasted into a classmate’s proposal unchanged.
    • Each one connects to something in 4c — what the tool could not do or got wrong.
  6. 6.Set the scope.
    1. What will exist by the mid-term (week 8).
    2. What will exist by week 12.
    3. What is out.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    The ScopeWhat it means. The boundary around what you are taking on — what is in, what is deliberately out, and what “done” means.Why it matters. Naming the boundary in week 4 is what lets you finish in week 12.What a good one looks like. Small enough to finish, big enough that finishing it shows something. has three parts. 6a is what you will be able to show in class on 14 October; 6b is what will exist by week 12; 6c is what you are deliberately leaving out.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    6a. One category — tutoring — end to end: the office’s real information, found in under a minute by a student who has not seen the page, shown on my laptop.
    6b. Three categories, a way for each office to update its own entry, and one month of the information staying current.
    6c. A mobile app, a campus-wide rollout, anything for another campus.

    Reflection

    What would you cut first if week 6 goes badly?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • 6a is something you could show in class on 14 October.
    • 6b is something that will exist by week 12, and it includes 6a.
    • 6c includes at least one thing you are leaving out on purpose.
  7. 7.Name the evidence.
    1. What a hiring manager will be able to see at the end — repo, write-up, demo, data.
    2. What external verifications you are considering to demonstrate quality, impact and your human value — a user who tried it, someone in the field who reviewed it, data from real use.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    7a is what a hiring manager opens; 7b is who or what confirms it — a user who tried it, someone in the field who reviewed it, data from real use.

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    7a. The repo, a two-page write-up of what was built and what changed after testing, and the log of updates the offices made themselves.
    7b. Five new students timed finding a resource, and what they said; the office contact confirming the information was still correct a month later; Arlene’s review of it against her survey.

    Reflection

    Which verification would be hardest to get, and is it the one that matters most?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • 7a lists things a hiring manager could open — a repo, a write-up, a demo, data.
    • 7b identifies who or what would confirm it: a particular user, a particular reviewer, or a particular measurement — not just “users”.
  8. 8.Write your rubric: three to five categories, each with A, B and C descriptors, detailed enough for someone else to grade you with. Team project: say what you are accountable for.
    Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Choose three to five categories for your own work. Give each an A, a B and a C descriptor detailed enough that a classmate could grade you with it and would not have to guess. Team project: say what you are accountable for.

    An example of one category, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Real use
    A: two or more people outside the team have used it for its purpose and their feedback is recorded.
    B: one person has used it once.
    C: it has been demonstrated but not used.

    Reflection

    Which category would you least like to be graded on, and why is it on the list?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • There are three to five categories.
    • Every category has an A, a B and a C descriptor.
    • Read each descriptor as a classmate would: they could grade you with it and would not have to guess.
    • Team project: it says what you are accountable for.

Criteria

Done — all eight tasks answered; every a/b/c part present; the postings in 1 are linked; the evidence in 3c has a source for each item; the AI test in 4 says what was tried, with what, and what came out; the rubric in 8 has three to five categories with A, B and C descriptors; a team project says what you are accountable for.

Strong — the gap in 2 is traceable to the postings in 1; the need in 3c rests on something you observed, someone you asked, or data you found, not on a general claim; 4 and 5 read as a real test and an honest account of what was left for you to do; the week-8 scope in 6a is something you could show in class on 14 October; the verifications in 7b name who or what, not just “users”; the rubric could be handed to a classmate to grade you with, and they would not have to guess.

  6. Answer 6a, 6b and 6c: week 8, week 12, and what is out.
