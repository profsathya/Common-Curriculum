    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    The evidence, and where it will be — my notes from asking students and two office staff where they lose time looking for office hours; the live site, linked from my Interactive Poster, with a short page on how the hours stay current; and the times from five students who looked for tutoring hours on the old campus page and on my site.

    What exists today — the search works on a list of twelve offices that I typed by hand.

    More examples

    The evidence, and where it will be — two pull requests to Kolibri, the open-source learning platform, each with the test I wrote, linked from my Interactive Poster; and a one-page map of the parts of the system I had to understand to make the fixes.

    What exists today — one pull request is open, and a maintainer has asked me for one change.

    What still has to happen — make the requested change, pick and fix a second issue, and draw the map.

    The evidence, and where it will be — my Security+ result, and a write-up of the firewall rules I set up on a home lab network, both on my Interactive Poster.

    What exists today — two practice exams above the passing score, and the lab network running with default settings.

    What still has to happen — take the exam on the date I booked, set up and test the firewall rules, and write up why I chose each one.

  3. 3.
    Read the two reports below, then look at your claim and identify:
    • the part of your claim that shows depth in one domain
    • the part that reaches into a second domain, if there is one
    • if your claim stays in one domain, why, and one way your work could move toward a second domain
    (A domain is an area of knowledge or skill that stands on its own: a technical area, the field your work serves, a way of working with people, a way of handling data. Use the domains you listed in task 3 of the Quality review.)

    An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy

    Depth — web development: the search, and pulling and reading each office's page so the hours are always the ones the office posted.

    Breadth — understanding what users need: asking students and office staff where they lose time, and tracing it to its cause. The hours are posted in several places that do not agree, and that cause decides what my software has to fix and which pages it pulls.

    More examples

    Depth — reading and changing a large codebase: tracing an issue through the system design to the place where a fix belongs, and proving it with a test.

    Breadth — Kolibri is used in classrooms without reliable internet. The second issue I pick will come from how teachers use it offline, which means learning how those classrooms work as well as how the code works.

    Depth — network security: firewall rules on a real network, and the exam that tests them.

    Why it stays in one domain, and one way to move toward a second — my claim stays in security for now, because the exam is the evidence I need first for the jobs I am applying to. One way to move toward a second domain: write a one-page summary of my firewall choices for the owner of a small business I know, and ask whether it answers the questions they would have about cost and risk.
