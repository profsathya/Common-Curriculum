## Worked examples (shown beside the form; only the chosen route's examples show) [for Sathya's read]

### Route A examples

**From a first draft to a stronger reflection**
- *First draft:* Task 1 — It helped me understand users better. Task 2 — I improved the search. Task 3 — Users liked it more.
- *Stronger:* Example A below. The first draft does not say where the second domain entered the work, what changed, or what a reviewer could check. Example A names the decision the second domain influenced (what the search returns), what the search did before and does now, and timings a reviewer can find on the poster.

**Example A · A campus office-hours tool**
- *How the second domain influenced my project:* Understanding what users need. I talked with four students and two office staff about where they lose time when they look for help on campus. It influenced my project where I decide what the search returns. The students told me they search by the problem they have, such as "drop a class", and not by the name of the office.
- *The key change:* Before, my search returned every office page that matched a word. Now it uses a list I built from the conversations, which matches twelve common problems to the office that handles each one, so a search for "drop a class" goes to the Registrar first.
- *The evidence:* I timed five students finding the right office for three problems, once on the old version and once on the new one. The average time went from 2 minutes 10 seconds to 40 seconds. The timings and the problem list are linked on my poster. A skeptical reviewer could ask whether five students are enough, so I say it is five students and three problems, and I do not claim more.

**Example B · A contribution to Kolibri, an open-source learning platform**
- *How the second domain influenced my project:* How teachers use Kolibri in classrooms without reliable internet. I read the project's guides for setting it up offline and two issue threads written by teachers. It influenced my project when I chose which issue to fix: a sync between two devices that failed without telling anyone when the connection was slow.
- *The key change:* Before, I planned to fix only the error behind the failure. Now my fix also shows the teacher a message that the sync did not finish and will try again, because the teachers' threads showed that not knowing was the real problem for them.
- *The evidence:* My pull request, with a test that slows the connection and checks that the message appears, and the maintainer's review of it. Both are linked on my poster.

### Route B examples

**From a first draft to a stronger reflection**
- *First draft:* Task 1 — I didn't have time. Task 2 — Nothing, the project works. Task 3 — Maybe design.
- *Stronger:* Example D below. The first draft names an obstacle every student has and stops there. Example D names the obstacle as it showed up in the work, ties what is missing to the project goal, and picks a domain with a first step that can happen this week.

**Example C · A Security+ certificate with a home lab**
- *The key obstacle:* Time. My exam is booked for 12 December, and every week until then goes to practice exams and the lab.
- *What my project goal may be missing:* My goal is to be ready for a junior security role. I chose the firewall rules on my lab network from my study guide, so I can explain how each rule works but not why a business would want it, or what it costs the business when it blocks something.
- *A domain that could fill the gap:* How a small business owner thinks about risk and cost. My first step: ask the owner of the family restaurant where I used to work which outage would hurt her most, and rank my rules by her answer.

**Example D · A study-group scheduling app**
- *The key obstacle:* Not seeing where another domain fits. I treated the app as a coding problem: the database, the sign-in and the calendar view.
- *What my project goal may be missing:* My goal is an app that students in my major actually use to form study groups. I have not asked anyone how they find a study group today, so I do not know whether a calendar is what they need, or whether they would open a new app at all.
- *A domain that could fill the gap:* Understanding what users need. My first step: ask five classmates how they found their last study group, and write down where it broke down.
