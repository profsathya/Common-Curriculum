## Worked examples (shown beside the form)

**Example A · A campus office-hours tool**
- *The second domain and how it is integrated:* Understanding what users need. I talked with four students and two office staff about where they lose time when they look for help on campus. It met my main project where I decide what the search returns. The students told me they search by the problem they have, such as "drop a class", and not by the name of the office.
- *The key change:* Before, my search returned every office page that matched a word. Now it uses a list I built from the conversations, which matches twelve common problems to the office that handles each one, so a search for "drop a class" goes to the Registrar first.
- *The evidence:* I timed five students finding the right office for three problems, once on the old version and once on the new one. The average time went from 2 minutes 10 seconds to 40 seconds. The timings and the problem list are linked on my poster.

**Example B · A contribution to Kolibri, an open-source learning platform**
- *The second domain and how it is integrated:* How teachers use Kolibri in classrooms without reliable internet. I read the project's guides for setting it up offline and two issue threads written by teachers. It met my main work when I chose which issue to fix: a sync between two devices that failed without telling anyone when the connection was slow.
- *The key change:* Before, I planned to fix only the error behind the failure. Now my fix also shows the teacher a message that the sync did not finish and will try again, because the teachers' threads showed that not knowing was the real problem for them.
- *The evidence:* My pull request, with a test that slows the connection and checks that the message appears, and the maintainer's review of it. Both are linked on my poster.

**Example C · A Security+ certificate with a home lab**
- *The second domain and how it is integrated:* How a small business owner thinks about risk and cost. I talked with the owner of a family restaurant about what would hurt her business most if her network failed. It met my main work when I chose the firewall rules for my lab network, which I set up to match her restaurant.
- *The key change:* Before, I ranked the rules by the order they appear in my study guide. Now I rank them by what would cost her most if it failed, and I removed two rules that would have blocked updates to her card reader.
- *The evidence:* A one-page summary of the rules I kept, written for her, with her written answers to three questions I asked after she read it, and my lab test log showing the card reader still updates. Both are linked on my poster.
