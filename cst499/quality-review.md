# Quality review: the two or three changes that step up the quality of my work, and the domains they bring together

Week 9 · due Monday 26 October, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

## Purpose

Review the current quality of your project's outcomes, choose the two or three changes that will step it up, and decide which domains of knowledge and skills you will bring together to make them. Clearly identifying the current quality of a project and ways to improve it is a skill that will remain valuable and be noticed throughout your career.

The grade is for the student's thinking, so keep it in view: before you produce something, ask what they expect and why; after, ask what they would check or change. ## Tasks

“Your work” means what exists today — what runs, what is written, what you showed in the mid-term — not what you plan to build.

Each **+** opens guidance, one example, a reflection question and a box to write in. Nothing under a **+** is required.

### Task 1 — Where my work stands

Describe the current quality of your work: what it does well today, and what someone who holds the job you want would expect to see that is not there yet.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 1.** What your work does well today, and what is not there yet.
*Placeholder:* Does well — … ⏎ Not there yet — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- Both parts are about work that exists today, not about the plan.
- The "not there yet" part names something a reader could look for in your work and confirm is missing.
- The comparison is to work by people who hold the job you want, not to classmates.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** Open your work as a stranger would — run it, read it — and write down what it does well and what is missing. For the second part, picture the work a person who holds the job you want produces, and name what theirs has that yours does not yet. Two or three sentences per part is enough.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Does well** — the search box returns the right office for the twelve campus services we loaded, and a new student found tutoring hours in under a minute.
- **Not there yet** — the data is a hand-typed list; a product manager at the company I want would expect to see where the data comes from and how it stays current.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which part of your answer would you be least comfortable showing to that person today?

### Task 2 — The two or three changes

Identify the two or three changes that would step up the quality of your work the most, and say what each one changes for the person who uses or reads the work.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 2.** Two or three changes, numbered; for each, what it changes for the user or reader.
*Placeholder:* 1. Change — … What it changes — … ⏎ 2. … ⏎ 3. …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- There are two or three changes, not a longer list.
- Each change answers something named in task 1.
- Each change says what is different for the user or reader once it is made, not only what you will do.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** Start from what task 1 says is not there yet. Choose the two or three changes that would move your work the most, and for each write what the user or reader can do or see afterwards that they cannot now. Changes you can finish before the portfolio piece is due (Monday 16 November) count more than changes that would need the whole semester.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Change 1** — pull the office hours from each office's published page instead of a typed list. What it changes — a student sees the hours the office itself posted, and the site stops going stale.
- **Change 2** — add a short "how this data is kept current" page. What it changes — a reader can see the process, not take my word for it.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which of your changes would the hiring manager notice first?

### Task 3 — The domains each change brings together

For each change in task 2, name the domains of knowledge or skills it brings together, and what each domain contributes to the change.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 3.** For each change, matching the numbers in task 2: the domains, and what each contributes.
*Placeholder:* 1. Domains — … What each contributes — … ⏎ 2. … ⏎ 3. …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- Every change from task 2 has an answer here, with the same number.
- At least one change brings together more than one domain.
- For each domain, you say what it contributes to that change — a reader could see why the change needs it.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** A domain is an area of knowledge or skill that stands on its own — a programming area, a field the project serves, a way of working with people, a way of handling data. For each change, write which domains it needs and what each one contributes. Where a change needs only one domain, say so; the Integrative Solving report in week 11 will draw on the change that needs more than one.

*Tooltip on “domains”* — **Domains** — *What it is.* Areas of knowledge or skill that stand on their own: a technical area, the field your project serves, a way of working with people, a way of handling data. *Why it matters here.* Bringing more than one domain together on a real problem is the evidence of a T-shaped professional, and the week-11 report asks for that evidence.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Change 1** — Domains: web scraping and scheduling (technical); how campus offices publish and update their pages (the field). What each contributes — the technical side fetches the hours on a schedule; knowing how offices publish tells me which page to fetch and how often it changes.
- **Change 2** — Domain: technical writing for a reader who is not the author. What it contributes — a page a stranger can follow.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which of these domains is one you are still learning, and where did it show up in your Learning Plan?

## Criteria

**Checklist** — one line per task. Run it on your own draft; your reviewer runs the same list. *(the click-in at the top of Criteria)*

1. Describe what your work does well today and what is not there yet, compared with work by people who hold the job you want.
2. Name two or three changes, each with what it changes for the user or reader.
3. For each change, name the domains it brings together and what each contributes.

**Done** — task 1 covers both parts and is about work that exists today; task 2 has two or three numbered changes, each with what it changes for the user or reader; task 3 answers for every change with the same numbers, and at least one change brings together more than one domain.

**Strong** — the "not there yet" in task 1 is something a reader could confirm by looking at your work; the changes in task 2 answer task 1 directly and can be finished by Monday 16 November; in task 3, what each domain contributes is specific enough that a reader could see why the change needs it.
