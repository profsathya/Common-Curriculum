Set up your AI Dojo

Do this before week 1
Your Dojo is your own AI — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — set up once as a coach that sharpens your thinking instead of doing the work for you. Fifteen minutes to set up, and you use it all semester.

Purpose

Your Dojo is your own AI — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — set up once as a coach that sharpens your thinking instead of doing the work for you. You set it up in about fifteen minutes and use it all semester.

Step 1 — Sign in, then create the project

Sign in first. All three tools need an account. ChatGPT will let you type without one, but it cannot save a project until you are signed in.

ChatGPT. chatgpt.com → sidebar → Projects → New project → name it CST499 Dojo.

Claude. claude.ai → sidebar → Projects → New project → name it CST499 Dojo.

Gemini. gemini.google.com → Gems → New Gem → name it CST499 Dojo. If Gems is not in the sidebar, open Settings — it is listed there.

Pick the model. In your tool’s model selector, choose the latest, most capable model you have access to — the Dojo coaches best when it can reason about your work. A “thinking” / reasoning model gives sharper, more careful feedback but pauses longer before each reply; a faster standard model answers quickly with a little less depth. If the waits feel long, switch to the faster one; if the feedback feels shallow, switch to the thinking one.

Step 2 — Paste the instruction

Find the project’s Instructions field (ChatGPT: project settings · Claude: “Set project instructions” · Gemini: the Gem’s Instructions box), paste this, and save:

You are my Dojo — a coach that sharpens my thinking. Follow the Dojo Core file I’ve added to this project: it defines how you behave (the characters and the rules).

Always check the latest version of the Google doc(s) in your context at the beginning of any chat — do not use a cached copy of the document(s). These documents are updated on a regular basis, so it is important that you read the latest version from the source Drive each time we start.

The document has more than one tab. Read all of them: Dojo (how you work with me), Course (the course material), and Syllabus (policies, schedule, grading, and what is required). When I ask about deadlines, requirements, or how the grade works, the Syllabus tab is where to look — answer from it and tell me which tab you found it in. If a tab is missing, carry on with the ones that are there.

The core rule: I make the decisions and do the thinking. You help me see what to change and — once I’ve decided what to say — help me write it well. You never decide for me, and you never invent anything I didn’t give you.

Guide me through questions, not answers — one focused question at a time. I make the decisions and do the thinking; you help me see what to change, and you help me word things only after I have decided what to say. Never write my proposal, my evidence log, or my assignments for me — when I ask you to just do it, ask for my facts and my rough version first, then help me sharpen it. Acknowledge good thinking; name what is weak and why, without making me feel small. If course files are loaded in this project, follow them; when you do not know something about the course, say so instead of guessing.

Be warm and supportive, and be direct and honest — you’re a coach in my corner, not a critic and not a hype machine. Acknowledge real thinking when you see it; tell me plainly when something is weak or wrong and why; don’t cushion a hard truth into vagueness. Keep it conversational, challenge shaky assumptions, and leave me room to think. Keep each turn short — one point, then one focused question; save the long bulleted read-outs for when I ask for the full picture.

Step 3 — Link the course document. Link, not copy.

The document is CST499 — Dojo. It carries several tabs — Dojo, Course, and Syllabus — so linking it once gives your Dojo the method, the material, and the course policies together. Add it by its Google Drive link — do not paste its text in. The document is updated through the semester: a linked doc updates with it, a pasted copy freezes at today’s version and your Dojo falls behind the course.

Claude. Connect Drive before you can pick the file: Settings → Connectors → Google Drive. Then, in the project, click + and the Drive option appears.

ChatGPT. In the project’s sources, add the doc and connect Google Drive when it asks.

Gemini. The Gem’s Knowledge → Drive → choose the document.

If the connector dialog hangs or the tab crashes, reload the page and try again — it usually connects on the second attempt.

Criteria — you are set up when

Open a chat inside your project and ask: “I would like to understand the purpose of this course.”

You are set up when the answer is grounded in the course document rather than generic advice, and it comes back the way a coach would — a short answer, then one question that puts the thinking back on you. If it hands you an essay, or clearly has not read the document, check that the instruction saved and the document is linked.

If it still is not behaving, bring it to your first check-in — we will fix it together.

What to submit

Submit this in Canvas as AI Dojo Setup — one text box, due Friday 28 August, 11:59 p.m. It sits in Own your progress guidance, which means it carries points you can see but does not count toward your grade. Its job is to catch a Dojo that is not set up properly now, in week 1, rather than in week 5 when you actually need it.

Paste in a conversation you had with your Dojo. That is the whole submission. Before Friday, use it for something you genuinely wanted to know — a question about the course, about a project you are weighing up, about the job market, about anything you were stuck on — and then paste that chat into the text box.

Do not tidy it up. The messy parts are the useful parts: where you said you still did not get it, where you told it that it was wrong, where you changed your mind halfway through. If you had more than one chat, paste the one that did the most work.

Getting the chat out and into Canvas

Scrolling up and highlighting a long conversation is fiddly, and it usually drops something. Get the Dojo to hand it to you in one piece instead:

Reproduce our entire conversation as one message I can copy and paste.

Rules:
— Include every turn, in order, from my very first message to this one.
— Put “Me:” or “Dojo:” on its own line before each turn, with a blank line between turns.
— Use my exact words and your exact words. Do not summarise, shorten, fix, or reorder anything, and do not add commentary before or after.
— Keep lists and code blocks formatted the way they appeared.
— If it will not fit in one message, stop at a turn boundary and end with the single word CONTINUED. I will reply “continue” and you pick up from exactly where you stopped.

Start with one header line: CST499 — Dojo transcript — [today’s date].

Then, in order:

1. Click Copy on the box above, paste it into your Dojo chat as your last message, and send it.

2. The Dojo answers with the whole conversation as one block of text. Select that reply and copy it — click at the very start of the reply, drag to the very end, then press Ctrl + C (Cmd + C on a Mac). Some tools show a small copy icon underneath the reply, which does the same thing in one click.

3. Open the AI Dojo Setup assignment in Canvas and click inside the text box.

4. Paste — Ctrl + V (Cmd + V on a Mac).

5. Click Submit, then reopen your submission and scroll to the bottom to check the whole conversation actually landed. Long pastes sometimes get cut short, and it is much easier to fix now than after the deadline.

If the Dojo’s reply stops partway and ends with CONTINUED, type continue, then paste both parts one after the other.

You are done when

Your submission holds one whole conversation with your Dojo, start to finish, and reading it shows a coach working with you rather than a machine writing for you.

If your Dojo is still not behaving like a coach — if it writes essays at you, or clearly has not read the course document — say so at the top of your submission and bring it to your first check-in. Telling us it is not working is a perfectly good submission. Saying nothing is not.
