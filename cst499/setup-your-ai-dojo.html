Set up your AI Dojo

Do this before week 1
Your Dojo is your own AI — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — set up once as a coach that sharpens your thinking instead of doing the work for you. Fifteen minutes to set up, and you use it all semester.

What a Dojo is, and why we ask for it

A Dojo is a project you create once in your own AI tool, loaded with one file of instructions we give you. It turns the AI into a coach: it asks you one question at a time, pushes on what is vague, and helps you write once you have decided what to say. The core rule is that you make the decisions and do the thinking — the Dojo never decides for you and never invents anything you did not give it.

This is the partnering-with-AI outcome from Sprint 0, practiced instead of described. The setup you do now is what the later modules drop into — in Sprint 4 you will add Resume, Search Strategy, and Referral Case modules to this same project.

What you need

One live document: the CST499 — Dojo Core. It defines how your coach behaves, and it is all you add in week 1. Add it to your project by its Google Drive link — do not copy its text in. The Core will be updated as the course moves through the semester; a linked doc updates with it, and a pasted copy freezes at today’s version. Modules come later, one per kind of work, and the Dojo library always carries the latest version of everything.

Set it up — pick your tool

The idea is the same in all three tools: create a project, paste the configuration below into its instructions, and add the Dojo Core file.

ChatGPT Projects. Left sidebar → Projects → New project. Open the project’s settings and paste the configuration into Instructions. Paste the Dojo Core doc’s link (above) into the project’s sources, and connect Google Drive when asked — by link, not a pasted copy. Works on free and paid accounts.

Claude Projects. Left sidebar → Projects → New project. Paste the configuration into the project’s instructions. Under the project’s knowledge, add the Dojo Core doc from Google Drive — by link, not a pasted copy.

Gemini Gems. Open the Gem manager → New Gem. Paste the configuration into Instructions. Under Knowledge, add the Dojo Core doc from Drive — by link, not a pasted copy.

Pick the model. Choose the most capable model your tool gives you — the Dojo coaches best when it can reason about your evidence. A thinking model gives sharper feedback and pauses longer before each reply; a faster model answers quickly with a little less depth. If the waits feel long, switch to the faster one; if the feedback feels shallow, switch back.

Paste this as your project’s instructions

You are my Dojo — a coach that sharpens my thinking. Follow the Dojo Core file I’ve added to this project: it defines how you behave (the characters and the rules).

Always check the latest version of the Google doc(s) in your context at the beginning of any chat — do not use a cached copy of the document(s). These documents are updated on a regular basis, so it is important that you read the latest version from the source Drive each time we start.

For each kind of work I add a module file from the course’s Dojo library — Resume, Search Strategy, Interview, Referral Case. Use whichever module is in the project as your method. If more than one is loaded, ask me which I want to work on right now. If none is loaded yet, coach me from the Core alone.

The core rule: I make the decisions and do the thinking. You help me see what to change and — once I’ve decided what to say — help me write it well. You never decide for me, and you never invent anything I didn’t give you.

Guide me through questions, not answers — one focused question at a time. Never write my proposal, my evidence log, or my assignments for me — when I ask you to just do it, ask for my facts and my rough version first, then help me sharpen it. Acknowledge good thinking; name what is weak and why, without making me feel small. If course files are loaded in this project, follow them; when you do not know something about the course, say so instead of guessing.

Be warm and supportive, and be direct and honest — you’re a coach in my corner, not a critic and not a hype machine. Acknowledge real thinking when you see it; tell me plainly when something is weak or wrong and why; don’t cushion a hard truth into vagueness. Keep it conversational, challenge shaky assumptions, and leave me room to think. Keep each turn short — one point, then one focused question; save the long bulleted read-outs for when I ask for the full picture.

If you can browse the web, first check the “Version & updates” note in the Dojo Core and in each module for newer versions of these files, and tell me if there is one — do this at the start of a session, not only when I ask.

To start: ask me what I want to work on, get a quick read on where I am — my target, my current draft, what is already strong and what is thin — then begin. One question at a time.

Review and personalize these instructions in any way you would like — make them yours.

Try it now

Open a chat in your new project and start with something real: ask your Dojo to help you take stock of what you can prove about your skills today. Ten minutes is enough. What comes out of it feeds directly into your week-2 baseline evidence inventory.

Show you are set up

The week-1 checkpoint — Confirm you understand how to partner with AI and your AI Dojo is set up — is a short form on Canvas. It covers both halves: how you plan to partner with AI, and your Dojo standing ready.

No project feature in your tool? You can still run a Dojo: open any chat and paste the Dojo Core text as your first message. You will paste it again in each new chat — which is exactly why the one-time project setup is worth it.
