CST499 · Computer Science Capstone · Fall 2026 · 4 units

Know Your Niche, Build the Proof

Graduate with evidence a specific employer would find convincing.
The entry-level market for software work is moving fast. This semester you work out the slice of it you fit, name the gap between what you can prove today and what those roles ask for, and spend the middle of the term building the proof that closes it. You run your search with that work in hand.
Computing Talent
Initiative

Six sprints across sixteen instructional weeks. Two of them overlap on purpose: your niche and your gap sharpen each other through weeks 3–5, and your search starts in week 13, before the build is finished. Fall Break is the week of 23 November.

Beyond the grade
What you build here is meant to be used.

In Sprint 4, Prof. Sathya can send a referral email on your behalf — an introduction to someone he knows at a company, backed by his name. It is optional, and it goes to students whose week-13 artifact clears the published bar, which is why it comes after you have built something.

We are also planning an invitation-only virtual networking session with engineers from industry, so you finish the semester with people in your corner as well as a portfolio.

Sprint 4 · Own the search while finishing the proofWeeks 13–15 · Nov 16 – Dec 13▸
What you will be able to do
  • Turn “I am applying to jobs” into a stated bet, with a fair test and a signal you would accept
  • Rebuild a resume around evidence rather than coursework
  • Make a written case for yourself strong enough that someone would put their name behind it
  • Hold up in an interview about work you actually did
Why this comes after building. A search is far easier to run when you have something to point at. You arrive here with finished work, which is what turns “I am interested in X” into “here is X, working.”
