Build Work Worth Putting in Your Professional Portfolio

You graduate with a piece of work you can put in front of a hiring manager, and the ability to explain every decision in it.
What has changed about getting hired?
The entry-level market for computing is moving fast. This semester you do three things with it. First, work out the Your nicheWhat it means. The specific slice of the job market you are aiming at — a kind of work, in a kind of company, solving a kind of problem.Why it matters. “Software engineer” describes two hundred thousand openings and helps you target none of them. A niche is what makes your resume, your project choice, and your cover letter all point the same direction.What a good one looks like. Specific enough that the description stops being true if you swap the industry out. “Backend work on data pipelines at climate-tech companies” passes. “Full-stack at a startup” does not.. Then name the biggest The gap in your preparationWhat it means. The distance between what you can prove about your skills today and what the jobs in your niche ask for.Why it matters. Your whole capstone is the answer to this one sentence. If the gap is vague, the work you choose to close it will be vague too.What a good one looks like. Named, specific, and closeable in one semester. “I have never shipped code another engineer reviewed” is a gap. “I need more experience” is not.. Then build the work that closes it. Doing that once, end to end, is what lets you do it again every time the market moves.

+ The big picture What you get out of this semester, and how you get there
What you get

Develop the ability to adapt as the market changes. Three capabilities carry that, and your project builds all three: you Adaptive BuilderWhat it means. Executing when the full information is not available and the right answer cannot be reasoned out in advance — build, test, learn from what failed, adapt, go again.The part people miss. Restraint. Simple solutions executed well usually deliver more value than sophisticated ones, and iteration is how you find that out.What you do with it here. Your project is built in versions, and the second version has to be measurably better against a bar you did not set yourself.; you Integrative SolverWhat it means. Working at the intersections — between systems of knowledge, and between the people whose perspectives have to be reconciled — which is exactly where AI struggles and human value concentrates.What it is not. Building the thing you were asked for. It is spending enough time in the other domain to find the problem worth solving.What you do with it here. You add a second area of depth to your project, and write up what bringing the two together made possible.; and you Self-Directed LearnerWhat it means. Acquiring expertise in an unfamiliar domain fast — knowing what you need to learn versus what you can strategically ignore, and using AI as learning infrastructure to build just-in-time depth.Why the build teaches it. The work tells you which gaps are real. Learning because the work demands it is how professionals learn.What you leave with. A learning plan you can point at as proof, not just a claim that you pick things up quickly..

How this course was made

The design of this course is mine — what you’ll practice, what you’ll practice it on, how you’ll be guided, and how you’ll be graded, the focus on human connection through conversations — and I own the design of your learning experience. AI does the building: turning those decisions into pages, assignments, and your coach — that I review and test. That’s what lets me be more nimble and adapt to improve the course so I can serve all of you well.

What you are graduating into has changed, and that kind of change asks for innovation — trying approaches that fit the new situation better, building them as well as we can, and learning from what happens. This course is one of those attempts. Paige and I invite you to take it in that spirit: try the approach, tell us what is helping and what is getting in your way, and we will adjust while the semester is still running.

What that looks like — what I decided, what AI built, and what it gets you

What I decided

  • You choose from a bounded set of real problem spaces. A blank page stalls most people and an assigned project belongs to someone else, so you get real problems with room to pick the one you want to own.
  • You build before you search. The middle of the semester goes into finishing two pieces of work, so that when you start talking to people you have something to point at.
  • Certification is an equal route, paired with an applied piece — because what you did with it is the part that carries.
  • Conversations are how the work is graded. Assignments and interviews mirror each other, so turning something in is the start and explaining it is what raises the letter. You will have three to five interviews, with the early rounds in small groups.
  • The Capstone Festival is designed by the class, and up to five earned referrals are the one thing on offer beyond the grade.
  • What every assignment is for, the guidance around it, and how it is graded are mine.

What AI built, and how we check it

  • These pages. Drafted from my design, with the parts that make them usable — the week-by-week plan you can read end to end, the terms you can hover over for a definition.
  • The Canvas side. Assignments, modules and due dates set up and held together, so nothing falls between a page and the gradebook.
  • Your AI coach, generated from this same course design — which is why it knows what you are working on, and can meet you at eleven at night when I can’t.
  • How we check it. Paige and I click through the pages, submit the Canvas items ourselves before you ever see them, and run the coach through a student conversation to see how it answers. Where it comes out wrong, I edit it by hand. The entry-level market figures on this page come from the Stanford Digital Economy Lab’s payroll data, checked against the source; the problem spaces were checked against each project’s own contributor documentation.

What that combination gets you

  • A course that can change while you are in it. Because a decision becomes a built page quickly now, something that isn’t working in week 4 can be different in week 5 rather than next fall.
  • That speed is spent on you. It goes into adapting the course so it works for everyone in the room — not only for the students it already happened to fit.
  • And it is the thing you are here to learn. Directing AI to build something you designed, then checking it hard enough to stand behind it, is the same skill this course asks of you. This page is what it looks like when I do it.
