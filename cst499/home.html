CST499 · Computer Science Capstone · Fall 2026

Find Your Niche, Build the Proof

Graduate with evidence that a specific employer would find convincing.
The entry-level market for software work is moving fast. This semester you'll work out a specific slice of it you fit, find the gap between what you can prove today and what those roles actually ask for, and spend the back half building the proof that closes it — work that goes straight into your search.
Computing Talent
Initiative

Four parts. The first half works out where you fit and what you're missing. The second half builds it, and the Capstone Festival is where you show it.

Beyond the grade
What you build here is meant to be used, not filed.

From week 12, Prof. Sathya can send referral emails on your behalf — a real introduction to a real person at a company, backed by his name. That is not a participation prize. It goes to students whose work makes a case he can stand behind, and the whole first half of this course is you building that case.

We're also planning an invitation-only virtual networking session with engineers from industry, so you finish the semester with people in your corner as well as a portfolio.

Part 1 · Know Your NicheWeeks 1–4 · Aug 24 – Sep 20▸
What you'll be able to do
  • Read current job-market data and say what it means for your own next twelve months
  • Name what you're good at using evidence someone else would find convincing — not adjectives
  • Describe a specific slice of the market you fit, specific enough that the sentence breaks if you swap the sector out
  • Use AI as a thinking partner without handing it the thinking
Start here. Everything you build in the second half comes out of these four weeks. The clearer you are here, the less of your capstone is guesswork.
Week 1 (Aug 24) — What's actually happening out there
The entry-level market, in payroll dataIn person
Stanford's Canaries dashboard tracks 4.6M+ workers' actual payroll records. Early-career workers (22–25) in the most AI-exposed occupations are down 5.4% over the past year — software developers among them, while mid-career in the same job holds steady. We'll look at the real chart together and talk about what it means for you.

Part 3 · Close the GapWeeks 8–13 · Oct 12 – Nov 22▸
What you'll be able to do
  • Scope work that genuinely closes your gap — and defend why it's the right work
  • Ship something that functions, not something that demos
  • Keep going past the point where it stops being fun — the part employers are actually asking about
  • Explain your work to someone who isn't an engineer, in their terms
Builds on: the gap you named in Week 7. Your capstone is whatever closes it — a project, a shipped artifact, a certification, or some combination. What matters is that it closes your gap, and that you can show it does.
Week 8 (Oct 12) — Your capstone proposal
What you'll build, and why it closes your gapMajor
Scope, plan, and the evidence you'll have at the end. Mentored projects, self-directed builds, and certification paths all answer the same question here.
Weeks 9–13 — Build
Protected build time
Class time is yours to work in. Optional deep-work sessions run alongside — come if it helps you get it done.
Progress conversations — roughly every three weeksIn person
Small-group. We pick one piece of your evidence and go deep on it: what you did, why, what broke, what you changed. Preparation is the point — these replace the old written progress quizzes.
Week 12 (Nov 9) — Referrals open
Make your case for a referralOpt in
