CST499 · Computer Science Capstone · Fall 2026 · 4 units

Find Your Niche, Build the Proof

Graduate with evidence that a specific employer would find convincing.
The entry-level market for software work is moving fast. This semester you'll work out a specific slice of it you fit, find the gap between what you can prove today and what those roles actually ask for, and spend the middle of the term building the proof that closes it — then run your search with that work in hand.
Computing Talent
Initiative

Five parts. Work out where you fit, build two pieces of proof, then run your search with them in hand — and finish at the Capstone Festival.

In Part 3, Prof. Sathya can send referral emails on your behalf — a real introduction to a real person at a company, backed by his name. That is not a participation prize. It goes to students whose work makes a case he can stand behind, which is why it comes after you've built something: by then you have two finished pieces to point at, not adjectives.

We're also planning an invitation-only virtual networking session with engineers from industry, so you finish the semester with people in your corner as well as a portfolio.

Part 2 · Close the GapWeeks 6–11 · Sep 28 – Nov 8 · two sprints▸
What you'll be able to do
  • Scope work that genuinely closes your gap — and defend why it's the right work
  • Ship something that functions rather than something that demos. Twice.
  • Do it better the second time, because you learned something the first time
  • Keep going past the point where it stops being fun — the part employers are actually asking about
  • Explain what you built to someone who isn't an engineer, in their terms
Builds on: the gap you named in Week 5. Your capstone is whatever closes it — a project, a shipped artifact, a certification, or a combination. What matters is that it closes your gap and that you can show it does. Two sprints, two pieces of proof — the second one is where you show you learned how to build, not just that you built.

Part 3 · Own Your Search & Warm ReferralWeeks 12–15 · Nov 9 – Dec 6▸
What you'll be able to do
  • Turn "I'm applying to jobs" into a stated bet you can test — with a fair test and a signal you'd accept
  • Rebuild your resume around the two things you just built
  • Make a written case for yourself strong enough that someone would put their name behind it
  • Use a warm introduction well when you get one
Why this comes after building. A search is far easier to run when you have something to point at. You arrive at this part with two finished artifacts, which is what turns "I'm interested in X" into "here's X, working."
