The form

Task 1 · Why this project

In your Project Proposal you named a gap your capstone would close. Think through your choice of project against that gap:
  • What were at least two other ways you could have closed the same gap?
  • Why did you choose this project instead of those?
  • What would have made you choose one of the others?
  • What is this choice likely to mean for you, both the good and the bad?
(The other ways should be real options: a different project, a certificate, a job or internship, a course, or a different kind of work on the same problem. Your reasons are strongest when they come from outside you, such as a job posting, a person in the field, a user, or a result you saw.)

Task 2 · A choice inside my work

Think through one choice you made inside your work, such as a tool or method you chose, something you decided to cut or add, or a change of direction partway through:
  • What was the choice, and what were at least two other directions you could have taken?
  • Why did you choose this one instead of those?
  • What would have made you choose one of the others?
  • What is this choice likely to mean for your work, both the good and the bad?
(Choose a choice that changed what you did next. If you could undo it in five minutes, it is probably not the one.)

Task 3 · My decision card

Which of your two decisions goes on your Interactive Poster, and what does the card say? Include:
  • the card's title, in the shape "[what you chose] instead of [another option you considered]"
  • the reason, in one line that starts with "Because"
  • one thing a visitor can look at that supports the reason
  • one sentence on what this choice cost you
(Add the card to your poster before the rehearsal on Wednesday 2 December, so a classmate can see it.)

Worked example

Example · A Security+ certificate with a home lab

(the second sample poster, written as answers)

Why this project:
The gap I named was that I could not show hands-on security work, only coursework. Two other ways to close it were to study for the Security+ exam on my own, or to build a small software tool that checks a network for open ports. I chose the certificate with a home lab because 10 of the 12 analyst postings I saved named Security+, and most of them asked for hands-on investigation, which the lab gives me and studying alone does not. If most of the postings had asked for development experience first, I would have built the tool. The good: by December I will have the exam and a written investigation a visitor can look at. The bad: I will not have an app that is my own, and if I do not pass on December 12 the claim is weaker.
A choice inside my work:
My practice scores were stuck around 62% for three weeks, and I chose to pause exam prep for two weeks to learn networking basics. Two other directions were to keep taking practice exams until the score moved, or to drill flashcards on only the questions I missed. I chose the pause because when I sorted my wrong answers, most of them were networking questions, so more practice tests would have repeated the same gap. If my wrong answers had been spread across every topic, I would have kept taking practice exams. The good: my score rose to 81%. The bad: my exam date moved back two weeks.
My decision card:
Security+ and a home lab instead of studying for the exam alone. Because: 10 of the 12 postings I saved named Security+, and analyst roles ask for hands-on investigation. A visitor can look at the diagram of my three lab machines on a network that cannot reach the internet. It cost me time I could have spent on exam study, and I had to learn how to keep the lab safely cut off.
