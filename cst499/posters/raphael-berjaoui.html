CST499 · Capstone

Interactive Poster

Raphael BerjaouiSoftware Engineering

I can build a game that recognizes what a player draws and turns it into a weapon, and I can use game design to give each weapon a clear role.

Capstone: Doodle Arsenal, a first-person game where you draw your own weapons

The work

The game has to tell which weapon a player drew from a messy doodle

Why it matters: drawing is the whole idea of the game, so if a player draws a sniper and gets a shotgun, the game feels unfair

Decision

Melee kills in one hit and a sniper headshot kills in one shot, instead of all weapons doing similar damage

Because: if every weapon felt the same, it would not matter what the player drew. Each weapon needs a strength and a risk

Obstacle or open question

Every gun is a long sideways shape, so the recognizer mixes them up

What I did or will try: gave each gun a feature to draw (scope, magazine, pump), added a teach mode to record more examples, and the notepad says “not sure” when it can’t decide

Where it stands: the menu, settings, movement, drawing recognition for melee and four guns, aiming, headshots and the rainbow power-up work in the training room. Enemies, sound, a pause menu and accuracy testing with other players aren’t done yet.

What a visitor can see proven now

The notepad naming a drawing as melee, pistol, shotgun, machine gun or sniper, and saying “not sure” when it can’t decide. Each weapon’s damage, headshots, and the rainbow star working on the training dummy.

What I will change and why

Right now a tiny pistol and a huge pistol work exactly the same, so drawing carefully gives the player nothing. I will make the size of the drawing change the weapon: a bigger drawing hits harder but fires slower and moves you slower.

When and with what

By 23 Nov: a one-page design sheet with the size rules for each weapon. By 30 Nov: build it in Godot, using the drawing’s size that the game already measures. 2 Dec rehearsal: have classmates draw each weapon and count how many it gets right. By 5 Dec: record a short video of each card and add the links.
