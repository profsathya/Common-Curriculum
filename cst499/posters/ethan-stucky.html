Capstone: Autonomous Vehicle Sensor Simulation for Aprentech

The work

Contribute toward the development of a low-speed autonomous vehicle.

Why it matters: Aprentech's goals span engineering, as well as education, this project would serve to inspire and teach students the opportunities and possibilities of robotics and engineering.

Decision

Build the simulation in Gazebo.

Because: Gazebo is free and open source, and supports integration with other open source robotics operating systems.

Obstacle or open question

Original project was bringing online a physical sensor system, but the vehicle for the project never materialized.

What I did or will try: I found simulation tools to model what the sensors would likely have been. 

Where it stands: [what is finished, and what isn’t yet]

The work

Evidence

At present the evidence shows as a recorded demo of the simulation environment of the vehicle with both sensors visualized. the points surrounding the vehicle represent the lidar contacts, and on the top right of the recording shows the depth camera feed color coded according to the distance data in the image.

Open evidence

What I’m still not sure about

My next open question is to investigate how to translate this to a physical system running a supported OS like ROS2, or to explore integrating a path solver to start autonomous navigation using the sensors data.

How I worked with AI

AI heavily drove the implementation. While I directed the design of the simulation. I started with a simple car inspired from built in examples. Then changed to a 4 wheel design with an Ackermann steering system over a single front caster wheel.

The sensors similarly inspired from built in examples. Then I generated an environment with shapes for the vehicle and sensors to interact with.

A custom depth camera feed was generated and the polishes were project file reorganization, and GUI changes
