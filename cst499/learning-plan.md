# Learning Plan

Week 6 · due Monday 5 October, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

## Purpose

Identify what you individually must learn to complete your project, and plan how you will learn it and how you will prove that you did.

## Tasks

“You” means you personally, also in a team project: what you must learn, not what the team must.

**Button:** Copy the questions — Paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it.

Each **+** opens guidance, one example, a reflection question and a box to write in. Nothing under a **+** is required.

Identify what you individually must learn to complete the whole project — the skills, tools, concepts or domain knowledge you do not have yet. List each one.

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- Each item is something you do not have yet, and the project needs it.
- The items are numbered, so tasks 2 to 5 can refer to them.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** List the specific skills, tools, concepts or domain knowledge you do not have yet and the project needs — one line each, numbered, because tasks 2 to 5 come back to this list item by item. The list is yours even on a team project: what you must learn, not what a teammate already knows.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Item 1** — how the offices that own campus information actually publish and update it (interviews, and one office’s process end to end).
- **Item 2** — a form of accessible search on a static site.
- **Item 3** — a way for an office to edit its own entry without me.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which item did you find hardest to admit to the list?

### Task 5 — Verification

For each item, state how you will verify you learned it: what you will be able to show, what it will demonstrate, and what external verification step, if any, you will use to confirm it — a review by someone who knows the area, a test with a real user, a result others can check.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 5.** Label each answer by item, matching task 1: Item 1 — …, Item 2 — …
*Placeholder:* Item 1 — what I will show; what it demonstrates; external step (or none planned). ⏎ Item 2 — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- Every item from task 1 has a verification here, labelled with the same number.
- Each one says what you will show and what it demonstrates, and a stranger could check it by looking.
- Each one says whether an external step is planned, and at least one item has one: a named reviewer, a real user, or a result others can check.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** For each item, write three things: what you will be able to show, what it demonstrates, and what external verification step, if any, you will use. Not every item needs an external step; say for each whether you plan one.

*Tooltip on “external verification step”* — **External verification** — *What it is.* Someone or something outside you confirming that what you show is real: a review by a person who knows the area, a test with a real user, a result others can check for themselves. *Why it matters.* A hiring manager can only take your word for what you learned; they can act on a coordinator’s email or a timed test with three students. The external step is what turns a claim into evidence.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Item 1** — the one-page process description, confirmed by the coordinator as accurate (their email).
- **Item 2** — a new student finds tutoring hours from the search box in under a minute, timed, three students.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which verification would carry the most weight with a hiring manager?

## Criteria

**Checklist** — one line per task. Run it on your own draft; your reviewer runs the same list. *(the click-in at the top of Criteria)*

1. List each thing you must learn, one line each, numbered.
2. For each item, say which part of the work cannot be done, or done well, without it.
3. For each item, name the resources, with links where links exist.
4. For each item, give the action steps: what, in what order, and when.
5. For each item, say what you will show, what it demonstrates, and whether an external verification step is planned.
6. Optional: link or screenshot what exists and runs today.

**Done** — task 1 lists each thing to be learned; tasks 2 to 5 cover every item in that list; the resources in 3 are linked where links exist; the action steps in 4 say what, in what order, and when; the verification in 5 says what will be shown and what it demonstrates, and says whether an external verification step is planned.

**Strong** — each item in 1 is something the project cannot be finished without, and 2 says which part; the resources in 3 are ones you have opened, not a search result; the action steps in 4 have dates that fit between now and the mid-term scope; the verification in 5 is something a stranger could check, and at least one item has an external step — a person who knows the area, a real user, a result others can check.

*Tooltip on “mid-term scope”* — **The mid-term scope** — *What it is.* What you said in task 6a of your Project Proposal would exist by week 8 — the mid-term, Wednesday 14 October. *Why it matters here.* The learning that scope needs has to happen before it; steps dated after 14 October belong to a later part of the project.
