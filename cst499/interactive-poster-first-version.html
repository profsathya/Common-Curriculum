Look back

What an Interactive Poster is

At the festival you sit at your laptop and visitors sit beside you. Your Interactive Poster is what they see on your screen. The first screen fits one laptop screen: your claim at the top, one line for each card, and one line on where your work stands. When a visitor asks about a card, you open it and show the evidence behind it.

Look at the two sample posters before you start. They come from two composite students, one contributing to an open-source project and one earning a Security+ certificate with a home lab.

Build your poster in any tool that runs in a browser on your laptop: a Google Slides deck with links between slides, a Google Site, a web page, or an app you build in Google AI Studio. The tool is your choice. What matters is that a visitor can see your claim proven on the screen.

The form

Task 1 · My claim

What is the claim at the top of your poster, and what line sits under it to name your project or certificate? (Start from your claim in My claim. Change it if your work has moved since then. Claim only what a visitor can see proven on your screen.)

Task 2 · The work card

What problem did you work on, and what does a visitor see when they open this card? Include:
  • the problem in plain words, and why it matters to the people it affects
  • the evidence: the work itself, such as the bug and the test that proves the fix, the investigation and what you observed, or the screen you built, with the part that matters pointed out
  • one honest sentence on what you are still not sure about
  • how you directed the AI you used: what it first gave you, what you asked next and why, and how you checked the result
(This is the card where the evidence from your Integrative Solving report usually belongs, because it shows the second domain at work.)

Task 3 · The obstacle card

What went wrong in your work, or what is still not working, and what did you do about it? Include:
  • the obstacle or open question, in plain words
  • what you changed, or what you are trying next
  • what a visitor can look at that shows the problem, and what changed if anything did
  • one sentence on what it cost you, if it cost you anything
(An obstacle that is not solved yet is fine on this card. Say so, and say what you are trying next.)

Task 4 · Where it stands, and what remains

Where does your work stand today, and what exactly still has to happen before Monday 7 December? (Write one honest sentence on what is finished and what is not. Then list each milestone that remains, each with a date.)

Task 5 · My poster so far

Where can a reviewer open the first version of your poster? (Share a link that opens without asking for access. If your tool cannot share a link, add screenshots of the first screen and of each card to your submission.)

Worked example

Example · A contribution to Kolibri, an open-source learning platform

(the first sample poster, written as answers)

My claim:
I can find the cause of a bug in a large codebase I didn't write, and prove my fix with a test. Under it: Capstone: contributing to Kolibri, an open-source learning platform.
The work card:
Content imports said "complete" even when files were missing, so a teacher with no internet would find lessons missing in front of the class. The evidence is my test, which copies 40 files and makes 3 of them fail: before my fix it failed and the import still said "complete", and after my fix it passed and named the 3 missing files. I am still not sure whether teachers should see the warning or only the person who runs the device, and I asked on the issue thread. Claude Code first gave me a fix for the one case in the bug report. I asked it to find every place that copies files the same way, and it named two more. I tried to reproduce the bug in both places: one really had it, and the other was already handled, so I left it alone.
The obstacle card:
11 tests failed on my laptop before I had changed anything. I moved to Linux so I could trust my test results. A visitor can see the same test run with 11 failures on Windows and none on Linux. It cost me a week of progress on the bug itself.
Where it stands, and what remains:
My pull request is open, and a maintainer asked for one change, which I made. Remaining: respond to the second review by Friday 20 November; pick and fix a second issue by Monday 30 November; add the second fix to my poster by Friday 4 December.
My poster so far:
a Google Slides link, set so anyone with the link can view it.
