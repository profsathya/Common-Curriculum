Learning outcomes

1. Identify one specific gap in the evidence in my portfolio, and demonstrate the importance of that gap by pointing to how often employers asked for it in the postings I read.

Two worked examplesBoth point at the counts in the postings.

Example 1 — the six new-grad software and data postings. Nothing I can show has been read or reviewed by anyone else. Every commit in my repo I wrote alone and merged myself. It matters because code review is part of the job from day one in three of the six postings — and not only the software ones: Airtable’s “Participate in code reviews and technical discussions”, Underdog’s “participating in code reviews and ensuring engineering best practices”, and, on the data side, Chewy’s “Participate in code reviews to understand design principles, version control, and testing best practices”.

Example 2 — the five cybersecurity postings. I have never reviewed anyone else’s code for security. Everything I have done has been on deliberately vulnerable practice targets, by myself. It matters because reviewing other people’s code is in three of the five postings, and in two of them it is a hiring qualification, not just a duty: both TikTok graduate roles require “writing and reviewing code in at least two” languages, and Modern Health’s role is to “Conduct secure code reviews on services and applications built with modern frameworks and technologies.”

Why this one keeps coming up. Reviewing code is the same skill whichever author produced it — a teammate or an AI: read it closely, work out what it assumes, ask the question that exposes the assumption, and suggest something better. It runs on critical thinking, and it happens in conversation. What an employer sees in a review thread is not the diff — it is how you thought about someone else’s work.

2. Write the one argument I could make to a hiring manager if I filled that gap — an argument my evidence would let me make, and my current work does not.

Two worked examplesBoth end on what most other applicants could not say.

Example 1. “Every commit in this project was reviewed by someone else before it merged, and I reviewed theirs. Here are three threads where I changed my approach because of what a reviewer said, and the principle I took from each one into later work.” A repository I wrote alone shows what I can build. The review threads show what I did when someone pushed back on my code — and that is the part I cannot show today.

Example 2. “Here are five pull requests in an open-source project where I raised a security concern, the question I asked, and what changed. Two of them were rejected, and here is why the maintainer was right.” A CTF score shows I can find a vulnerability. The pull requests show that I raised a concern about someone else’s code and handled the conversation that followed — and that is the part I cannot show today.

3 · What is missing, and why it matters

This is your The gap in your preparationWhat it means. The distance between what you can prove about your skills today and what the work you are aiming at asks for.Why it matters. It turns a vague sense of not being ready into one specific thing you can close. Vague gaps produce vague work.What a good one looks like. Specific, and closeable. “I have never shipped code another engineer reviewed” is a gap. “I need more experience” is not. — the overlap of all three circles. It has to satisfy three things at once: employers ask for it (Wednesday), it sits inside work that holds your attention (Friday), and your current work does not yet make it visible (box 1).

One sentence, and one — not five. “I have not yet shipped code another engineer reviewed” is specific enough to act on; “I need more experience” is not.

Open Friday’s pattern before you write this. A capability the market wants that has nothing to do with work you would do anyway is the wrong one to spend a semester on. If nothing overlaps all three, say which two do — that is a real finding.

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
  • Does it name a particular capability or experience, rather than a general level?
  • Does it connect to one of your two market findings?
  • Does it sit inside the pattern you found on Friday? If not, which two of the three circles does it cover?
  • Could a single project this semester produce evidence for it?

It is a picture of where this semester is going: the evidence you will have built, and what it lets you say. What in it would be unique to you, that others in the program could not say?

5 · Set your re-run date, and the loop

The routine that re-runs Wednesday’s read after this week, so your picture stays current. Fill in the brackets with your own space, counts, driver, signal and the evidence from box 2 — that is what makes it yours rather than a general market summary. Run it once a month, and before any stretch of applications.
Use this template

Re-run my market read. My space is [SPACE]. Last time, the two things employers were asking for were [A] and [B], found in [N] of [M] postings, on [DATE]. The change I expected was [CHANGE], driven by [DRIVER], and the signal I said I would watch is [SIGNAL].

Do three things. First, pull 10 currently open postings in my space and tell me whether [A] and [B] still repeat, with the new count. Second, tell me what is repeating now that was not there last time. Third, check my signal: has [SIGNAL] moved, and what is your evidence? Quote the postings and link them, and tell me where you are inferring rather than reading.

Finish with one line: given the evidence I am building — [GAP] — has the most useful next thing for me to build changed?

Without AI, the same routine on a calendar. A recurring 30 minutes, once a month, with the three questions written down: do those phrases still repeat in ten fresh postings, what is repeating now that was not before, and has my signal moved? Write the date and the answers underneath the last set, so the drift is visible.

Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI

  • Is there a date on it, so the drift is visible next time?

Optional

The Know Yourself practice DojoFor when box 1 is done and box 2 will not come down to one sentence.

What it does. It starts from one occasion you were absorbed in, then names a strength your own account shows that you did not claim. From there it widens, looks for the unusual combination of two things you can do rather than any single skill, and helps you say what that is worth from the employer’s side — the problem you solve for them. It ends by setting that against a real niche and finding what is not yet covered, which is what box 2 is asking for.

The employer-side sentence it helps you write is close to the middle of next week’s introduction. Keep it.

What it will not do: market questions. It will send you back to Wednesday’s work, which you have already done.
