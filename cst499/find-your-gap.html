Find Your Gap

Due Tuesday 8 September, 11:59 p.m. · about 40 minutes

Where this is going

In two to three months, Prof. Sathya may send an email about you to one of his industry contacts. [slot — Sathya’s exact wording on the promise goes here] He has the final say, and he is open to doing this for as many of you as he can. “May” means there is a bar, and meeting it is yours to do.

Next week you draft that email yourself. Everything you do this week is the raw material for it: what the market is asking for gives the email something specific to point at, what you would be doing anyway gives it a story worth reading, and the gap you find gives it the honest line about what you are building next.

Would Prof. Sathya actually send this? Would his contact actually be spurred to action by it?

The second question is the harder one, and it is the reason this week starts with job postings. A busy person acts when they can see a specific opportunity and a specific reason you fit it. Neither is something you can write without having read the market first.

It also has to be short enough to read in one go and sharp enough to hold attention from the first line — under 150 words [slot — length is Sathya’s call]. That is a high bar, and holding yourself to it is the point.

This page feeds the honest line — what you are building next, and why that one. It is what stops the email reading as a brag.

Purpose

Find the one thing worth closing next — the specific piece the market keeps asking for that you cannot yet prove — and set up the routine that keeps finding it after this week. Knowing your own gap is what turns a general worry about being ready into one thing you can go and do.

You come out of this with an honest audit of what you can prove today, one specific gap, and a routine you can re-run in thirty minutes a month.

What you are building on

From Wednesday: your market read. Two things employers are asking for, with the counts behind them, and one expected change with its driver and its signal.

From Friday: your moments. The times work stopped feeling like work, and what runs through them.

Have both open. This activity is mostly about putting them next to each other and being honest about the third thing.

What to work out

Four boxes. The first is the honest audit, the second is the gap it exposes, the third is what keeps this current after the week ends, and the fourth is what you do next.

One framing before you start, because it changes what a good answer looks like. Everything here is a photograph of one week. Postings change, the signal you named on Wednesday either moves or it does not, and what you can prove grows. So the gap is not a verdict — it is the thing you are about to go and close, which is exactly what a contact wants to hear someone say.

Two ways to work on what you write

Use one, or both. Under every box there is a Get AI feedback button that reads what you actually wrote against what a strong answer to that question looks like, and a Check it yourself list with the same questions, for you to run on your own. They go at the same thing from two directions and they end in the same box.

Working through the questions yourself is where a lot of the thinking happens, and it is worth doing for its own sake. Having something push back on what you just wrote is a different skill, and that is worth practising too. If you want both, answer first, run the check yourself, then press the button and see what it caught that you did not.

Your writing is saved in this browser as you type, so you can close the page and come back. Nothing is sent anywhere until you press the button or copy your summary.

Take the two things employers are asking for from Wednesday, one at a time, and for each write what you could put in front of someone as evidence. Not what you have covered in a class — what you could show. A repository someone else has read, a thing you built that someone used, a piece of work with your decisions visible in it. Where you have nothing to show, write nothing. That blank is the useful part of this step, and it is not a verdict on you: almost every senior has several.

One sentence: something the market keeps asking for, inside work you would do anyway, that you cannot prove yet. One, not five. A common wrong turn is a gap so general it could belong to anyone in the room — if your sentence would fit your neighbour, it is not yours yet. “I have never shipped code another engineer reviewed” is a gap. “I need more experience” is not.
Check it yourself — the same questions, without the AI
  • Would this sentence fit your neighbour? Then it is not yours yet.
  • Is it closeable, or is it the size of a degree?
  • Is it the one you would have written on Monday without doing any of this?
  • Read it as the honest line in an email about you. Does it make you more credible, or just smaller?

The routine that keeps this current

This is the part that outlives the week. Take one of these two, fill it in, and put the result in box 3.

Re-run my market read. My space is [SPACE]. Last time, the two things employers were asking for were [A] and [B], found in [N] of [M] postings, on [DATE]. The change I expected was [CHANGE], driven by [DRIVER], and the signal I said I would watch is [SIGNAL].

Do three things. First, pull 10 currently open postings in my space and tell me whether [A] and [B] still repeat, with the new count. Second, tell me what is repeating now that was not there last time. Third, check my signal: has [SIGNAL] moved, and what is your evidence? Quote the postings and link them, and tell me where you are guessing.

Finish with one line: given my gap — [GAP] — has the most useful next thing for me to build changed?

Filled in, it reads like this, and yours will look nothing like it: “My space is backend work on data pipelines at climate-tech companies. Last time the asks were ‘own a service end to end’ and ‘write and maintain tests’, in 7 of 12 postings, on 8 September. The change I expected was more postings asking for evaluation of AI-generated code, driven by teams shipping more of it than they can review, and the signal I said I would watch is whether review and testing language starts appearing in junior postings.”

Without AI, the same routine, on a calendar. A recurring 30 minutes, once a month. The three questions written down so you do not have to remember them: do those phrases still repeat in ten fresh postings, what is repeating now that was not there before, and has my signal moved? Write the date and the answers underneath the last set, so you can see the drift.

If you get stuck, or want to go further

The Know Yourself practice DojoFor when you have the audit but cannot get the gap down to one sentence.

The condition. Box 1 is done and box 2 will not come. You keep writing sentences that would fit anyone. That is what this is for.

What it does. It starts from one real moment you were absorbed in, then plays the outside mirror — it points at a strength in your own story that you did not claim. From there it widens, looks for the unusual combination of two things you can do rather than any single skill, and then makes you say what that is worth from the employer’s side: not what you are good at, but what problem you solve for them. It ends by testing that against a real niche and finding what is missing. That last part is your gap.

The employer-side sentence it helps you write is, near enough, the middle of next week’s email. Keep it.

What it will not do. Market questions. If you ask it where the jobs are, it will send you away — correctly, because you already did that on Wednesday.

Open https://dojo.symbioticthinking.ai/?topic=know-yourself — if it asks for a key, use the link from the week 1 email, subject Symbiotic Thinking Dojo API Key.

What you do with what it gives you. It ends by handing you a summary block. That is raw material, not your submission — paste the parts you agree with into the boxes above, in your own words. What goes to Canvas is the boxes and the button at the bottom of this page.

Your own AI DojoFor the audit, and for drafting the standing routine.

The condition. You want something holding all three lists at once and asking what you are avoiding. Paste in your market read and your moments, then give it this:

Above are two things: what I found employers in my space are asking for, and the moments when work stopped feeling like work for me. Now interview me about the third thing — what I can actually prove today. Go one thing at a time. For each thing employers are asking for, ask me what I could put in front of someone as evidence, and push back when I answer with a course I took instead of something I could show.

When we are done, tell me where the three do not line up, and ask me which single gap I think matters most. Argue with my choice if you think I picked the comfortable one. Do not pick it for me.

You decide, it argues. A gap it chose for you is not one you will spend a semester on. Its job is to make the comfortable answer harder to keep; yours is to be the one who ends up saying the sentence. If you have it draft your standing routine, fill in the space, the signal and the gap yourself — those are the parts that make the routine yours.

What done and good look like

Not graded — and this is the one to spend the time on anyway, because this gap is the thing your project proposal has to argue it closes, so the clearer it is now, the less of the semester you spend deciding.

Done is an honest audit with its blanks left visible, one specific gap, a routine you could actually run, and your next move.

Good is a gap that surprised you a little. If it is the one you would have written on Monday without doing any of this, either you were already right or you have written the comfortable one — and it is worth twenty minutes to work out which.

Before you finish

Three questions, worth answering after the work.

Which row were you most tempted to be generous with yourself about?

What did the three circles disagree about — and what did you decide to do about the disagreement?

If Prof. Sathya put your gap sentence in an email to someone who hires for this work, would it make them trust the rest of the email, or worry? What would have to change for it to be the first one?
