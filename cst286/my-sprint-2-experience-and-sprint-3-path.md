# My Sprint 2 experience, and the path I choose for Sprint 3

Graded item · Due Date: Friday 30 October · 100 pts

This graded item will affect your final grade, and the late penalty applies.

## Purpose

Reflect on how your learning went during Sprint 2 - when you worked on each piece, where your hours went, what you had to look up - and use that record to choose the approach that fits your own needs as a self-directed learner in Sprint 3.

## What Sprint 3 asks of you

In Sprint 3 we do another deliberate round of self-directed learning:

- identify a new phenomenon you want to understand,
- find a quantitative relationship that lets you make predictions about it,
- apply it in three real situations, and
- find two similar-looking situations where it cannot be applied.

### Step 1 · Tell the story of your learning in Sprint 2

Describe your learning journey in Sprint 2 as a story. Choose three or four of these questions to build it around; you do not have to answer all of them. What motivated you to learn this particular topic? How did you go about it? Did you use AI, and how? When did you work on it, and for how much time? What surprised you? Who or what helped?

   **My Sprint 2 story.** (Page note: the box opens with this sample in grey text, which disappears when the student starts typing.)

   > I chose how a bike stays upright because I ride every day and had never thought about it. I started on the Tuesday after the goal form was due, mostly late evenings, maybe six hours in all. I asked my Dojo for a place to start and it pointed me at angular momentum; the OpenStax chapter got me the formula. What surprised me was that the relationship was simple but choosing realistic numbers for the scenarios took longer than the physics. My guide group helped by asking why a stationary bike falls over, which became my first look-alike scenario.

   > My topic was why a phone's screen rotates. I worked on it in two sittings, both the night before each due date, about three hours each. My Dojo helped by asking what single detail I could change in Scenario 1.

## Hover text (a bubble on the first use of each word; not a section on the page)

**GlowScript** (first use in Path C)
- *What it is.* A free website (glowscript.org) where you write short Python programs that make 3D objects move on screen according to the physics you give them.
- *Why it fits this path.* You decide the forces and the starting conditions, so when the motion on screen matches or fails to match what you expected, the relationship you are after is sitting in your own code.
- *Where to start.* glowscript.org, Sign in, then the first example on the Help page runs a bouncing ball you can change.

**Nand2Tetris** (first use in Path C)
- *What it is.* A free course and simulator (nand2tetris.org) in which you build a working computer from a single kind of logic gate upward, one layer at a time.
- *Why it fits this path.* Each layer works only because the one below it obeys an exact rule, so the quantitative relationships of computing circuits (timing, voltage levels, gate counts) show up as you test what you built.
- *Where to start.* nand2tetris.org, Project 1, which needs only the free simulator download.
