CST286 · Fall 2026 · Sprint 3

# Sprint 3 Exam · What to prepare

Wednesday 2 December · In class · 200 points · Closed-book written exam and partner conversation

The exam has two halves of 100 points each. First you write your answers to two questions on paper, without notes. Then you and a partner read each other's answers and ask each other questions about them. [for Sathya's read]

## Purpose

**Builds on your earlier learning.** The relationship you found and confirmed, your five real-world scenarios, and what you changed about how you learn during Sprint 3. [for Sathya's read]

**This Learning Experience.** Show that you can use what you learned in a new situation, tell where it stops applying, and explain how you changed your approach to learning. [for Sathya's read]

**Prepare you for.** After the exam, [My Sprint 3 experience](my-sprint-3-experience.html) asks you to decide what you can now use and what you want to practice further.

## What the written half asks

**Question 1 is about your relationship (60 points).**

- Explain the quantitative relationship you learned in Sprint 3, and why it is helpful to know it.
- Apply it to one scenario to make a prediction, and explain each step.
- Present two scenarios that look similar but where the relationship does not apply, and explain why in each case.

**Question 2 is about you as a self-directed learner (40 points).** It asks about one moment in Sprint 3 when something in your work made you notice that you needed to change your approach to learning:

- what the moment was
- what change you made, and how you expected it to help your learning
- what the moment shows you about yourself as a learner, and what you will do differently in the future

## How to prepare [for Sathya's read]

- Practice question 1 on paper without your notes: write your relationship with its quantities and units, work one prediction step by step, and write your two look-alike scenarios.
- Check your practice answer against the criteria printed on [Find and confirm the relevant quantitative relationship](find-and-confirm-my-relationship.html) and [Five real-world scenarios](five-real-world-scenarios-sprint-3.html): the words and the formula agree, the units are consistent, your assumptions justify using the relationship, and your conclusion stays within your evidence.
- Choose the moment you will write about in question 2. The Look forward section at the end of [Five real-world scenarios](five-real-world-scenarios-sprint-3.html) has two examples.

## The partner half [for Sathya's read]

After you write, you and a partner exchange papers. You type your partner's answers, ask at least two follow-up questions about what they wrote, and write a summary of what you learned from the conversation. A follow-up question teaches you more when it points at one specific thing in your partner's answer.

## After the exam

- Submit [My Sprint 3 experience](my-sprint-3-experience.html) by Monday 7 December.
- Review one classmate's relationship and scenarios in [Review a classmate's Sprint 3 work](grade-my-two-partners-sprint-3.html) by Wednesday 9 December.

## For your AI Dojo (hidden on the page)

The Sprint 3 Exam is on Wednesday 2 December, in class: a closed-book handwritten half (100 points) and a partner conversation half (100 points). Written question 1 (60 points): explain the quantitative relationship learned in Sprint 3 and why it is helpful to know it; apply it to one scenario to make a prediction, explaining each step; present two scenarios that look similar but where the relationship does not apply, and explain why. Written question 2 (40 points) is about the student as a self-directed learner: one moment in Sprint 3 when something in their work made them notice that they needed to change their approach to learning; what change they made and how they expected it to help; what the moment shows them about themselves as a learner and what they will do differently in the future. Help the student prepare by asking them to explain their own relationship and assumptions, then offer one unfamiliar situation without supplying the answer and ask whether the relationship applies and why. For question 2, ask what was happening in their work at the moment they chose, and follow their answers with questions; a general statement such as "I need to manage my time" needs the event behind it. Do not write exam answers for the student to memorize, do not supply a moment for them, and do not claim to know more about the exam than this page says. In the partner half the student types a partner's answers, asks at least two follow-up questions that point at specific parts of those answers, and writes a summary of what they learned.
