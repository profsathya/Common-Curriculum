Sprint 3 Exam - the paired half

Week 15 · Wednesday 2 December, in class · 100 points · Sprint 3 Exam

Purpose

Learn how to ask questions to understand another person's work and help them deepen their understanding, and write down what you learned.

Tasks

You have your partner's paper. Complete the tasks below in order. When you finish, press Copy my answers and paste the text into the Canvas box.

  1. 1.Select your partner's name.

    Partner with someone new today: your partner must be someone who was not your partner in the Sprint 1 Exam or the Sprint 2 Exam.

    If you work with a partner you had in the Sprint 1 Exam or the Sprint 2 Exam, you will not receive credit for this activity.

    My partner has read all six boxes against their paper and confirms they say what they wrote.

  3. 3.Ask your partner at least two follow-up questions about what they wrote. Write down each question you asked and what your partner said.

    A follow-up question that teaches you something points at one specific thing in their answer - a number, a step in the working, the condition they named, a scenario they chose, the moment they described, the change they made, a word they used. A general question could be asked of any paper in the room, so the answer tells you nothing about this paper.

    • General: "Was your topic hard to learn?"
    • Specific: "In 1b you divided by the mass at the third step. What would your prediction have been if the mass were twice as large?"
    • General: "Was it hard to change how you learn?"
    • Specific: "In 2b you said you expected the change to leave you time to ask a question. What happened the first time you tried it?"

    Come up with your questions in either of these two ways, or both.

    Write your own questions
    1. Read your partner's six answers again.
    2. Write down every question that comes to you. Do not stop to judge or answer them. If you write a statement, change it into a question. Truly care about the questions.
    3. For each question you wrote, find the exact words or phrases in their answer that it points at. Cross out any question you cannot point at.
    4. Pick the two questions you are most curious about. Ask those two.
    Use the AI Question Suggestions button

    Press the button. It reads the six answers you typed and suggests five questions, each one naming the part of the answer it comes from. Pick two and adapt to ask your partner - again your curiosity is your guide. Change the wording to make it your own.

    Type all six answers and have your partner tick Checked by the writer, then the suggestions open.
  4. 4.Write a summary of what you understand about their work. Include what they could explain when you asked and what they were less sure about. Identify at least one thing you learned from talking to them that was not on the written response.

What makes it strong. Your two questions explore the part of the paper that is interesting or relevant - the step in 1b where the number comes out, the condition in 1c that the look-alike scenario breaks, or the moment in 2a - and your summary is traceable to what your partner said when you asked, rather than repeating the paper back.

Grading. 100 points, the paired half of the Sprint 3 Exam. You will be asked to explain this to a peer, the TA and/or the instructor, so make sure you are comfortable with what you have written here.
