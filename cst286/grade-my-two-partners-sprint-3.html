Practice applying two criteria

Before reviewing your classmate, score this short sample on your own. Use two criteria from Find and confirm my relationship: formula, quantities and units (15 points), and physically justified assumptions (20 points). This practice is included in your review record and adds no points.

A student predicts the period of a 1.0 m pendulum released from 60°: “T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(1.0 m / 9.8 m/s²) ≈ 2.01 s, where T is period in seconds, L is length in meters, and g is gravitational acceleration in m/s². My assumption is that the bob is heavy, so the small-angle formula applies.”

Enter both scores and your reasons before comparing.

For the formula-and-units criterion (15 points), 15/15 is defensible here: the formula and quantity units are given correctly and the substitution is consistent.

For the assumptions criterion (20 points), full credit is not justified. A heavy bob does not establish a small angle, and 60° is outside the small-angle approximation. A suggested score is 0/20 because no relevant applicability assumption is justified; partial credit would require some relevant assumption supported by the setup.

The arithmetic can be correct while the physical prediction is not justified. Explain any different score using the criterion and evidence; discuss remaining differences with Prof. Sathya or the TA.

Task · Review one classmate

Ask one classmate to grade your submitted Find and confirm my relationship and Five real-world scenarios work, and record their agreement in My Sprint 3 goal and plan. Agree to grade one other classmate, so each student receives one review.

Follow both conditions when arranging grading:

  • No reciprocal grading: the person you grade must be different from the person grading you.
  • A new grading partner: neither your grader nor the classmate you grade may be someone you partnered with in the Sprint 1 or Sprint 2 exam.

Send your submitted work to your grader, and ask the classmate you are grading to send you theirs. If you cannot arrange an eligible grader or classmate to review, contact Prof. Sathya or the TA.

Read the classmate's work and record your own judgment. Use the criteria printed on the original assignments; each assignment has its own recommended score out of 100.
