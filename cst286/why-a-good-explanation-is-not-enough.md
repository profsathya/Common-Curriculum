# Why a good explanation is not enough

Own your progress · Recommended Date: Wednesday 7 October · Veritasium, 8 min

Take ownership of your progress — this activity is provided to help you, but the grade for this activity will not affect your final grade.

## Purpose

Explore how questioning an explanation can help you check your understanding, then consider how to use that approach in your Commons conversations.

## Watch

Watch before your first online chat on The Commons: Derek Muller (Veritasium), [Khan Academy and the Effectiveness of Science Videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8), about 8 minutes.

Muller describes a study comparing what students understood before and after watching different physics explanations.

## Tasks

Write your answer to each task in the box under it. The links point to approximate starting places in the video.

1. Compare the scores Muller reports before and after students watched the clear explanation: 6 and 6.3 out of 26. What do these results suggest about their learning in this study? [Watch this part, ~1:10–2:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8&t=70s)

   **Your answer to task 1.** What the comparison suggests about learning in this study.

2. Explain how students interpreted the video through their existing ideas, using an example Muller describes. How could this help explain why they became more confident even when their answers were incorrect? [Watch this part, ~3:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8&t=200s)

   **Your answer to task 2.** How students interpreted the explanation and how that affected their confidence, with an example.

3. Compare the two videos. What did the second video ask students to reconsider, and how did that differ from the clear explanation? Use details from the video to explain what might account for the difference in scores. [Watch this part, ~4:40–5:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVtCO84MDj8&t=280s)

   **Your answer to task 3.** What students were asked to reconsider and how that might have affected their learning.

4. Describe one way a classmate's question could help you check your understanding of the physics behind your Sprint 2 goal. What would you do to work out your answer?

   **Your answer to task 4.** How a classmate's question could help you check your understanding, and how you would work out an answer.
