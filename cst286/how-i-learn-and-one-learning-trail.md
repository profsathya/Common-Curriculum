# How I learn, and one learning trail to explore

Graded item · Due Date: Monday 12 October · 100 pts

This graded item will affect your final grade, and the late penalty applies.

## Purpose

Reflect on how you learned in Sprint 1 so that you can improve your learning skill. Then explore one physics topic deeply on a learning trail, and apply at least one lesson from your reflection as you work.

## Part 1 · Look back at how you learned in Sprint 1

Write your answer to each task in the box under it. If you submitted the activity *What misconceptions about learning we all have*, paste your answers to tasks 1 to 5 here and revise them.

1. Describe what habits helped you make progress toward your physics learning goal and what, if anything, made progress difficult. Use specific moments from Sprint 1 to explain what happened and what you learned from it.

   **Your answer to task 1.** What habits affected your progress, with specific moments from the sprint.

2. Reflect on your interest: how much you cared about learning the topic, and how that interest helped your learning. Give a specific moment as an example, if possible.

   **Your answer to task 2.** The interest in your goal and how it influenced your approach.

3. Describe how you and your partner impacted each others' learning experience. If you had little or no opportunity to work together, describe how you can improve on that going forward.

   **Your answer to task 3.** Your experience working with partners, any challenges in working together, and how you can improve going forward.

4. Describe how you used the AI Dojo, if you used it. Include what you asked it to do, in what ways it helped your learning, and how you confirmed that. If you did not use AI, share your reasons.

   **Your answer to task 4.** Your AI Dojo use, in what ways it helped your learning and how you confirmed that, or your reasons for not using AI.

5. Since you had a lot of freedom to use your time in this course, describe how you balanced your work in this course with other interests and responsibilities, and how your choices affected your progress. Based on your reflections, identify how you will do better in sprint 2.

   **Your answer to task 5.** Your choices and their effects, and what you will do going forward.

6. Name one belief about learning that shaped how you worked in Sprint 1 and that you now want to refine. If there is a specific moment when you noticed this, please share.

   **Your answer to task 6.** The belief, and the moment that made you question it.

7. Identify one lesson from tasks 1 to 6 that you will apply while you work through the learning trail in Part 2. Write it as something you will do.

   **Your answer to task 7.** The one lesson, written as an action.

## Part 2 · Explore one topic deeply on the learning trail

Open the learning trail [How does your phone know it moved?](trail-accelerometer.html) in a second tab. It has six stops. At each stop, watch the video, work through the example on the trail page, then come back here and answer that stop's task. Show the numbers with their units in every calculation.

**Stop 1 · Units**

a. A car slows down by 15 miles per hour every second. Convert this to meters per second squared, using 1 mi = 1609 m and 1 h = 3600 s. Write out the chain of conversion factors.
b. Explain how the units told you that each conversion factor was the right way up.

   **Your answer to stop 1.** The chain of factors with the result, and how the units confirmed it.

**Stop 2 · Direction**

a. You walk 30 m east, then turn around and walk 10 m west. The whole walk takes 20 s. Calculate your distance, your displacement, your speed and your velocity.
b. Explain why your speed and your velocity came out as different numbers.

   **Your answer to stop 2.** The four quantities with units and direction, and the reason speed and velocity differ.

**Stop 3 · Newton's first law**

a. Your phone lies on a smooth car seat, and the driver brakes hard. Predict where the phone ends up on the seat, and use the first law to explain why.
b. Explain how the small mass inside the phone's sensor behaves in the same way when the phone itself slows down.

   **Your answer to stop 3.** Your prediction with the first-law reason, and the connection to the sensor.

**Stop 4 · Newton's second law**

a. A phone has a mass of 150 g. You push it along a smooth table with a force of 0.6 N. Predict the acceleration its sensor reports.
b. The same push acts on a tablet with a mass of 600 g. Before you calculate, say whether the acceleration is larger or smaller and why. Then calculate it.

   **Your answer to stop 4.** Both accelerations with units, and your reasoning before the second calculation.

**Stop 5 · Inside the sensor**

a. A large model of the sensor has a mass of 0.02 kg on a spring with a spring constant of 50 N/m. The model is accelerated at 5 m/s². Predict how far the mass shifts.
b. Explain what the sensor reads when the phone lies flat on a table and what it reads when the phone is in free fall, and why the two readings differ.

   **Your answer to stop 5.** The shift with units, and the two readings with the reason for each.

**Stop 6 · Explore from the phone**

a. Choose one of the five problems at stop 6 of the learning trail, or write your own in the same form. Write it as a goal: Given ___, I can predict ___. This is useful because ___.
b. Work on the problem as far as you can. Show your prediction with numbers and units.
c. Identify one part of the problem that you cannot explain yet.

   **Your answer to stop 6.** The goal, your work on it, and the part you cannot explain yet.

**Apply your lesson**

Describe one moment on the learning trail when you applied the lesson you wrote in task 7. Explain what you did differently because of it.

   **Your answer.** The moment, and what you did differently.
