What Are My Priorities

Due Monday 31 August, 11:59 p.m. · about 30 minutes
A guided conversation about what you actually care about. It is the first thing you do in this course, because in week 3 you pick something to spend the semester digging into — and that choice goes much better if you have already worked out what matters to you.

Purpose

In week 3 you will choose an interest and spend the rest of the semester getting underneath it. Not a topic from a list someone handed you — something you actually care about. Cars, music, cooking, climbing, video games, your phone, the ocean, a sport, a machine you have always wondered about.

That works because of something that is easy to say and hard to believe until you see it: whatever you care about has physics underneath it. The part of it that seems like magic is a piece of the physical world behaving in a way someone has worked out how to describe. This course is you finding that out about your own thing.

But you cannot pick well in week 3 if you have never said out loud what you care about. Most of us have not. We have said what we are supposed to care about, or what sounded good when someone asked. So that is what this half hour is for: before anything else in this course, get honest about what you actually want.

What this is — and how it is different from your own Dojo

This runs in a practice Dojo, which is a different thing from the AI Dojo you set up on the Set Up Your AI Dojo page. You do not have to configure anything. You click a link, and a conversation that has already been prepared for this one question starts asking you things.

It behaves the way this whole course behaves: it asks, you think, you answer. It will not hand you a list of priorities. If you ask it to just tell you what your priorities should be, it will hand the question back to you. That is not it being difficult — that is the point of it.

Step 1 — Open the Dojo

The link arrives before week 1. It will look like https://dojo.symbioticthinking.ai/?topic=what-are-my-priorities&key=… — the class key goes out with the week 1 announcement and this page will carry the full clickable link then. Nothing for you to do until it shows up.

Open it on a laptop if you can. You will be typing for half an hour and a phone makes that harder than it needs to be.

Step 2 — Have the conversation

Give it about thirty minutes, in one go. This is not a form to fill in between other things. The answers worth having usually show up after the first ten minutes, once the obvious ones are out of the way.

Be honest, including about the unimpressive parts. If the true answer is “I picked this major because it pays well,” say that. If it is “I am not sure I want to be here,” say that. There is no version of this where you lose points for having ordinary human reasons. You will lose the value of the exercise by writing what you think a professor wants to read.

Say so when it gets you wrong. If it sums you up and the summary is off, tell it. A conversation where you corrected it twice is worth more than one where you agreed with everything it said.

Do not stop at your first answer. When it asks why something matters to you, the first answer is usually the surface one. The second and third are usually the real ones. Sit with the question for a moment instead of moving on.

Before you finish, ask it to play back the priorities it heard from you, and tell it whether it got them right.

Step 3 — Submit

One text box in Canvas, two parts.

1. Your priorities, in your own words. Three to five of them, one line each, in the order that matters to you. Then two or three sentences about what surprised you — something you said that you had never put into words before, or a priority that turned out to matter less than you assumed.

2. The whole conversation, pasted underneath. All of it, unedited.

You do not have to copy it out by hand. The Dojo has a download / copy-to-clipboard option built in — use that when you are finished, then paste it straight into the Canvas box.

How this is graded

This is a graded item worth 100 points. Like everything you submit in this course, it can come up in a check-in — so expect to be asked about your priorities out loud, not just on paper.

What earns the points is evidence that you did the thinking: priorities specific enough that they would actually rule something out, a conversation that shows you working rather than agreeing, and at least one place where your answer changed as you talked.

What does not earn the points is a tidy list of admirable-sounding goals with a conversation underneath showing you accepted the first thing the Dojo offered.

There is no right set of priorities. There is only whether these are actually yours.
