CST286 · Physics of Computing (with AI) · Fall 2026

Get Truly Good at Something You Care About

Pick something you already care about. Spend a semester finding out how it actually works underneath — and get better at it because of what you find.
Think about something you already do: riding, cooking, playing, building, fixing. You have a feel for it. Most of that feel came from repetition, so you know what works without knowing why it works. Now think about what changes when the why arrives. A cyclist who finds out why a softer tyre rolls faster over rough ground stops guessing at tyre pressure and starts choosing it. That move — from a feel for something to an understanding of it — is what this course is for.
Underneath the thing you care about, there is PhysicsWhat it is. The precise description of how the physical world behaves, written in mathematics.Why that is powerful. A handful of exact relationships let you predict something you have never seen. That is what every technology is built on top of — engines, radio, semiconductors, and the machines running AI today.What you do with it here. You use it as the way into whatever you chose. A handful of exact relationships is enough to predict something nobody has ever seen, and that is what every piece of technology we have is built on top of — engines, radio, the semiconductors in your laptop, and the machines running AI today. This semester you get to use that on something of your own choosing.

Beyond the grade
Depth is the practice.

Courses end. What you keep is a way of working that professionals use on anything unfamiliar: choose something worth understanding, find out how it really works, let that change what you do, and then do it again on the next thing. By December you will have run it twice.

When we meet. Mondays are online and belong to check-ins — small groups or one-on-one, rolling through the roster all semester. Wednesdays are in person: the sessions that open each sprint, the three Sprint Exams, and the check-ins that are better in a room.

Start Here · How this course works Read before week 1 ▸
Every week
One assignment and one reflection
Each week carries one piece of work and a few honest lines on how it went and how you feel about it. The work on its own can be produced without you growing; the feelings on their own can be performed. Together, and agreeing with each other, they are hard to fake — and they are what your interviews draw on. Late work loses 10% a day, which is deliberate: we would rather catch you in week 4 than in week 12.
How the grade works
Half for doing it, half for explaining it
Half your grade comes from submitting each week’s work on time. The other half is earned live, in interviews where we pick something you already submitted and ask you to walk us through it. You will not know in advance which piece we pick, so prepare all of it.
Measured from your own starting line
Depth is judged against the baseline you write in week 4, so you are never compared to a classmate who arrived with three physics courses behind them. There is no fixed bar everyone has to clear.
Recovering from a rough interview
There are no mid-course retakes. A rough conversation is recovered by showing up better prepared at the next one. If the semester gets away from you, there is one catch-up at the end — talk to us before then, not after.
The conversations
Interviews
Four rounds, one per sprint. The first round is a small group and every student is seen. After that, how your rounds are packaged depends on how the work is going — some people meet us fewer times, with more covered each time. Every sprint’s work is checked for everyone.
Sprint Exams
The last Wednesday of Sprints 1, 2 and 3 — weeks 6, 10 and 14. One page, hand-written, with the questions published ahead of time. Then you swap with a partner, type their answers into a short AI form, talk it through with them, and submit your evaluation of their work.
