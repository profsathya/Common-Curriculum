CST286 · Physics of Computing (with AI) · Fall 2026

Get Good at Something You Care About

Let’s start with something you are already interested in. Use this class to learn the physics underneath it, and find out what changes when you understand why it works.
Have you wondered how something works the way it does?
Think about something you already do — riding, cooking, building, playing, fixing. You have a feel for it, mostly from repetition: you know what works without knowing why. The cyclist who works out the aerodynamics of riding, or the guitarist who works out what the strings are actually doing, can take their interest further than feel alone allows. They also get better at working out how other things behave — and that part stays with you.
Why physics?
Underneath the thing you care about, there is PhysicsWhat it is. The precise description of how the physical world behaves, written in mathematics.Why that is powerful. A handful of exact relationships let you predict something you have never seen. That is what every technology is built on top of — engines, radio, semiconductors, and the machines running AI today.What you do with it here. You use it as the way into whatever you chose. There is no list of physics topics to cover.: the precise description of how the physical world behaves, written in mathematics. A few exact relationships are enough to predict something nobody has ever seen — which is what every technology we have is built on, including the machines running AI. This semester you use that on something of your own choosing.

How this course was made

The design of this course is mine — what you’ll practice, what you’ll practice it on, how you’ll be guided, and how you’ll be graded, the focus on human connection through conversations — and I own the design of your learning experience. AI does the building: turning those decisions into pages, assignments, and your coach — that I review and test. That’s what lets me be more nimble and adapt to improve the course so I can serve all of you well.

What you are graduating into has changed, and that kind of change asks for innovation — trying approaches that fit the new situation better, building them as well as we can, and learning from what happens. This course is one of those attempts. I am inviting you to take it in that spirit: try the approach, tell me what is helping and what is getting in your way, and I will adjust while the semester is still running.

What that looks like — what I decided, what AI built, and what it gets you

What I decided

  • Self-directed learning is the real subject of this course. Physics is what you practice it on. Going after something hard that nobody assigned you is the capability that keeps its value, and it is not a talent — it is a practice, so this course is built to give you reps at it.
  • You go after topics you actually care about. Curiosity is the fuel for that practice; a topic handed to you doesn’t generate any.
  • You use simulation tools to go deeper than reading allows. Run a system, change it, watch it break — that is where a mechanism stops being words and becomes something you can see.
  • AI here is a sparring partner, not an answer key. Your coach is set up to push back on your explanations and test whether they hold, because being questioned is how you find out whether you understand something or only recognize it.
  • Conversations and interviews are the graded work. Explaining your thinking out loud — to me, and to a classmate — is how thinking gets exercised, and it is how I see what you know rather than what you can produce.
  • How much you choose and how much I scaffold is a deliberate balance. Too much structure and you never practice choosing; too little and you stall. I set where it starts, and I move it as the semester goes.
  • What every assignment is for, the guidance around it, and how it is graded are mine.

What AI built, and how we check it

  • These pages. Drafted from my design, with the parts that make them usable — the week-by-week plan you can read end to end, the terms you can hover over for a definition.
  • The Canvas side. Assignments, modules and due dates set up and held together, so nothing falls between a page and the gradebook.
  • Your AI coach, generated from this same course design — which is why it knows what you are working on, and can meet you at eleven at night when I can’t.
  • How we check it. My TAs and I click through the pages, submit the Canvas items ourselves before you ever see them, and run the coach through a student conversation to see how it answers. Where it comes out wrong, I edit it by hand.

What that combination gets you

  • A course that can change while you are in it. Because a decision becomes a built page quickly now, something that isn’t working in week 4 can be different in week 5 rather than next fall.
  • That speed is spent on you. It goes into adapting the course so it works for everyone in the room — not only for the students it already happened to fit.
  • And it is the thing you are here to learn. Directing AI to build something you designed, then checking it hard enough to stand behind it, is the same skill this course asks of you. This page is what it looks like when I do it.
