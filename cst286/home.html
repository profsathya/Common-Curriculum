CST286 · Physics of Computing (with AI) · Fall 2026

Get Truly Good at Something You Care About

Pick something you already care about. Spend a semester finding out how it actually works underneath — and get better at it because of what you find.
Have you wondered how something works the way it does?
Think about something you already do — riding, cooking, building, fixing. You have a feel for it, mostly from repetition, so you know what works without knowing why. A cyclist who finds out why a softer tyre rolls faster over rough ground stops guessing at tyre pressure and starts choosing it.
Why physics?
Underneath the thing you care about, there is PhysicsWhat it is. The precise description of how the physical world behaves, written in mathematics.Why that is powerful. A handful of exact relationships let you predict something you have never seen. That is what every technology is built on top of — engines, radio, semiconductors, and the machines running AI today.What you do with it here. You use it as the way into whatever you chose. There is no list of physics topics to cover.: the precise description of how the physical world behaves, written in mathematics. A few exact relationships are enough to predict something nobody has ever seen — which is what every technology we have is built on, including the machines running AI. This semester you use that on something of your own choosing.

Beyond the grade
What you leave with
  1. A Deep diveWhat it means. Going under your pursuit far enough to reach the mechanism — how the thing actually works, rather than a summary of it.How far is far enough. Measured from where you started, written down in your own words in week 4. There is no fixed bar everyone has to clear.How you will know. You can walk someone through it and keep going when they ask “why?” and “what would happen if…?” you can walk anyone through. Not a summary you read somewhere: the actual how-it-works, in your own words, holding up while someone asks why.
  2. Something you do differently because of it. Understanding that changes nothing is trivia. The point is the change it makes to what you do, make, or choose.

Your ability to explain what you understand out loud is how anyone — an interviewer, a colleague, a collaborator — will know what you actually have.

Courses end; the habit does not have to. What you keep is a way of working on anything unfamiliar — choose something worth understanding, find out how it really works, let that change what you do, and then do it again on the next thing. By December you will have run it twice.

When we meet. Mondays are online and belong to check-ins — small groups or one-on-one, rolling through the roster all semester. Wednesdays are in person: the sessions that open each sprint, the three Sprint Exams, and the check-ins that are better in a room.
