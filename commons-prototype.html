What this prototype is showing

  • One foundation, three surfaces. Course, roster, identity and conversation-capture are built once. What changes between the three assignment types is who the student is talking to.
  • Pseudonymous with peers, named with staff. The campfire keeps pseudonyms and 25 messages now triggers a question rather than an automatic reveal. Live sessions and interviews use real names, and the two are not linked on the student side.
  • Not a hard wall, but not everything at once either. Students see their own check-in status and everything a teacher or TA has shared. Staff also keep working notes — held back until the moment is right, then promoted to shared with a chance to rewrite. Students are told the notes exist.
  • Completion, not scores. Per-student completion drives the participation grade. Nothing about the quality of a student's thinking is shown as a number — to them or on a dashboard.
  • Check-ins are booked, and can be a group of up to three. One student books and invites; the others are asked, not added. The booking is what tells the recording who is in the room — and guidance is written per student, never once for the group.
  • Voice is built once, for the check-ins. Instructor and TA interviews get the microphone — one-to-one, close mic, quiet room, no Sensei in the middle of it. Peer conversations type their notes as they go, the way Jeremy's tool already does. Voice for peer conversations comes back later, Zoom first.
  • Everything here is in the launch set for 24 August except voice for peer conversations, which comes back after the first class hour has run — Zoom first.
