Fireside Chat — How Hiring Really Works, from Two Recruiters

How to use this page

Christopher Alarcon (campus recruiting: Square/Block, Raytheon, Workday — now Saronic) and Henry Ngo (startup and headhunting: Crunchyroll, health tech) answer the questions your search runs on. Read a section's takeaways, then hit Watch to jump the player there — or play it straight through.

Full recording (89 min) · also on Panopto with captions and speed controls.
0:00 · ~7 min

Meet the recruiters

  • Two vantage points on the same process: Chris has run campus hiring at big tech and defense for 10+ years; Henry has hired for startups from seed stage to scale, and now builds hiring systems.
  • Between them they've recruited nearly every role you're applying for — what follows is how your application actually gets read.
7:42 · ~4 min

The magic wand: what they wish every candidate did

  • Put your graduation month + year on your resume. Chris estimates 20% of student resumes are missing it — and without it a recruiter can't even tell which role you're eligible for.
  • Know the problem you care about (Know Your Niche). A real interest — agtech, health, defense — becomes your story, and a story answers interview questions better than memorized answers ever will. Passion is audible, even on a phone screen.
12:12 · ~3 min

Today's market — and where the openings moved

  • The new-grad market is saturated: strong candidates stay available far later in the year than they used to.
  • Big companies are pulling back on junior hiring — and startups picked it up: companies as small as 15 people now run intern programs. The openings moved; your digging should too (Know the Market).
15:09 · ~9 min

No posting? Get on the radar anyway

  • Startups hire people who show up before the posting exists: find the founder or chief of staff on LinkedIn, send a real message, and follow up — "they'll let you know if it's too much."
  • Y Combinator companies are a proving ground — founders answer cold email all day. (Also: bold.com's job board.)
  • Chris landed his current job exactly this way: AI built the list (companies hiring interns with no campus recruiter), one human cold email did the rest — offer within two weeks. AI does the deep work; the human touch closes (Superagency + Human Value).
24:03 · ~9 min

Be the one they remember

  • Flip your mindset: it's your one interview of the day, but the recruiter's ninth. The candidates who blend together lose; the one real story — Chris still retells a student's Iron Man answer a decade later — gets remembered and championed (purpose alignment — one of your three signals).
  • Your real "why" gives the recruiter a button to push for you when the process stalls.
  • Henry shortlisted 2 of 300 applicants on the strength of their human-written cover letters. Writing your own builds the muscle AI can't (hand-write first — ownership shows) — and yes, write the optional cover letter.
33:02 · ~10 min

Resumes that survive a thousand reads

  • High-volume recruiters read to reject. The format that survives: education + graduation date up top, then skills, then experience and projects that show where you used them — "tell me, show me" (Resume Dojo — X/Y/Z and the recruiter's-eye test).
  • Your first reader is almost never an engineer — write so a recruiter with a history degree can see your fit in seconds.
  • Clubs and fundraisers are the cherry on top, not the case. The case is the technical skills the role needs.
43:03 · ~6 min

No internship yet? Build the experience

  • "A project is something you're doing that you're not getting paid for — but somebody in the world is." (your skill-to-learn, made visible) Pick real work (Chris's example: build on Square's payment APIs when applying to Square) and write it up like a job, not an apology.
  • Henry built a portfolio site in two hours with v0 after a head of talent challenged applicants to build something — it got him the interview. A small working artifact changes how you're read (Show, don't tell — your optional Q4 artifact).
48:48 · ~7 min

Interviews: prepare with AI, show up human

  • Chris's "study sheets": feed each interviewer's LinkedIn + the job description to AI → how you'd work together, questions they might ask, questions worth asking back.
  • Henry's frame for "tell me about yourself": People, Purpose, Product — who you worked with, what your role was for, what the product/business was (compare: your three signals).
  • Never use AI during the interview. Both have caught candidates reading from a teleprompter — it's always visible, and it ends the process.
55:48 · ~9 min

Negotiating without fear

  • Shoot your shot with a reason (making your case): a competing offer, real costs, or market research (levels.fyi) (Know the Market) — "I want more" isn't a case, but "here's why" opens a conversation.
  • When base pay is fixed (many intern programs set rates for pay equity), ask about sign-on and relocation bonuses — one-time money is a much easier yes.
  • Stay on good terms with your recruiter: they're the one who goes to bat for you inside the company.
64:42 · ~19 min

Student Q&A: certs, master's degrees, career switchers, big tech

  • Certifications count only when the role asks for them — a relevant project beats a cert "150% of the time" (Armando's question).
  • Master's degree: if you graduate without an offer, enrolling re-opens internship eligibility while you keep hunting (William's question).
  • Career switchers: keep the corporate story (Know Your Niche — your story is part of the case), but don't let it overshadow the skills you're switching to. "Resume space is beachfront property: this is a six-figure document" (Keidra's question).
  • Big tech's generic postings: research the actual teams on LinkedIn and tailor toward the one that fits (sharpen your bet) — a good recruiter routes a specific, aligned candidate (Kim's question).
