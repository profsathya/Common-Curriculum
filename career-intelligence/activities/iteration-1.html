Career Intelligence · Week 5 · Own Your Search

Iteration 1 — Run Your Next Bet

This week's goal

Turn your Week 4 results into a sharper second bet — that's the skill: reading a signal and adjusting, and it's what makes your second round smarter than your first.

Week 5 is the second turn of the same loop, not new content. Your Search Log carries forward: each iteration adds a bet → a read → an adjustment. Silence is still data.

The iteration

Step 1 · Read your Week 4 signal

What did the 10 + 10 actually return?

  1. Open your Search Log. For group A and group B: how many sent, and what came back — replies, screens, silence?
  2. Write one honest line: what does that result say about your bet — confirmed, disproven, or too early to tell?
  3. If it's quiet, ask the Week 5 question: was the idea wrong (your bet), or the execution (too few sent, edge not visible on the resume, unfair test)?
Step 2 · Refine your niche, then pick your next bet

Adjust, don't just repeat

  1. Refine your niche: based on what round 1 returned, sharpen who you're for — tighter role types, a clearer edge.
  2. Pick your next bet: a new variable (if round 1 answered your question), the same bet run properly (if execution was the problem), or a sharper version of B. Name your new A and B.
  3. Keep the test fair: one resume, your B-edge visible, and name the signal you'll watch.
Step 3 · Run it

Same rhythm, new rows

  1. Add this iteration's rows to your Search Log — same tab, the log is the story of your search.
  2. Keep the rhythm: 3–5 quality applications a day, logged as you go.

Dojo: your Search Strategy Dojo already knows this loop — bring it your Week 4 results and let it pressure-test your read and your next bet. If you're applying for a warm referral this week, aim your iteration at the role you want referred to.

Capture & submit

Submission type: Text entry. Paste in:

  • Your read: what Week 4's 10 + 10 returned (A vs. B) and what it tells you — idea, execution, or too early.
  • Your next bet: the new A and B (or the same bet run properly) and why.
  • One adjustment you're making this iteration that you can demonstrate.
