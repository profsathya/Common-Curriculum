## What makes this not just another effort (the message spine — Sathya, 7/21)

The ask is feedback and partnership, not a victory lap — and the poster must also make clear CTI is not one more AI-and-education effort. Four things set it apart, stated as affirmative CTI commitments (no comparative framing):

1. **We ask a fundamental question.** How do you identify and nurture the human qualities that matter most as AI grows — agency, curiosity, judgment, adaptability, and the ability to work with uncertainty?
2. **We propose a framework, as a hypothesis.** Comprehensive, grounded in research, and inspired by what AI makes possible — testable, not a slogan.
3. **We learn through short experiments.** Learning experiences that average about six weeks, each designed to teach us something; we measure, learn, and iterate.
4. **We develop the framework in ourselves, alongside learners.** We only ask learners to grow capabilities we are actively growing in our own work — so the team and the learners co-evolve.

These four are the poster's real differentiator and the reason the "hypothesis, in the open" posture invites partnership: a hypothesis is something serious people can test with us. They map to CTI's framework (the five qualities live inside SDL / Superagency / judgment work / Adaptive Builder / uncertainty tolerance) and to `vision/what-we-are-trying-to-do.md` (the co-evolution discipline). Woven into the poster and both fliers.

## The demo (Sathya, 7/21 — one solo experience, optionally shared)

Direction as of 7/21: the anchor is a **solo Dojo experience** the visitor runs on their own device; a **connection layer** (add a partner via a shared code) is likely folded into that same experience rather than being a separate demo — Sathya is still deciding (see below).

### The anchor — the Dojo coaches you (Self-Directed Learner, productive friction)

The strongest proof of the approach is feeling it. Their device, their material:

- **A small "INSPIRE demo" Dojo module** built from the Dojo Core (dojo-design skill): one QR → a clean page with a copy-one-block prompt + "paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini." No account, no setup, no app of ours.
- **The move that makes it land: it coaches them on *their* work.** The prompt invites them to bring one real sentence — a course goal, a program description, a policy aim — and the Dojo runs two or three Sensei/Challenger rounds on it. Faculty and administrators feel real coaching on material they care about, with every decision staying theirs. That is the pedagogy, demonstrated, in the time a gallery walk allows.
- **Productive friction by design.** The Dojo makes you think; it does not think for you. This is the exact thing the field is now learning matters (see the Khanmigo section below) — and it is what CTI built on from the start.
- **Fallbacks:** a printed sample dialogue on the poster (works with zero wifi), and a table card with the QR + prompt so people can take it home. The demo travels — every card is a seed.

### The connection demo — AI-enabled human connection (STILL BEING DECIDED, Sathya 7/21)

Sathya wants a demo that involves **more than one person** — a core claim is that AI can deepen human connection, not replace it (Integrative Solver: "Connect humans, domains, and perspectives"). He is **not settled on the exact demo** and is thinking it through. What he wants it to do: *seamlessly integrate into the conference experience and help attendees connect with a partner, with AI playing a constructive role.*

**Current direction (his, 7/21):** likely **one demo, not two** — a visitor starts solo, then has the option to **add a friend by creating a unique code they share with their partner(s)**, so the same experience extends from individual to shared without a separate booth flow. This fits the gallery walk: someone tries it alone, then pulls in the person next to them with a code.

**Direction chosen (7/21): give the visitor the choice of three paths, one engine.** Sathya's call — offer all three flows as "doors," each conference-relevant, each showing the Dojo make you think rather than answer, each ending with **share-by-code** so a second person picks up the same chat and the two go deeper. Full flow scripts in `alan/inspire-demo-flow-ideas-2026-07-21.md`.

**Prototype BUILT (7/21): `alan/dojo-demo.html`** — a mobile-native, single-file web UI of the whole Dojo demo, focused on making metacognition visible (Sathya's brief). What it has: a three-door start screen (Sharpen the question / Steelman the other side / Bring it home); a real chat UI with character chips (Sensei, Challenger, Framer, Auditor, Connector), one question at a time, tap-to-respond chips + free text, typing indicators; an inline **metacognition tag** under each Dojo turn and a pull-up **"Your thinking, made visible" trace** (the 🧠 counter in the header); an end-of-path recap; and the **share screen** — mints an inquiry code (e.g. DOJO-XDLJ) + invite link, "simulate a partner joins," then a two-person deepening turn. Smoke-tested end-to-end in a headless browser (all three paths + share + trace).

**What it is / isn't:** a front-end **prototype** with branching, scripted Dojo responses — it demonstrates the experience and the metacognition beautifully but does not call a live LLM. To run on real attendee input at the conference it needs one of: (a) a small backend that calls an LLM behind an inquiry-code store, or (b) the bring-your-own-AI handoff. Decided direction (7/21): build it as a **real topic in the Symbiotic Thinking Dojo app** — see `alan/inspire-demo-implementation-plan-2026-07-21.md`.

**Privacy model (Sathya, 7/21):** the solo path stores **nothing** server-side — the conversation stays on the visitor's device. Only if they choose to **share** do we push the conversation to our backend, and only after an explicit, friendly heads-up ("we'll save this so your partner can join — we hope that's okay, it's optional"); decline keeps it private. Marketing frames the shared version positively — human + human + AI as our approach to symbiotic thinking with human connection, optional by design. The consent gate is shown working in the prototype.

## Khanmigo lesson — validation for the approach (added 7/21)

Sal Khan's 2026 reflections on Khanmigo are a strong, citable validation of CTI's design, and a ready talking point for the gallery walk and funder conversations. In his own account the first Khanmigo did not produce the hoped-for learning gains; Chalkbeat and others reported it as "a non-event" that many students "just didn't use much." The lesson Khan draws is that the AI let students **sidestep productive struggle**, and the fix is to make "productive struggle harder to sidestep," weave the AI into practice, and prompt students to explain their reasoning — with the "biggest lever" being "investing in the human systems."

That is the ground CTI has designed on from the start. The Dojo is built as **productive struggle by design** — the struggle is enforced by the design, not requested by a prompt (dojo-design skill; pedagogy-principles). Symbiotic Thinking is human-led practice, not tool dependence — the same failure mode Lark named with the "floaties" caution. Peer conversation systems and human touchpoints (and Demo 2 above) are exactly the "human systems" Khan now points to.

The no-comparative-framing rule still holds on the artifacts: no "Khan failed, we didn't" copy on the poster. It shows up affirmatively — "productive friction by design; it makes you think, it does not think for you." The Khan reference lives here in the plan as evidence and as an opener at the table. Sources: Sal Khan's note (blog.khanacademy.org), Chalkbeat (2026-04-09).
