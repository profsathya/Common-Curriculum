The Question We Work On

As AI grows, how do you identify and nurture the human qualities that matter most, and how do you know they are growing?

The difficulty is one of pace. The technology moves quickly and crosses every boundary at once, and the systems that prepare people for work are built to move deliberately and within their own boundaries. Holding both is the real work: moving at the speed the technology sets while staying grounded in academic values.

Nobody has the full answer yet. We are working it out with real learners, in the open.

What is Unique About Our Work

How we frame the problem. We ask what AI changes across human knowledge and work as a whole, and which human qualities hold their value in every discipline.
How we approach the work. We learn alongside students, guiding and building together on ground that is new for faculty too. The work runs as small experiments of six to ten weeks, each a rigorous build-measure-learn loop that identifies, nurtures, and tracks the qualities behind self-directed action. The Dojo and the Commons are the tools we are building for conversations of that depth.
How we understand our audience. Students who begin at community colleges and regional public universities. Fifteen years of knowing where they start and where they need to arrive.

Our work lives where all three meet.

Six Qualities, Read From Situations

We are narrowing to six qualities that we can nurture and track as they develop the durable capabilities:

CareSelf-KnowledgeSelf-RegulationInitiativeAdaptabilityComfort With Uncertainty

Agency is what these add up to, so we read it from the six together rather than asking about it directly.

We read them from situations. A student meets a specific, real moment and we look at what they do in it: the move they make, the question they ask, the work they produce. We start from validated instruments and evolve them into situation-specific prompts, with anchor points and scoring guides fixed in advance so the evidence stays rigorous and comparable across pilots.

Two of the six definitions are locked. Four are still being sharpened, in the open.

Our commitment

CTI is committed to this work, because we believe that figuring out how to define, develop, and measure a discipline-neutral, transferable set of human capabilities in scale is imperative to ensure that the AI transition doesn’t become yet another technological advancement that widens inequality.
