Three Ways to Bring the Human In

The feedback on the current draft was that it reads academic and that no person is visible in it. That is accurate. Every element on the poster today sits at the level of category: framework, capabilities, qualities, pilots, percentages. The only words for people are “learners” and “students,” both plural and both abstract. Nobody on the poster has a face, a name, or a sentence of their own.

Three versions are below. Each one takes a different route to the same fix, and they combine. All three also share one small change so the comparison is fair: the middle column ground moved from a cool blue to a warm neutral. That warmth is separable from the content in each version and belongs to the design pass either way.

The people, names, quotes, and story in these drafts are written stand-ins for layout review. They are plausible and specific on purpose, so the layouts can be judged honestly, and every one of them needs to be replaced with real, cleared material before anything prints.

The Three Versions

Version AFaces and Names

A band of four portraits sits directly under the hero, each with a first name and one line about where they started and where they are now. Every pilot picks up a face. An overlapping row of portraits above the stats bar turns 750+ placements into 750+ people.

This is the most direct answer to the feedback and the least disruptive to the layout, because it adds rather than rearranges. It is also the one a passerby feels from ten feet away, which matters for a gallery walk.

What it needs from you: three to five cleared photographs with signed releases, plus real first names and one true line each. Releases are the long pole against the October date.

Version BLearner Voice

No photography required, so it survives if releases move slowly. A four-turn Dojo exchange sits in the middle column under the framework, showing a learner’s own sentence and the coaching question that came back. Three pilots carry a one-line quote from someone inside them. Beside the six qualities, a pair of sentences from week one and week eight of the same learner shows growth in her own words.

This version does something the others do not: the before-and-after pair proves the measurement claim while it humanizes. A reader sees Initiative and Care in a sentence rather than reading that they are tracked.

What it needs from you: consented or anonymized Dojo transcripts and a handful of real learner lines. You likely have most of this already.
Open Version B →
Version COne Story Spine

A full-width strip across the bottom follows one learner end to end in five steps: where she started, the situation she met, the move she made, what CTI read from that move, and where she is now. A short line sets it up, so everything above the strip reads as the method and the strip reads as one run of it.

This is the most distinctive of the three and the one that earns the most. Situation-based measurement is currently a claim on the poster; here a reader watches one situation actually get read, with the two qualities named and the anchors mentioned. It is also the biggest structural change and adds the most height.

What it needs from you: one well-documented learner story, used with permission, or clearly labeled as a composite.
Open Version C →

How They Combine

B and C are the strongest pairing if photo releases are slow, because neither depends on images and they reinforce each other: the story spine gives a reader one person to follow, and the voice elements keep that person audible in the columns above. Adding A on top of either is straightforward whenever cleared photos arrive, since the portrait band and the pilot faces slot in without moving anything else.

The Design Layer, Separately

Some of the academic feeling is content and some of it is drawing. A warmer ground, more air between blocks, and a Venn drawn with a softer hand would carry a good share of the warmth on their own. That work belongs to the August design pass, and any of these three gives it something human to design around.

One Thing to Decide With It

All three versions run taller than 4:3, as the current draft already does. Version A is roughly 1865 tall against 1400 wide, B is 1947, C is 1979, where 4:3 would be 1050. The reflow to true poster proportions is still worth doing before the design pass, and it is easier to do once the human content is settled, because that content is what will set the final column balance.
