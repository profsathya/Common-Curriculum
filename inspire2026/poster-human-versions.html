Three Ways to Bring the Human In

The feedback on the current draft was that it reads academic and that no person is visible in it. That is accurate. Every element on the poster today sits at the level of category: framework, capabilities, qualities, pilots, percentages. The only words for people are “learners” and “students,” both plural and both abstract. Nobody on the poster has a face, a name, or a sentence of their own.

Three versions are below. Each one takes a different route to the same fix, and they combine. All three now sit on Eros’s revised design: his palette distribution, type scale, column proportions, and section order, with our Venn diagram kept in place of his triangle. The base draft carries the same design, so the four are directly comparable.

The people, names, quotes, and story in these drafts are written stand-ins for layout review. They are plausible and specific on purpose, so the layouts can be judged honestly, and every one of them needs to be replaced with real, cleared material before anything prints.

The Three Versions

Version AFaces and Names

A band of four portraits sits directly under the hero, each with a first name and one line about where they started and where they are now. Every pilot picks up a face. An overlapping row of portraits above the stats bar turns 750+ placements into 750+ people.

This is the most direct answer to the feedback and the least disruptive to the layout, because it adds rather than rearranges. It is also the one a passerby feels from ten feet away, which matters for a gallery walk.

What it needs from you: three to five cleared photographs with signed releases, plus real first names and one true line each. Releases are the long pole against the October date.

Version BLearner Voice

No photography required, so it survives if releases move slowly. A four-turn exchange with the Sensei sits in the middle column under the framework, headed “A Simple Demonstration of Symbiotic Thinking,” showing a learner’s own sentence and the coaching question that came back. One pilot carries a one-line quote from someone inside it. Under the six qualities, a pair of sentences from week one and week eight of the same learner shows growth in her own words.

This version does something the others do not: the before-and-after pair proves the measurement claim while it humanizes. A reader sees Initiative and Care in a sentence rather than reading that they are tracked.

What it needs from you: consented or anonymized Dojo transcripts and a handful of real learner lines. You likely have most of this already.

Version COne Story Spine

A full-width strip across the bottom follows one learner end to end in five steps: where she started, the situation she met, the move she made, what CTI read from that move, and where she is now. A short line sets it up, so everything above the strip reads as the method and the strip reads as one run of it.

This is the most distinctive of the three and the one that earns the most. Situation-based measurement is currently a claim on the poster; here a reader watches one situation actually get read, with the two qualities named and the anchors mentioned. It is also the biggest structural change, since the strip has to be paid for out of the three columns above it.

What it needs from you: one well-documented learner story, used with permission, or clearly labeled as a composite.
Open Version C →

How They Combine

B and C are the strongest pairing if photo releases are slow, because neither depends on images and they reinforce each other: the story spine gives a reader one person to follow, and the voice elements keep that person audible in the columns above. Adding A on top of either is straightforward whenever cleared photos arrive, since the portrait band and the pilot faces slot in without moving anything else.

The Design Layer, Now Applied

Eros’s revised comp has been read for its color, type, and organization decisions, and all three of them are now carried through the base draft and all three versions. His palette turns out to use the same brand hexes we already had; what changed is the distribution, with green doing more of the work in the headings and the callout boxes and navy reserved for the hero and the stats bar. His type scale is tighter and his section headings are small uppercase rules-free labels rather than underlined blue headings. His column proportions are narrow, wide, medium rather than three equal thirds, which is what gives the framework diagram room to breathe. Our Venn diagram stays, per your note, in the place his triangle occupied.

One Thing to Decide With It

All four drafts now sit at true 4:3, 1400 wide by 1050 tall, with no column overflowing. Getting there cost some copy. The pace paragraph, the approach items, the Our Ask sentence, three of the four pilot descriptions, the six-qualities explanation, and the See How It Works line are all shorter than they were, and the “Our work lives where all three meet” line under the Venn is gone. Version B gave up the most: two of its three pilot quotes and its six-qualities explanation, with the week-one and week-eight pair doing that explanatory work instead. The Venn also renders smaller in the versions than in the base, at 150 pixels in the base and the voice version, 132 in the story version, and 124 in the faces version. All of that is worth a look before anything prints.
