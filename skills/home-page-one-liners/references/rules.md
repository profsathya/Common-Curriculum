# Home-page one-liners - the rules, and where they came from

_This is the reference behind `../SKILL.md`, the one-screen checklist run on the one-line descriptions under entries on a course home page (`home.html`) before they reach Sathya. Read this file once at the start; run the checklist on the output. Every checklist line traces to a section here. When a rule changes here, change its test in SKILL.md in the same edit; when a test is added there, its rule and the correction it came from go here. Version history is at the end._

On a bare-bones course home page, each assignment, activity, or exam entry has a title and a single line underneath it (`You leave with: ...` for upcoming or current work, `You left with: ...` for past sprints, or a direct description for an exam or closing story). Because students scan the home page before opening an assignment page, that one line carries the purpose of the entry. It tells the student what understanding, capability, or experience they gain from doing the work.

All before/after pairs below come from Sathya's 29 October 2026 revision of `cowork/fall-2026-courses/cst286-home-mockup-text.md` against `cst286-sprint3-home-mockup.html`.

## Outcome over inventory

Name the understanding, capability, or experience the student walks away with, not the form boxes they fill in or a count of the items they submit. A line built from the form's field labels turns the home page into a packing list.

- *My Sprint 2 experience, and the path I choose*
  - Before: "You leave with: the path you chose, and why"
  - Sathya's replacement: "You leave with: an understanding of the three possible paths to explore during Sprint 3"
- *Explore Physics Loop*
  - Before: "You left with: one physics behavior you tinkered with and a question about it"
  - Sathya's replacement: "You left with: experience of tinkering with physical system simulations"

## This activity, not the next step

State what the student accomplishes or demonstrates in this activity itself. Do not reduce an assignment to preparation for an exam or to the piece the next item in the sequence consumes.

- *Five real-world scenarios*
  - Before: "You leave with: one scenario you can walk through on the exam"
  - Sathya's replacement: "You leave with: having demonstrated where you can apply what you learned and where it doesn't apply"

## Full course terms

Write out the course's concepts in full, self-contained terms (`a physical system and its quantitative relationship`, `the physical conditions in which it is applicable`, `a physics learning goal`). Do not use shorthand lifted from inside the form (`from what`, `the condition it needs`, `written as a prediction`) that a student can only decode after opening the assignment page.

- *My Sprint 3 goal and plan*
  - Before: "You leave with: the quantity you will predict, and from what"
  - Sathya's replacement: "You leave with: a well-defined goal for learning about a physical system and its quantitative relationship"
- *Find and confirm my relationship*
  - Before: "You leave with: the relationship, its units, and the condition it needs"
  - Sathya's replacement: "You leave with: the quantitative relationship, its units, and the physical conditions in which it is applicable"

## How it works, and both halves

Ground the line in the concrete mechanism of the activity, and keep both halves of a two-part experience visible:

1. **Name the basis of a reflection instead of a vague output.**
   - *What are my priorities*
     - Before: "You left with: a list of what you actually care about, in your own words"
     - Sathya's replacement: "You left with: reflection on your priorities based on what you spend time on"
2. **Frame verification as checking your own learning, not performing for a grader.**
   - *Physics learning goal, and how you would demonstrate it*
     - Before: "You left with: a physics goal, and what you would do to show you reached it"
     - Sathya's replacement: "You left with: a physics learning goal, and how you verify your learning"
3. **Include both self-directed learning (*how you learn*) and subject matter (*what you learned*) when an activity covers both.**
   - *My Sprint 3 experience*
     - Before: "The story of what you learned, in your words"
     - Sathya's replacement: "The story of how you learn and what you learned"
4. **Name the full exam format and experience, not only the medium.**
   - *Sprint 3 Exam*
     - Before: "In class, on paper"
     - Sathya's replacement: "In class, closed-book written exam and partner conversation"

## No title echo or filler

The one-liner sits directly under the entry's title, so repeating the title's words (`the path you chose` under `...and the path I choose`, or `your sources, and your judgement of each one` under `Find and judge your sources`) wastes the line. Use the line to add what the title leaves unsaid: the understanding gained or how the activity works.

Cut voice filler and anti-AI tags (`actually`, `in your words`, `in your own words`, `your own read of`).
