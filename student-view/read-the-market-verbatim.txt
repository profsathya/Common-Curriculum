Two worked examples
What the outcome looks like when it is met — written by us as models, not student work.

Example 1 — six new-grad software and data postings.

The niche. Backend and data engineering roles for new graduates at consumer software companies.

Asking for now, one. Code review from day one — 3 of the 6 postings. Underdog: “participating in code reviews and ensuring engineering best practices”; Chewy: “Participate in code reviews to understand design principles, version control, and testing best practices”.

Asking for now, two. Side projects listed as a qualification, next to the degree — 2 of the 6. Underdog: “Track record of shipping high-quality, feature-rich products with a portfolio of side projects”. I had assumed side projects were a nice-to-have.

What I expect to change. More postings will say up front that AI screens applications before a person reads them. Today 2 of the 6 say so — Sigma Computing: “This hiring process utilizes artificial intelligence tools to assist in candidate screening and assessment”. Driver: states are starting to require employers to tell applicants when AI is used in hiring decisions. Signal: in three months, how many of 10 fresh postings in this niche carry a screening disclosure.

What I would do. Use the rest of my time in college on projects with real impact, so that what I can show is the qualification.

Example 2 — five cybersecurity postings.

The niche. Graduate application-security and product-security roles at large consumer platforms.

Asking for now, one. Code in more than one language, as a minimum — 2 of the 5. Both TikTok graduate roles, Product Security Engineer, Seattle and Application Security Engineer, Singapore: “writing and reviewing code in at least two of the following programming languages: Kotlin, Swift, TypeScript, Go, Python, Rust” — and none of those is the language most of my coursework is in.

Asking for now, two. Public security work done on my own — 2 of the 5 list “CTF or Live Hacking Event experience” and “Bug bounty experience” as preferred qualifications, things I can do this semester without waiting for an internship.

What I expect to change. Reviewing code that an AI wrote will show up as its own line in these postings. Driver: more of the code these teams ship is AI-generated, and someone has to check it for the same flaws a person would introduce. Signal: in three months, whether any of 10 fresh postings mention reviewing AI-generated code.

What I would do. Use the rest of my time in college on projects with real impact — pick up Go or Rust properly and enter events where the work is public.

Futures thinking, properly
The method behind the driver and signal in box 3.

Finding the force behind a change and then finding the thing you can watch is the core move in futures thinking. The Institute for the Future teaches it in Ready, Set, Future! Introduction to Futures Thinking on Coursera, part of a longer Futures Thinking specialization. Nothing on this page depends on it.

The Know the Market practice Dojo
For when you have postings open but the terms are not resolving into a pattern.

What it needs from you first: 3 to 5 real postings pasted in as text. It cannot open links or browse, so bring the actual words.

What it does. It walks you through harvesting the exact terms in five passes — titles, skills, tools, qualifications and domain, then whole phrases that repeat — one per turn. Then it groups them into a keyword set, states your segment as a one-sentence working hypothesis from a small sample, and builds a search you can click to find more postings like them.

What it does not cover: box 3. It reads the market as it is now; the driver and the signal stay yours.

Open https://dojo.symbioticthinking.ai/?topic=know-market — if it asks for a key, use the link from the week 1 email, subject Symbiotic Thinking Dojo API Key. It ends by handing you a summary block and a JSON block: those are working material to draw on for the boxes above, in your own words. What goes to Canvas is the boxes and the copy button.

Your own AI Dojo
For carrying this further than the page asks.

Your own Dojo keeps your context across the semester, which this page does not, and it is where the standing routine from Tuesday will live. A prompt to start from:

I am an early-career computing student aiming at [YOUR NICHE]. Find 12 real, currently open job postings from different companies where that niche is explicit in the responsibilities or requirements. For each, give me the company, title, link, and quote the 3–5 exact phrases that describe the work. Then tell me which phrases recur across at least half of them, and show me the count. Do not invent postings — only ones you can link to.

Then, separately: what has changed in postings like these over the last two years, what is driving that change, and what observable signal would tell me in three months whether it is continuing? For each claim, show me your evidence, and tell me where you are inferring.

What comes back is material for your answer, not the answer. You supplied the niche; you decide which repeated phrases matter for the work you want. Pick one of its claims and check it against a posting you open yourself.
