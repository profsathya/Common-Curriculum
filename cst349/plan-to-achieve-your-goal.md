# Plan to achieve your goal, and how you will verify it

Week 4 · due Friday 18 September, 11:59 p.m.

Take ownership of your progress — this activity is provided to help you, but the grade for this activity will not affect your final grade.

### Purpose

Start with the activity sheet you worked on with your partner in class. If you missed class, you can answer each of the questions for the first time here. Then develop a more detailed plan for building the habit that will demonstrate your growth.

### Tasks

Each + opens guidance, examples, a reflection question and a box to write in. The guidance and reflection are provided to support you to do a high quality job of answering the questions.

**1. Type up your sheet. Section A as it stands now, including anything you changed after your partner's questions. Then Section B: the two questions your partner chose, and their notes on your answers.**

*Guidance.* Write it so that a hiring manager who was not in the room could check your habit from what is written. Section A has four parts: your growth goal, and why it takes you out of your comfort zone; the habit change that will demonstrate your growth — what you will do, on which days, for how long, and starting when; the evidence of this new professional habit you will have collected by the end of the sprint; and the validation that comes from someone or something external, so you can demonstrate the habit to a hiring manager without any bias. Then add the two questions your partner chose, and what they wrote about your answers. If a question showed you something missing from Section A, fix it in the typed version.

**If you missed class.** Open the [activity sheet (Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1-iznyVV3PloCaelCkaW6nEJ-JeyfeKhfM87CsN6u2l4/edit) and fill in Section A. For Section B, find a partner who also missed class and question each other. You can also be your own partner for this entry only: write the questions a hiring manager would ask you, choose the two best, and answer them.

*Example, to shape your thinking, not a model to copy.*
**Section A, item 2, as typed** — Every Monday and Thursday, 8 to 8:45 pm, starting Thursday 17 September, I read one pull request in an open-source project I use. I write three lines on what the change does and one question I would ask its author. **Section B, Q1** — “How would you know your three lines are right?” My partner noted that I had no way to check my summary, and suggested I compare it with the maintainers’ review comments on the same pull request.

*Your answer to task 1.* Sections A and B, typed in full.

*Check it yourself.*
- Section A is complete: your growth goal and why it takes you out of your comfort zone, the habit change with what, which days, how long and starting when, the evidence you will have collected by the end of the sprint, and the external validation.
- Section B has the two questions your partner chose and their notes on your answers.
- A hiring manager who was not in the room could check the habit from what is written.

*Reflection.* What changed in Section A because of your partner’s questions?

**2. My habit plan. Between now and the Sprint 1 Exam on 30 September, when will your habit happen each week, and what evidence of it will each week add? Open your calendar and block the time now; write the days and hours here.**

*Guidance.* Work backwards from the exam. Week 6 is the exam itself; in week 5 you write up *Demonstrate growth in your Sprint 1 goal*, due Monday 28 September; this week is where the habit starts. Put each session of the habit into your calendar before you write it here. A block names the day, the hour, the place, and the one thing you will do in it. “Work on my communication skills” is not a block. Next to each week, write the evidence that week adds, such as a recording, a log entry, a commit, or a note from the person or source that validates it.

*Example, to shape your thinking, not a model to copy.*
**Week 4** — Thursday 8–8:45 pm, desk at home: read one pull request and write the three lines and the question. **Week 5** — Monday and Thursday 8–8:45 pm, same place; on Friday, compare my three summaries with the maintainers’ comments and mark where I was wrong. **Evidence by the exam** — five summaries in one document, each with the comparison under it.

*Your answer to task 2.* Week by week, when the habit happens and the evidence it adds — the same blocks that are now in your calendar.

*Check it yourself.*
- Each week to the exam has at least one block.
- Each block names the day, the hour, the place, and the one thing you will do.
- Each week names the evidence it adds.
- The blocks are in your calendar, not only on this page.

*Reflection.* Which block are you most likely to skip, and what would help you keep it?

**3. My main obstacle. Identify the main obstacle that could stop your new habit from happening.**

*Guidance.* Look at your habit plan and identify the obstacle most likely to break the habit. You might have limited time around work and other courses, a habit that depends on someone else being available, or a week when a deadline makes the habit feel less urgent. Explain how this obstacle could affect your plan, and draw on past experience where it helps.

*Examples, to shape your thinking, not models to copy.*
**Time** — I work until 10 pm on Mondays, so a Monday session at 8 pm only exists on paper.
**Depending on someone else** — my roommate is my external validation, and he works weekend nights, so if I leave the listen-back to Sunday it will not happen.

*Your answer to task 3.* The obstacle and how it could affect your plan.

*Check it yourself.*
- It identifies one obstacle, not a list.
- It explains how the obstacle could affect the habit plan.

*Reflection.* Has this obstacle stopped a habit of yours before? What happened that time?

**4. How I will address it. One concrete move you will make against that obstacle, and when.**

*Guidance.* Choose one action to address your obstacle this week, and say when you will take it. Ask your AI Dojo to review your plan from the perspective of a hiring manager, then use its feedback to improve the plan where it helps.

*Examples, to shape your thinking, not models to copy.*
**Against time** — I moved the Monday session to Tuesday 7:30 am, before my 9 am class, and told my partner so they can ask me about it next Wednesday.
**Against depending on someone else** — I asked my roommate for Tuesday at 9 pm, and it is now in both our calendars.

*Your answer to task 4.* The move, and when.

*Check it yourself.*
- It is one concrete move, not a resolution.
- It has a day and a time.
- Someone else could tell whether it happened.

*Reflection.* If you make this move once, what would tell you it worked?

### Criteria

Check it before you submit — the same list your partner can run.

- Sections A and B are typed in full, and a hiring manager could check the habit from them.
- Every week to the exam has at least one calendar block with a day, hour, place and one thing to do, and names the evidence it adds.
- One main obstacle is identified, with how it could affect the habit plan.
- One concrete move against it, with a day and a time.

**What makes it strong**

- Section A describes a habit change that would demonstrate your growth goal, and includes the useful changes from your partner’s questions.
- Your calendar includes time this week to start the habit.
- The evidence and the external validation are things a hiring manager could look at, not only your own account.
- Your move against the obstacle is specific enough for your partner to check whether you followed through.
