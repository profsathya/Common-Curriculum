# Plan to achieve your goal, and how you will verify it

Take ownership of your progress — this activity is provided to help you, but the grade for this activity will not affect your final grade.

### Purpose

Start with the activity sheet you worked on with your partner in class. If you missed class, you can answer each of the questions for the first time here. Then develop a more detailed plan for building the habit that will demonstrate your growth.

### Tasks

Each task has guidance, examples, a reflection question and a box to write in. The guidance and reflection are there to help you do a high quality job of answering the questions.

**1. Type up your sheet.** Section A as it stands now, including anything you changed after your partner's questions. Then Section B: the two questions your partner chose, and their notes on your answers.

*Guidance.* Write it so that a hiring manager who was not in the room could check your habit from what is written. That means your growth goal and why it takes you out of your comfort zone, the habit change (what you will do, on which days, for how long, and starting when), the evidence you will have by the end of the sprint, and the external validation. Then add the two questions your partner chose and what they wrote about your answers. If a question showed you something missing from Section A, fix it in the typed version.

*If you missed class.* Open the [activity sheet](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1-iznyVV3PloCaelCkaW6nEJ-JeyfeKhfM87CsN6u2l4/edit?usp=sharing) and fill in Section A. For Section B, find a partner who also missed class and question each other. You can also be your own partner for this entry only: write the questions a hiring manager would ask you, choose the two best, and answer them.

*Example, to shape your thinking, not a model to copy.* Section A, item 2, as typed: "Every Monday and Thursday, 8 to 8:45 pm, starting Thursday 17 September, I read one pull request in an open-source project I use. I write three lines on what the change does and one question I would ask its author." Section B, Q1: "How would you know your three lines are right?" My partner noted that I had no way to check my summary, and suggested I compare it with the maintainers' review comments on the same pull request.

*Check it yourself.*
- Section A is complete: the growth goal, the habit change with its days, length and start date, the evidence, and the external validation.
- Section B has the two questions your partner chose and their notes on your answers.
- A hiring manager who was not in the room could check the habit from what is written.

*Reflection.* What changed in Section A because of your partner's questions?

**2. My habit plan.** Between now and the Sprint 1 Exam on Wednesday 30 September, when will the habit happen each week, and what evidence will each week add? Open your calendar and block the time now, then write the days and hours here.

*Guidance.* Put each session of the habit into your calendar before you write it here. A block names the day, the hour, the place, and the one thing you will do in it. "Work on my communication skills" is not a block. Next to each week, write the evidence that week adds, such as a recording, a log entry, a commit, or a note from the person or source that validates it.

*Example, to shape your thinking, not a model to copy.* Week 4: Thursday 8 to 8:45 pm, desk at home, read one pull request and write the three lines and the question. Week 5: Monday and Thursday 8 to 8:45 pm, same place; on Friday, compare my three summaries with the maintainers' comments and mark where I was wrong. Evidence by the exam: five summaries in one document, each with the comparison under it.

*Check it yourself.*
- Each week to the exam has at least one block.
- Each block names the day, the hour, the place, and the one thing you will do.
- Each week names the evidence it adds.
- The blocks are in your calendar, not only on this page.

*Reflection.* Which block are you most likely to skip, and what would help you keep it?

**3. My main obstacle.** Identify the main obstacle that could stop the habit from happening.

*Guidance.* Look at your plan and identify the obstacle most likely to break the habit. You might have limited time around work and other courses, a habit that depends on someone else being available, or a week when a deadline makes the habit feel less urgent. Explain how this obstacle could affect your plan, and draw on past experience where it helps.

*Examples, to shape your thinking, not models to copy.* Time: I work until 10 pm on Mondays, so a Monday session at 8 pm only exists on paper. Depending on someone else: my roommate is my external validation, and he works weekend nights, so if I leave the listen-back to Sunday it will not happen.

*Check it yourself.*
- It identifies one obstacle, not a list.
- It explains how the obstacle could affect the plan.

*Reflection.* Has this obstacle stopped a habit of yours before? What happened that time?

**4. How I will address it.** One concrete move you will make against that obstacle, and when.

*Guidance.* Choose one action to address your obstacle this week, and say when you will take it. Ask your AI Dojo to review your plan from the perspective of a hiring manager, then use its feedback to improve the plan where it helps.

*Examples, to shape your thinking, not models to copy.* Against time: I moved the Monday session to Tuesday 7:30 am, before my 9 am class, and told my partner so they can ask me about it next Wednesday. Against depending on someone else: I asked my roommate for Tuesday at 9 pm, and it is now in both our calendars.

*Check it yourself.*
- It is one concrete move, not a resolution.
- It has a day and a time.
- Someone else could tell whether it happened.

*Reflection.* If you make this move once, what would tell you it worked?

### Criteria

Check these before you submit. Your partner can use the same list.

- Sections A and B are typed in full, and a hiring manager could check the habit from them.
- Every week to the exam has at least one calendar block with a day, hour, place and one thing to do, and names the evidence it adds.
- One main obstacle is identified, with how it could affect the plan.
- One concrete move against it, with a day and a time.

### What makes it strong

- Section A describes a habit change that would demonstrate your growth goal, and includes the useful changes from your partner's questions.
- Your calendar includes time this week to start the habit.
- The evidence and the external validation are things a hiring manager could look at, not only your own account.
- Your move against the obstacle is specific enough for your partner to check whether you followed through.

You can write on this page or in your own document, and your submission can be your own write-up, the chat log of your AI Dojo conversation, or any other form that shows the sheet in full (Sections A and B), the calendar blocks, the main obstacle, and your move against it.
