# Sprint 3 Exam - the paired half

Week 15 - Wednesday 2 December, in class - 100 points - Sprint 3 Exam

## Purpose

Learn how to ask questions to understand another person's work and help them deepen their understanding, and write down what you learned.

## Tasks

You have your partner's paper. Complete the tasks below in order. When you finish, press **Copy my answers** and paste the text into the Canvas box.

### Task 1

Select your partner's name.

*Field:* Your partner's name -> a text box with the course roster as suggestions ("Firstname L."). A name that is not on the list can still be typed.

*Beside the name field, highlighted:* **Partner with someone new today: your partner must be someone who was not your partner in the Sprint 1 Exam or the Sprint 2 Exam.**

*Under the name field:* a tick box labelled **I partnered with someone new**, not my Sprint 1 or Sprint 2 Exam partner, with the line *If you work with a partner you had in the Sprint 1 Exam or the Sprint 2 Exam, you will not receive credit for this activity.* The tick is copied with the answers.

### Task 2

Type your partner's six handwritten answers into the six boxes below, exactly as they wrote them. If you cannot read something, ask them. When all six are typed, read them back to your partner and have them tick **Checked by the writer** under the sixth box.

Each box shows the question from the paper above it.

*Box 1 label:* **1a. Write the line you chose: your "Do you think" question and its follow-up question, as closely as you remember them.**
*Box 2 label:* **1b. Describe what your quality assurance partner will look at in your report to answer it, such as a task, a sentence or a piece of evidence.**
*Box 3 label:* **1c. Explain whether your quality assurance partner can answer it from your report alone. If something is missing or not clear in your line that could trip up your quality assurance partner, identify what is missing or not clear and rewrite the line.**
*Box 4 label:* **2a. Apply your own "Do you think" question to your own report to evaluate it: yes, partly or not yet.**
*Box 5 label:* **2b. Give the reason for your answer, referring to what is in your report as much as possible from memory.**
*Box 6 label:* **2c. Answer your follow-up question yourself, and describe one thing you will do because of your answers.**

*Under the sixth box:* one tick box labelled **Checked by the writer** with the line *My partner has read all six boxes against their paper and confirms they say what they wrote.*

*Beside each box:* **Read it aloud** button, as on the CST286 page.

### Task 3

Ask your partner at least two follow-up questions about what they wrote. Write down each question you asked and what your partner said.

A follow-up question that teaches you something points at one specific thing in their answer - the line they chose, what they said their quality assurance partner will look at, the reason they gave for their own answer, the thing they will do, a word they used. [for Sathya's read] A general question could be asked of any paper in the room, so the answer tells you nothing about this paper.

*Worked pair, shown on the page:*
- General: "Was it hard to evaluate your own report?" [for Sathya's read]
- Specific: "In 1b you said your quality assurance partner will look at the two dates in task 1. What do those dates show them about what you learned?" [for Sathya's read]

Come up with your questions in either of these two ways, or both.

**Route 1 (+): Write your own questions**

1. Read your partner's six answers again.
2. Write down every question that comes to you. Do not stop to judge or answer them. If you write a statement, change it into a question. Truly care about the questions.
3. For each question you wrote, find the exact words or phrases in their answer that it points at. Cross out any question you cannot point at.
4. Pick the two questions you are most curious about. Ask those two.

*Box label:* **The questions I wrote down**

**Route 2 (+): Use the AI Question Suggestions button**

Press the button. It reads the six answers you typed and suggests five questions, each one naming the part of the answer it comes from. Pick two and adapt to ask your partner - again your curiosity is your guide. Change the wording to make it your own.

*Button:* AI Question Suggestions. Greyed until all six boxes have text and the **Checked by the writer** tick is on. *Line beside it while greyed:* Type all six answers and have your partner tick **Checked by the writer**, then the suggestions open.
*What it does:* reads the six typed answers and returns five questions. Each question quotes the words in the answer it comes from, and asks about that. It does not suggest questions about the report in general, about how hard it was to evaluate, or about anything that is not in the six boxes.
*If the call fails:* AI Question Suggestions is not available right now. Use "Write your own questions" above.

*Fields:* Follow-up question 1 / What I learned from my partner's response - Follow-up question 2 / What I learned from my partner's response - Follow-up question 3 (if you asked one) / What I learned from my partner's response

### Task 4

Write a summary of what you understand about how your partner evaluated their own report. Include the line they chose, their own answer to it and what they will do because of it. Identify at least one thing you learned from talking to them that was not on the written response. [for Sathya's read]

*Box label:* **Your summary**

## Self-check

Tick each line your submission meets. Your ticks are copied with your answers; the grader verifies each one.

- [ ] My partner's name is selected · 5 pts
- [ ] All six answers are typed as my partner wrote them, not summarised · 15 pts
- [ ] My partner has ticked **Checked by the writer** under the six answers · 5 pts
- [ ] I asked at least two follow-up questions, and each one is written down · 15 pts
- [ ] Each follow-up question points at a specific part of one of the six answers, and my question says which part · 20 pts
- [ ] No follow-up question is a generic one I could ask anyone · 5 pts
- [ ] What I learned from my partner's response is written down for each question · 15 pts
- [ ] The summary says what I understood about the line my partner chose, their own answer to it and what they will do because of it · 15 pts [for Sathya's read]
- [ ] The summary identifies at least one thing I learned that was not on the written response · 5 pts

**What makes it strong.** Your two questions explore the part of the paper that is interesting or relevant - what their quality assurance partner will look at in 1b, or the reason for their own answer in 2b - and your summary is traceable to what your partner said when you asked, rather than repeating the paper back. [for Sathya's read]

**Grading.** 100 points, the paired half of the Sprint 3 Exam. You will be asked to explain this to a peer, the TA and/or the instructor, so make sure you are comfortable with what you have written here.
