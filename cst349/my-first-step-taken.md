# My first step, taken: what I did, what it produced, and where I go from here

Week 10 · due Monday 2 November, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

## Purpose

Take the first step you planned in your Sprint 2 goal, and write down what you did, what came of it, and what you will do next. The answer you wrote in week 8 stays a plan until you act on it, so this is the week the plan starts to become an experience you can talk about in an interview. The Sprint 2 Exam on Wednesday 4 November asks you to reflect on how you identified your question, developed your answer and strengthened it, and the step you report here is the part of that story that actually happened. Taking a small, concrete step and reporting plainly on what it produced is a habit that will serve you in every job, because that is how work moves forward.

## Tasks

"Your step" refers to the one you wrote in the "This week I will ..." box of your Sprint 2 goal. If you took a different step instead, that is fine; say so in task 1.

**Button:** Copy the questions — Paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it.

Each **+** opens guidance, one example and a reflection question. Nothing under a **+** is required. (Page note: the answer box sits outside the +, under the task, as on the other Sprint 2 pages.)

### Task 1 — The step I took, and what it produced

Describe the step you took: what you did, on which day, and who or what it involved. Then describe what it produced, in a form someone else could see - a reply, a meeting that happened, a file, a first version, a measurement. If the step produced less than you expected, write what it did produce, because that is still the result.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 1.** What you did, when, and with whom or what; and what it produced.
*Placeholder:* What I did — … ⏎ What it produced — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- The step is something you did, with the day it happened and the person or thing it involved.
- What it produced is something another person could see, and you say where it is.
- If your step was not the one you planned in week 8, you say what changed.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** Start with the day and the action, the way you would tell a friend what you did this week. Then look for the thing that exists now that did not exist before: a message in your sent folder, a calendar entry that happened, a repository with a first commit, a chapter with your notes in it. If the step was a message that got no reply, the sent message is what it produced, and you can say so. Two or three sentences for each part is enough.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **What I did** — on Tuesday I asked two people outside my major, one from my statistics class and one from my part-time job, whether I could show them my CST 338 project for fifteen minutes next week.
- **What it produced** — one said yes and we put Thursday at 3 in both our calendars; the other has not replied yet. The messages are in my sent folder and the calendar invite is accepted.

**Reflection (behind the +).** What was the moment where you almost did not take the step, and what got you past it?

### Task 2 — What got closer to real

Look back at the STAR answer you wrote in week 8. Identify which part of it - the situation, the task, the action or the result - got closer to real because of this step, and explain how.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 2.** The part of your STAR answer that moved, and how this step moved it.
*Placeholder:* The part that got closer to real — … ⏎ How — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- You name one part of your STAR answer: situation, task, action or result.
- You explain how this step moved that part, in a way a reader could follow from your task 1 answer.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** Most first steps move the situation or the task, because they put you in a place or in front of a person that the answer needs. Some move the action, because you did a small version of what the answer describes. Very few move the result this early, and that is expected. Read your week-8 answer, find the sentence that is a little truer now, and say why.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **The part that got closer to real** — the situation. My answer begins with giving a demo to people who do not write code, and I did not have anyone like that lined up.
- **How** — I now have one person outside my major who has agreed to sit through the demo on Thursday, so the situation in my answer has a real date and a real person in it.

**Reflection (behind the +).** If you gave your STAR answer aloud today, which sentence would you say with more confidence than you could two weeks ago?

### Task 3 — The next step now

Identify the next step you will take, with the day you will take it, and explain how it moves the "By the end of this semester" box of your Sprint 2 goal.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 3.** One step, the day, and how it moves your end-of-semester box.
*Placeholder:* Next step — … ⏎ Day — … ⏎ How it moves my end-of-semester box — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- There is one step, and it has a day.
- You say which part of your "By the end of this semester" box it moves, and how.

*The + reads:* Guidance, an example, a question, and a place to write

**Guidance (behind the +).** Start from what task 1 produced, because the next step follows from what actually happened rather than from the plan alone. If your step produced a meeting, the next step is probably what you will do in it or right after it. If it produced nothing yet, the next step might be the same ask to a different person, or a follow-up with the first. Then open your Sprint 2 goal, read the end-of-semester box, and check that the step you chose moves it. Pick a day you can keep.

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Next step** — give the fifteen-minute demo on Thursday, and write down every question my classmate asks, including the ones I could not answer.
- **Day** — Thursday 5 November, 3 p.m.
- **How it moves my end-of-semester box** — my box says I will have presented one project to three people outside my major with their questions written down. Thursday makes it one of three, and the questions I write down tell me what to prepare before the next two.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which part of this next step depends on someone other than you, and what will you do if they do not come through?

## Criteria

**Checklist** — one line per task. Run it on your own draft; your reviewer runs the same list. *(the click-in at the top of Criteria)*

1. Describe the step you took, with its day and who or what it involved, and what it produced that someone else could see.
2. Name the part of your STAR answer that got closer to real, and explain how this step moved it.
3. Name your next step, with a day, and explain how it moves your end-of-semester box.

**Done** — task 1 has a step with a day and a visible result, and says what changed if the step was not the planned one; task 2 names one STAR part and connects it to task 1; task 3 has one step with a day and the end-of-semester box it moves.

**Strong** — the result in task 1 is specific enough that a classmate could ask to see it; the connection in task 2 is one a reader could make from your week-8 answer without you explaining it; the next step in task 3 follows from what task 1 produced, not only from the plan. A step that produced less than planned, written plainly, meets these criteria as well as one that went to plan.
