### Task 1 — The step I took, and what it produced

Describe the step you took: what you did, on which day, who or what it involved, and the outcome. *(Be careful to consider your response from a second person's point of view: will they see something concrete and meaningful as an outcome, or only process steps that did not produce anything? If the step produced less than you expected, write what you learned and how you are going to improve.)*

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 1.** What you did, when, and with whom or what; and what it produced.
*Placeholder:* What I did — … ⏎ What it produced — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- The step is something you did, with the day it happened and the person or thing it involved.
- The outcome is something a second person would see as concrete and meaningful, and you say where it is.
- If the step produced less than you expected, you say what you learned and how you are going to improve.
- If your step was not the one you planned in week 8, you say what changed.

*The + reads:* An example, more examples, and a question

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **What I did** — on Tuesday I asked two people outside my major, one from my statistics class and one from my part-time job, whether I could show them my CST 338 project for fifteen minutes next week.
- **What it produced** — one said yes and we put Thursday at 3 in both our calendars; the other has not replied yet. The messages are in my sent folder and the calendar invite is accepted.

**More examples** (page note: a click-to-open inside the +, closed by default)

- **What I did** — on Tuesday I asked my supervisor at the help desk whether I could look at the ticket log for the last three months, and said why: I think most lockouts come from one group of staff.
- **What it produced** — she exported the log as a spreadsheet on Wednesday and shared it with me, with the names removed. It is in my work drive, 1,840 rows.

- **What I did** — on Saturday I set up PostgreSQL on my laptop and tried to load a public dataset of airline delays.
- **What it produced** — less than I planned. The install worked, but the load failed twice on a date column and I stopped after an hour. I learned that I should read the dataset's column notes before I write the table, and next time I will load a hundred rows first to find the problems before I load a million.

**Reflection (behind the +).** What was the moment where you almost did not take the step, and what got you past it?

### Task 2 — What got closer to real

Look back at the STAR answer you wrote in week 8. Notice which part, if any - the situation, the task, the action or the result - became more real because of the step you took, and explain how. *(This is a win. This is how we build momentum toward our professional growth.)*

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 2.** The part of your STAR answer that moved, and how this step moved it.
*Placeholder:* The part that got closer to real — … ⏎ How — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- You name one part of your STAR answer: situation, task, action or result; or you say that none moved yet, and why.
- You explain how this step moved that part, in a way a reader could follow from your task 1 answer.

*The + reads:* An example, more examples, and a question

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **The part that got closer to real** — the situation. My answer begins with giving a demo to people who do not write code, and I did not have anyone like that lined up.
- **How** — I now have one person outside my major who has agreed to sit through the demo on Thursday, so the situation in my answer has a real date and a real person in it.

**More examples** (page note: a click-to-open inside the +, closed by default)

- **The part that got closer to real** — the action. My answer says I pulled three months of lockout data and showed which group it came from.
- **How** — I have the three months of data now, and the first thing I did with it was count lockouts by department. That is the first half of the action in my answer, done for real, with a number I can quote.

- **The part that got closer to real** — none yet, and that is fine. My answer is about finding a slow query in a project that someone else depends on, and I do not have a database to find it in.
- **How** — this week's step was the install, and it got me to the point where the next step can touch real data. The situation in my answer starts to move when the dataset is loaded.

**Reflection (behind the +).** If you gave your STAR answer aloud today, which sentence would you say with more confidence than you could two weeks ago?

### Task 3 — Look ahead

Identify the next step you will take, with the day you will take it, and explain how it moves you closer to your Sprint 2 goal. *(Start from what task 1 produced, because the next step follows from what actually happened, not only from the plan. Pick a day you can keep.)*

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 3.** One step, the day, and how it moves you closer to your Sprint 2 goal.
*Placeholder:* Next step — … ⏎ Day — … ⏎ How it moves me closer to my Sprint 2 goal — …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- There is one step, and it has a day.
- You say how that step moves you closer to your Sprint 2 goal.

*The + reads:* An example, more examples, and a question

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

- **Next step** — give the fifteen-minute demo on Thursday, and write down every question my classmate asks, including the ones I could not answer.
- **Day** — Thursday 5 November, 3 p.m.
- **How it moves me closer to my Sprint 2 goal** — my goal is to be able to say I presented my project to people outside my major and took their questions. Thursday is the first of those, and the questions I write down tell me what to prepare before the next two.

**More examples** (page note: a click-to-open inside the +, closed by default)

- **Next step** — make one chart from the ticket log, lockouts per department per week, and show it to my supervisor with one question: is the policy change worth testing for that department only?
- **Day** — Friday 6 November, at our weekly check-in.
- **How it moves me closer to my Sprint 2 goal** — my goal is to be able to say I disagreed with a policy using data and proposed a test. The chart is the data, and the question on Friday is the proposal.

- **Next step** — load a hundred rows of the airline dataset, fix the date column, then load the whole file.
- **Day** — Tuesday 3 November, after class.
- **How it moves me closer to my Sprint 2 goal** — my goal is to be able to say I found and fixed a slow query on a real database. Once the file is loaded I have a table big enough for a query to be slow, which is where the goal starts.

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which part of this next step depends on someone other than you, and what will you do if they do not come through?
