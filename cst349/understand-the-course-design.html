Understand the Course Design

Read this before week 1
Five sprints across sixteen weeks. You define what growth means for you, run a weekly loop on it, add a new pillar to your professional shape, and leave with a self-improvement process that has become second nature. This page explains the machinery: the weekly rhythm, the grade, the interview check-ins, the Sprint Exams, and when attendance is required.

The weekly rhythm: one reflection, one assignment

Each week has one reflection and one assignment — never two assignments due the same week. The work builds one evolving piece per sprint, not a pile of separate tasks.

The reflection is the inside half of your evidence. A few honest lines each week on how the work went and how you feel about it. Work alone can be produced without you growing; feelings alone can be performed. Together, and agreeing with each other, they are hard to fake — and they are what your interview check-ins draw on.

The assignment is that week's single piece of work. Some weeks it is a key item — the sprint's main piece, the one your check-ins sample. Other weeks it is a checkpoint: a short item, credited on submission, there so you can tell you are on track while there is still time to adjust.

Late work loses 10% a day. That means you cannot drift far behind before the credit is gone, which is deliberate — we would rather catch you in week 4 than in week 12.

How the grade works

Half your grade comes from getting the work done — submitting each week's assignment and reflection on time. The other half comes from your ability to explain what you did and why — in interview check-ins and Sprint Exams that sample work you already submitted.

Two things about how we score the explaining half. We measure your movement from your starting point — the baseline you record in the first weeks — so you are never compared to a classmate who started somewhere else. And we grade the loop, not the line: an honest miss you reflected on and adapted from counts as evidence your process works, sometimes stronger evidence than an easy win.

There are no mid-course retakes. A rough check-in is recovered by showing up better prepared at the next one. If the semester gets away from you, there is a catch-up option at the end of the course — talk to us before then, not after.

What happens in the interview check-ins

A check-in is a short conversation where we pick a piece of the work you submitted and ask you to walk us through it: what you set out to move, what happened, what you changed, and why. You will not know in advance which piece we pick, so prepare all of it. Every check-in is a live session — in person or on Zoom with your camera on.

They run in small groups or 1-on-1, all semester. In weeks 1–2 every student is seen: you meet in a group of four, ten minutes with the TA first, then fifteen with Prof. Sathya — where you are starting from, in your own words. After that, rounds spread across the semester.

Your check-in times are posted on The Commons and sent by email. Each scheduled check-in is required, and missing one loses its points — they cannot be recovered. If a conflict is coming, tell us before the check-in, not after.

What happens in a Sprint Exam

The last week of Sprints 1–3 — weeks 6, 10, and 14 — class opens with a Sprint Exam. One page, written by hand. The questions are published ahead of time, with options, so the exam rewards preparation rather than surprise.

Then the page leaves your hands: you trade with a partner, type their answers into a short AI form, talk their thinking through with them, and submit your evaluation of their work. Writing by hand shows the thinking is yours; reading a partner’s page — and being read — is the fastest honest feedback in the course. The second hour of exam days runs interview check-ins.

When attendance is required

Attendance in class is required for weeks 1–3 (orientation, the first check-in sweep, and the goal-setting workshop), on the three Sprint Exam days (weeks 6, 10, and 14), and in week 15, the first wrap-up week.

Between those, your required sessions are the interview check-ins as scheduled on The Commons — also sent to you by email notification. Missing a scheduled check-in loses its points, and those points cannot be recovered.

Every other class session is a working session — structured and self-guided activity while check-ins run. Coming to them is how the work gets done with help in the room; there is no attendance penalty for handling that time your own way.
