CST349 · Professional Seminar · Fall 2026

Learn to Direct Your Own Growth

A key part of being a professional in the age of AI is to make sure you continuously learn and grow on purpose. This semester you build that skill to recognize the growth needed, develop a plan to achieve that and follow through.
Who becomes irreplaceable?
Think about the person on any team everyone calls irreplaceable. It is rarely the person who knows one thing best — it is the one who connects things: the engineer who understands the customer, the designer who reads the data. Careers used to reward being Professional shapes: I → T → π → combWhat it means. An I-shaped professional is deep in one thing. A T adds working knowledge across other fields. π, m, and comb shapes add more deep pillars — two, three, many.Why it matters now. AI is fastest at replacing single-pillar depth. The value it cannot reach sits where pillars combine — judgment that needs two fields, or two viewpoints, at once.What you do with it. In week 1 you locate your own shape. In Sprint 3 you make one move up the ladder — you add a pillar. — deep in one thing. The professionals thriving alongside AI are those who can work across domains, disciplines and systems. This semester you find where you are on that ladder of shapes — I, T, π, comb — and make at least a meaningful effort to move up.

Beyond the grade
What you leave with
  1. A growth process that is yours. Three versions of your own Goal Plan, showing decisions you made — set, measured, adapted — with decreasing levels of guidance and requirements from the instructor.
  2. Your first PillarWhat it means. One deep competence — the vertical stroke in the shape letters. An I-shaped professional stands on one pillar. The T keeps that pillar and adds breadth. π- and m-shaped professionals stand on two or three pillars; the comb on many.Why it matters. AI answers single-field questions well. The calls that need two fields at once — is this technically right, and will the customer accept it? — still need a person who holds both. Each pillar you add puts more of those calls within your reach.What counts as adding one. Real depth started in a second field or another person's viewpoint, connected to what you already know — deliberate practice, not a hobby sampler. move. Evidence you carried that process into a second field or another person's viewpoint — the ability you will need to repeat for the rest of your career.

Your ability to explain your own growth out loud is how anyone — an interviewer, a manager, a mentor, a collaborator — will know what you bring.

The process is the product.

Courses end. The self-improvement process (loop) you build here does not have to. What you keep is the process working professionals use to keep growing as AI reshapes their field: define growth in your own terms, measure it from your own baseline, adapt weekly, and add a pillar when the ground shifts. By December, running it should become second nature to you.

When we meet. Class time is built around conversations, not lectures. Sprints 1–3 open with a first-hour workshop (weeks 3, 7, 11) and close with a Sprint Exam (weeks 6, 10, 14). Every other session is a working session while interviews run — starting with every student in a small-group interview during weeks 1–2.

The design of this course is mine — what you’ll practice, what you’ll practice it on, how you’ll be guided, and how you’ll be graded — and I own the design of your learning experience. AI does the building: turning those decisions into pages, assignments, and your coach — that I review and test. That’s what lets me be more nimble and adapt to improve the course so I can serve all of you well.

What that looks like — what I decided, what AI built, and what it gets you

What I decided

  • Professional growth is a practice, not an event. This course is built around the habit — something you do every week — rather than around a résumé you produce once at the end.
  • The shape of a professional is the through-line. I → T → Pi → m → comb: you start deep in one thing, then keep adding depth in others. Widening is what makes you hard to replace, and it is a deliberate act rather than something that happens to you.
  • Self-directed learning is how you widen. You will teach yourself something outside what you already know, on purpose, and show what changed.
  • Integrative solving is what widening is for. Putting things from different areas together is where the interesting problems are, and you can only do it across ground you have covered.
  • Conversations and interviews are the graded work. Explaining your thinking out loud — to me, to a TA, to your group of four — is how thinking gets exercised, and it is how I see what you know rather than what you can produce.
  • I grade the loop, not the line. What counts is the cycle you run — try, look at what happened, change something — not a single polished artifact at the end of it.
  • What every assignment is for, the guidance around it, and how it is graded are mine.

What AI built, and how we check it

  • These pages. Drafted from my design, with the parts that make them usable — the week-by-week plan you can read end to end, the terms you can hover over for a definition.
  • The Canvas side. Assignments, modules and due dates set up and held together, so nothing falls between a page and the gradebook.
  • Your AI coach, generated from this same course design — which is why it knows what you are working on, and can meet you at eleven at night when I can’t.
  • How we check it. My TAs and I click through the pages, submit the Canvas items ourselves before you ever see them, and run the coach through a student conversation to see how it answers. Where it comes out wrong, I edit it by hand.

What that combination gets you

  • A course that can change while you are in it. Because a decision becomes a built page quickly now, something that isn’t working in week 4 can be different in week 5 rather than next fall.
  • That speed is spent on you. It goes into adapting the course so it works for everyone in the room — not only for the students it already happened to fit.
  • And it is the thing you are here to learn. Directing AI to build something you designed, then checking it hard enough to stand behind it, is the same skill this course asks of you. This page is what it looks like when I do it.
