# Sprint 2 Exam - the paired half

Week 11 - Wednesday 4 November, in class - 100 points - Sprint 2 Exam

## Purpose

Learn how to ask questions to understand another person's work and help them deepen their understanding, and write down what you learned.

## Tasks

You have your partner's paper. Complete the tasks below in order. When you finish, press **Copy my answers** and paste the text into the Canvas box.

### Task 1

Select your partner's name.

*Field:* Your partner's name -> a text box with the course roster as suggestions ("Firstname L."). A name that is not on the list can still be typed.

*Beside the name field, highlighted:* **Partner with someone new today: your partner must be someone different from your Sprint 1 Exam partner.**

*Under the name field:* a tick box labelled **I partnered with someone new**, not my Sprint 1 Exam partner, with the line *If you work with the same partner as in the Sprint 1 Exam, you will not receive credit for this activity.* The tick is copied with the answers. (Added 28 Oct 2026, Prof. Sathya.)

### Task 2

Type your partner's four handwritten answers into the four boxes below, exactly as they wrote them. If you cannot read something, ask them. When all four are typed, read them back to your partner and have them tick **Checked by the writer** under the fourth box.

Each box shows the question from the paper above it.

*Box 1 label:* **1a. What step did you take, on which day, and who or what did it involve?**
*Box 2 label:* **1b. What did it produce?**
*Box 3 label:* **2. Which part of your STAR answer became more real because of it, and how?**
*Box 4 label:* **3. What is your next step, on which day, and how does it move you closer to your goal beyond Sprint 2?**

*Under the fourth box:* one tick box labelled **Checked by the writer** with the line *My partner has read all four boxes against their paper and confirms they say what they wrote.*

*Beside each box:* **Read it aloud** button, as on the CST286 page.

### Task 3

Ask your partner at least two follow-up questions about what they wrote. Write down each question you asked and what your partner said.

A follow-up question that teaches you something points at one specific thing in their answer - the day they named, the person they asked, what the step produced, the part of the STAR answer they chose, a word they used. A general question could be asked of any paper in the room, so the answer tells you nothing about this paper.

*Worked pair, shown on the page:*
- General: "Was it hard to take your first step?"
- Specific: "In 2 you said the action part of your STAR answer became more real. What can you say in an interview now that you could not say before you took the step?"

Come up with your questions in either of these two ways, or both.

**Route 1 (+): Write your own questions**

1. Read your partner's four answers again.
2. Write down every question that comes to you. Do not stop to judge or answer them. If you write a statement, change it into a question. Truly care about the questions.
3. For each question you wrote, find the exact words or phrases in their answer that it points at. Cross out any question you cannot point at.
4. Pick the two questions you are most curious about. Ask those two.

*Box label:* **The questions I wrote down**

**Route 2 (+): Use the AI Question Suggestions button**

Press the button. It reads the four answers you typed and suggests five questions, each one naming the part of the answer it comes from. Pick two and adapt to ask your partner - again your curiosity is your guide. Change the wording to make it your own.

*Button:* AI Question Suggestions. Greyed until all four boxes have text and the **Checked by the writer** tick is on. *Line beside it while greyed:* Type all four answers and have your partner tick **Checked by the writer**, then the suggestions open.
*What it does:* reads the four typed answers and returns five questions. Each question quotes the words in the answer it comes from, and asks about that. It does not suggest questions about job searching in general, about how hard the step was, or about anything that is not in the four boxes.
*If the call fails:* AI Question Suggestions is not available right now. Use "Write your own questions" above.

*Fields:* Follow-up question 1 / What I learned from my partner's response - Follow-up question 2 / What I learned from my partner's response - Follow-up question 3 (if you asked one) / What I learned from my partner's response

### Task 4

Write a summary of what you understand about the step they took. Include what you understood about their goal and how the first step will help them in reaching it. Identify at least one thing you learned from talking to them that was not on the written response.

*Box label:* **Your summary**

## Self-check

Tick each line your submission meets. Your ticks are copied with your answers; the grader verifies each one.

- [ ] My partner's name is selected · 5 pts
- [ ] All four answers are typed as my partner wrote them, not summarised · 15 pts
- [ ] My partner has ticked **Checked by the writer** under the four answers · 5 pts
- [ ] I asked at least two follow-up questions, and each one is written down · 15 pts
- [ ] Each follow-up question points at a specific part of one of the four answers, and my question says which part · 20 pts
- [ ] No follow-up question is a generic one I could ask anyone · 5 pts
- [ ] What I learned from my partner's response is written down for each question · 15 pts
- [ ] The summary says what I understood about my partner's goal and how their first step will help them reach it · 15 pts
- [ ] The summary identifies at least one thing I learned that was not on the written response · 5 pts

**What makes it strong.** Your two questions explore the part of the paper that is interesting or relevant - what the step produced in 1b, or how it made the STAR answer in 2 more real - and your summary is traceable to what your partner said when you asked, rather than repeating the paper back.

**Grading.** 100 points, the paired half of the Sprint 2 Exam. You will be asked to explain this to a peer, the TA and/or the instructor, so make sure you are comfortable with what you have written here.
