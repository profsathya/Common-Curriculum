## How to come up with your questions

Start with genuine interest in connecting with the person at a human level and learning from their life and professional journey. Looking at networking as "what can I get from this" is likely to fail most of the time - no one likes to be seen as just a means to getting something.

1. **Focus on the person and their work.** Each guest has spent years doing work you are heading toward. You will learn the most by being curious about them as individuals, how they got there, and what surprised them along the way.

2. **Be open and transparent about yourself.** Tell them a little about where you are, so they can answer in a way that connects their experience to yours.

3. **Ask follow-up questions about specific actions they took or decisions they made.** Asking about concrete moments makes the conversation more interesting for both of you and helps you learn in greater depth.

4. **Be interested in helping and giving everyone a chance to speak.** Your presence in the conversation should be a positive experience for everyone - we will go further if we care about everyone's experience.

When you write your opening questions below, aim for questions about real moments in a person's work or journey - an interesting project, a hard call, or something that changed how they think - rather than general career tips. That way, whichever guests end up in your group, each person will have their own story to tell and your follow-up questions can grow naturally from what they share.

**Two examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- "Can you tell us about an interesting project you have worked on in the past year or so?"
- "What has been challenging for you, in an interesting way, over the past year?"

Both are short and conversational, and both give the guest room to talk about something they genuinely care about. Listen closely to their answer, because your best follow-up question comes from what they just shared. When you introduce your question or follow up, you can also share a quick line about what you are working on or exploring right now so they can connect their experience to yours.

## AI feedback (page note; criteria for the boxes when the Dojo box is unticked)

- **Look back:** why the student wants to keep their earlier question or what they want to rethink after seeing who is joining, grounded in having a real small-group conversation.
- **Task 1:** one or two concrete habits the student wants to practice during the conversations (such as sharing openly, listening closely for follow-ups, or making room for classmates) and how they hope to feel at the end of the evening.
- **Task 2:**
  - Each question invites the guest to talk about their own work, decisions, or journey rather than asking for general advice.
  - Any of the guests on the list could answer from their own experience, rather than only someone in one specific role or company.
  - Different guests would naturally share different stories; the question is conversational and concise enough for a small group.

The reply is two or three sentences: first affirm what the student's draft already does well, then offer one constructive question to help them sharpen anything that still sounds generic, overly narrow, or stiff (or, if all criteria are met, invite them to consider what follow-up they might listen for). Never rewrite the student's words and never supply a question to use.
