# Demonstrate growth in your Sprint 1 goal

Week 5 · due Monday 28 September, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded assignments

## Purpose

Being able to verify your own growth yourself is what lets you steer it, instead of waiting for a review to tell you how you are doing.

**By the time you submit, you will have:**

- Shown what you can do now that you could not in week 3, held against the evidence you set for yourself in week 3.
- Put that evidence through an interview — with your AI Dojo or with a person — and written down the question that tested it hardest.
- Named what still remains, specific enough to start on in the first week of Sprint 2.

## Tasks

**Button:** Copy the questions — If you would like to work on the assignment in a Google doc without the guidance from this assignment, you can paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it. The guidance structure provided in this page is the recommended approach to ensure you are being guided and challenged along the way.

Answer each task in the box under it. The **+** under each box opens guidance, examples and a reflection question, for when you want them.

### Task 1 — Goal

Restate the growth opportunity you chose in week 3 and the milestone you set. Describe how either of them changed as you worked, and explain why.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 1.** The week-3 goal and milestone, how they stand now, and why.
*Placeholder:* In week 3 I chose … and my milestone was … Now I would say … It changed because …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- The week-3 growth opportunity and milestone are both given.
- It says how either changed, or that it did not.
- If something changed, it says what you found out that changed it.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Open your week-3 submission and copy out the growth opportunity and the milestone as you wrote them then. Underneath, write them as you would set them now. If they are the same, say so — that is an answer. If either changed, the useful part is the *why*: what you found out in the last two weeks that made the old version too broad, aimed at the wrong thing, or bigger than four weeks.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- **Changed** — Week 3: get better at explaining my work. Now: be able to present something unfinished and take questions without deferring to someone else. It changed because presenting finished work turned out to be easy; it is the unfinished part I avoid, and that is where the fear actually is.
- **Milestone changed** — I had said “present to two people outside my team”. After the first one I moved it to “present twice and ask each of them what did not land”, because doing it once told me nothing about whether I had improved.

**Reflection question.** What did you have to find out before you could set the goal properly?

### Task 2 — Evidence

Demonstrate what you learned and how it helped you grow. What can you do now that you could not in week 3? Show it against the evidence you said in week 3 would count — the data, or what the person outside this class said when they saw it.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 2.** What you can do now, and the evidence you set in week 3 held against it.
*Placeholder:* What I can do now: … The evidence I set was … What it actually showed: …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It says what you can do now that you could not in week 3, as something you did.
- It puts that against the evidence you set in week 3 — the data, or the person’s own words.
- Where the evidence came back against you, it says so.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Two halves, and the second is what makes this a demonstration rather than a claim. First: what can you do now that you could not two weeks ago? Say it as something you did, with when and where. Second: put it against the bar you set yourself in week 3. If your evidence was a person, what did they actually say — their words, not your summary of them. If it was data, give the numbers. If the evidence came back against you, write that; an honest miss is worth more here than a claim nobody could check, and it is the thing you can act on in Sprint 2.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- **What I can do now:** On 24 September I presented an unfinished prototype to two people at the Salinas meetup and answered their questions myself.
- **Against my evidence:** I said Priya would tell me what she took away. She said she understood the problem but not why I had built it that way — so half of it landed. That is not what I hoped she would say, and it tells me the part I still cannot explain is my reasoning, not my work.
- **Another** — What I can do now: ask for help within a day of getting stuck. My evidence was the waiting times I wrote down: three times in two weeks, waits of 1 day, 4 hours and 2 days. The last one was the week I was busiest, which is when I most need it.

**Reflection question.** If someone read only your evidence, would they reach the same conclusion you did?

### Task 3 — Interview

Go to your AI Dojo and ask it to interview you about this the way a hiring manager would. Ask the AI to keep asking you harder follow-up questions that test your reasoning and evidence. Practice answering clearly, including what you are still unsure about and how you would investigate it. Write down one question that tested your reasoning and what you learned from answering it. If you would rather work with a person, find someone who will question you rigorously.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 3.** The question that tested your reasoning, how you answered it, and what you learned.
*Placeholder:* The question was … I answered … What I learned was …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It includes a specific question that tested your reasoning and describes how you answered it.
- It explains what the exchange helped you confirm, revise, or identify for further investigation.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Give the AI what you wrote for task 2 and ask it to interview you one question at a time, using your answers to develop harder follow-up questions. Ask it to probe the reasons for your decisions and the evidence supporting your claims. Describe one question and how you answered it. Explain what the exchange helped you confirm, revise, or identify for further investigation.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- **With the Dojo** — It asked what I would do differently if the audience disagreed with my approach, and I had nothing. I had prepared to explain my work, not to defend it. I went back and worked out which of my choices I could actually defend and which I had made by default, and now I can say which is which.
- **With a person** — My manager asked how I would know my explanation had worked. I said “people nod”, which is not an answer. I changed my evidence to asking one person afterwards what they took away.

**Reflection question.** What would help you explain your reasoning more clearly in your next interview?

### Task 4 — Comfort

Did moving out of your comfort zone get easier as you worked at it? If yes, what made the difference; if not, why not.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 4.** Whether it got easier, and what made the difference or why not.
*Placeholder:* The first time … The most recent time … What made the difference / why not …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It compares the first time with a later time.
- It answers either way, with what made the difference or why not.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Think about the first time you did the uncomfortable thing and the most recent time. Was it different? If it got easier, what actually changed — the preparation, the people, having done it once, knowing what to expect? If it did not, say so and say why: too few repetitions, the wrong kind of practice, a bad first experience that made the second harder. Both answers are useful, and the second is often more useful than the first.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- **Yes** — The first time I could not look up from my notes. The third time I could. What made the difference was practising out loud with someone rather than rereading what I had written; the fear was about being watched, not about the material.
- **No** — It did not get easier, and I think it is because I only did it twice. Two goes is not enough to know whether the first one was a fluke or the second one was.

**Reflection question.** What would have to happen for it to get easier next sprint?

### Task 5 — Partner

Did you have a partner who helped move your work? If yes, how; if not, why not.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 5.** Whether a partner helped, and how or why not.
*Placeholder:* Yes — my partner … / No — because …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It answers either way, with the how or the why not.
- If yes, it says what the partner actually did.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Answer for what actually happened. If a partner helped, say what they did — the question they asked, the thing they spotted, the meeting that made you finish something. If you did not have one, or it never got going, say why: no one available, schedules that did not meet, you did not ask, it did not seem worth it. Both answers tell us something we need for the rest of the semester.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- **Yes** — Diego asked me why I was practising on people who already knew the project. That one question is why I went to the meetup instead.
- **No** — We swapped goals in week 4 and then never met. I work Thursdays and Saturdays, the two times we tried did not come off, and I did not chase it.

**Reflection question.** What would you want from a partner in Sprint 2?

### Task 6 — AI

If you used AI, explain how you used it, how you checked its responses, and what you found. Describe the decisions you made and how they affected the work.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 6.** How you used AI, what your checks showed, and how your decisions affected the work.
*Placeholder:* I used it to … I checked … by … I found … I decided … This affected the work by …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It says what you asked for and what came back, or that you did not use AI.
- If AI was used, it describes a specific check and what the check showed.
- It describes a decision you made and how it affected the work.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Describe what you asked the AI to do and how you used its responses. Include a specific response you checked against a source, a person, or your own observations, and explain what the check showed. Describe a decision you made about the work and its effect. If you did not use AI for this, say so; that is a complete answer.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- I used it to rehearse and to generate questions I might be asked. It was good at the questions and bad at judging my answers — it called an answer strong that Priya later did not follow at all, so I stopped using it to tell me whether something landed and started using it only to make the questions harder. What I brought was the room: the actual people, and knowing which of their reactions mattered.

**Reflection question.** How did checking the AI responses inform your decisions?

### Task 7 — Remains

Identify what still remains — where you are not yet where you want to be on this, and what you will do about it in Sprint 2.

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 7.** What still remains, and your next step in Sprint 2.
*Placeholder:* I am still short on … In Sprint 2 I will …

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- It names something specific you are still short on.
- It names a next step you could start in the first week of Sprint 2.

**Guidance (behind the +).** Two things: where you are still short, and what you will do about it. Be specific enough to start in the first week of Sprint 2. “Keep practising” is not a next step; “present at the October meetup and ask two people afterwards what they took away” is. If the goal turned out to be bigger than you thought, say so — a goal that grew as you worked on it is a sign you understand it better, not a sign you failed at it.

**Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy**

- I can explain what I built. I still cannot defend why I built it that way when someone pushes, which is the harder half and the one interviews actually test. In Sprint 2 I am taking one design decision from the project and writing the case for it and the case against it, then putting both to Priya.

**Reflection question.** Is what remains a bigger thing than what you started with?

## Criteria

**Check it before you submit**

- It restates the growth opportunity and the milestone from week 3, and says how either changed and why.
- It says what you can do now that you could not in week 3.
- It shows that against the evidence you set in week 3 — the data, or what the person outside this class said.
- It includes an interview question that tested your reasoning, how you answered it, and what you learned.
- The comfort zone question is answered either way — what made the difference, or why not.
- The partner question is answered either way — the how, or the why not.
- It says how you used AI, or that you did not.
- It names what still remains, and what you will do about it in Sprint 2.

**What makes it strong** — what changed in 1 is a change in the goal itself, not a change of wording; task 2 shows the thing rather than reporting it — a recording, a message you sent, a note from the person who saw it, a number you can point at — and where the evidence came back against you, it says so; the interview in 3 tests your reasoning and evidence, and your account explains what you confirmed, revised, or still need to investigate; tasks 4 and 5 are honest when the answer is no, and a no with a real reason is worth as much here as a yes; what you name in 7 is specific enough to start on in the first week of Sprint 2.

**Grading** — 100 points, in Graded assignments. The Sprint 1 Exam on Wednesday works from these same questions: three of them are handed out, you answer two — question 2 and one of the others — closed book, about half a page each. What you write here is your preparation for it.
