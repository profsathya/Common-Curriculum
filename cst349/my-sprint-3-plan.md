## Before you write your plan (page note: a box between task 1 and task 2; the description and the link on the left, a space for the video on the right)

**The Power of Small Wins** · Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer · Harvard Business Review, 2011

The authors read nearly 12,000 diary entries written by people at work at the end of each day. On the days people felt most motivated, the most common reason was that they had made progress in work that mattered to them, even when the progress was small. A quality assurance plan helps you see that progress, because it names in advance what your work should produce and what it should change.

[Read the article](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins)

### Task 2 — My quality assurance plan

Write three or four lines that describe quality work toward your goal, each with its points, adding to 100. For each line, think about what you will do, what it will produce, and what it changes for someone, including you. At least one line must show what it changes. *(Write each line so that your partner can say yes or no to it from what your report will show them. "I worked hard" cannot be checked; "I gave the demo to three people and have their questions written down" can. A change for you counts when your partner can see it: "I can now answer the question that stumped me in the first demo, and my report gives my answer" can be checked; "I feel more confident" cannot.)*

**Two example plans, to shape your thinking — not models to copy** (page note: shown under the question, outside the +, the two plans side by side on a wide screen; the line about what the work changes is marked in each)

*The demo plan*
1. I gave the demo to three people outside my major, and my report names them and the dates · 25 pts
2. My report lists every question they asked, with the ones I could not answer marked · 25 pts
3. I changed one thing in the demo because of a question, and my report shows the before and after · 25 pts
4. I can now answer the question that stumped me in the first demo, and my report gives my answer · 25 pts — *what it changes, for me*

*The help-desk plan*
1. The chart of lockouts per department per week is in my report · 25 pts
2. My report contains the proposal I made to my supervisor, with the department and the test · 25 pts
3. My report records what my supervisor said about it, in her words · 20 pts
4. My supervisor decided whether to run the test, and my report says what she decided and what happens next · 30 pts — *what it changes, for my team*
