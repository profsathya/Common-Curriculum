# My Sprint 3 plan: the goal, how I will stay informed, my first step, and how I should be graded

Week 11 · due Monday 9 November, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded items

## Purpose (page note: three-box arrow chain, Builds on → This goal → Leads to)

**Builds on.** You took a first step toward your Sprint 2 goal and saw which part of your interview answer got closer to real ([My first step, taken](my-first-step-taken.html)).

**This goal.** Learn how to set a goal you can keep moving on your own: a goal, a way to keep learning about it, one step, and the terms you want to be judged by.

**Leads to.** Two classmates grade your Sprint 3 work against the terms you write here ([My two graders](my-two-graders.html), [My Sprint 3 report](my-sprint-3-report.html)).

## Tasks

Sprint 3 has no exam questions handed to you and no form that tells you what to do each week. You set the goal, you choose how to stay informed, you take the step, and you write the terms you will be graded on. Two classmates then grade you against those terms.

**Button:** Copy the questions — Paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it.

Each **+** opens an example, more examples if you want them, and a reflection question. Nothing under a **+** is required.

- **My Sprint 3 goal** — give my CST 338 project demo to three people outside my major and write down every question they ask.
- **How it follows from Sprint 2** — my Sprint 2 goal was to be able to say I presented my work to people who do not write code; my first step got me one listener, and three is the number in my end-of-semester box.

**More examples** (page note: a click-to-open inside the +, closed by default)

**Reflection (behind the +).** If you reached this goal, which sentence of your interview answer could you say without the word "imagined"?

### Task 4 — How I should be graded

Write the terms you want to be graded on for Sprint 3: three or four lines that a classmate could check against your Sprint 3 report, each with the points it is worth, adding up to 100. *(Write each line so that someone who is not you can say yes or no to it from what you will show them. "I worked hard" cannot be checked; "I gave the demo to three people and have their questions written down" can. Two classmates will grade you on these exact lines, so write the lines you would be proud to be held to.)*

*Box label:* **Your answer to task 4.** Three or four checkable lines, each with its points, adding to 100.
*Placeholder:* 1. … · … pts ⏎ 2. … · … pts ⏎ 3. … · … pts ⏎ 4. … · … pts

**Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI**

- Three or four lines, and the points add up to 100.
- Each line is something a classmate could check from your report, yes or no, without having to trust you.
- At least one line is about what the work produced, not only that you did it.

*The + reads:* An example, more examples, and a question

**An example, to shape your thinking — not a model to copy**

1. I gave the demo to three people outside my major, and my report names them and the dates · 30 pts
2. My report lists every question they asked, with the ones I could not answer marked · 30 pts
3. I changed one thing in the demo because of a question, and my report says what and why · 25 pts
4. My report says what I will do with the questions I could not answer · 15 pts

**More examples** (page note: a click-to-open inside the +, closed by default)

1. The airline table is loaded with the full file, and my report shows the row count · 25 pts · 2. My report names one query that took more than a second, with its time · 25 pts · 3. My report shows the change I made and the time after · 35 pts · 4. My report says what I would try next if the time were still too long · 15 pts

**Reflection (behind the +).** Which of your four lines would be hardest to fake, and is that the one with the most points?
