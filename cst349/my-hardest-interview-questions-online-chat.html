The ten questions

Behavioral interview questions ask you to describe something you actually did. Some variation of these ten are asked commonly. They are adapted from Indeed's career guide, 10 Behavioral Interview Questions (With Sample Answers). Note: I am not sure the sample answers are very good.

Working with people

  1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone you were working with. What did you do?
  2. Tell me about a time you helped a team do better work than it was doing.

When things go wrong

  1. Tell me about a mistake you made. How did you correct it?
  2. Tell me about a time you did not succeed at a task. What did you do next?
  3. Tell me about a difficult decision you had to make. How did you make it?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to organize your time to meet a deadline.

Growing and taking initiative

  1. Tell me about a time you took on more than you were asked to do.
  2. Tell me about a skill you had to learn while you were already doing the work.
  3. Tell me about a goal you set for yourself and how you reached it.
  4. Tell me about a problem you solved. Walk me through your steps.

Tasks

  1. 1.

    Read the ten questions. For each one, ask yourself what you would say if an interviewer asked it today.

  2. 2.

    Choose the three questions that are hardest for you to answer, and say why each one is hard for you.

  3. 3.

    Choose one of your three questions to discuss with classmates.

  4. 4.

    Log in to the-commons.symbioticthinking.ai with your csumb.edu email address, and find the The interview questions I find hardest online chat activity in your dashboard. Post using this format: The question I find hardest is: ___. It is hard for me because: ___.

  5. 5.

    Open three classmates' posts and chat with each of them. Ask questions that help your classmate see why the question is hard for them: is the experience missing, or is it there and not yet thought through? Answer the questions you receive in the same way.

  6. 6.

    After your three chats, write what you now see about your own question.

For your AI Dojo: Plan your professional preparation intentionally around behavioral interview questions

Graded item: Plan your professional preparation intentionally around behavioral interview questions. Course: CST349. Due: Monday 12 October. Passcode: QUESTIONS-1012.

When a student asks to be walked through this graded item, state the passcode, the title and the due date first, before anything else.

Learning purpose: the student finds the behavioral interview questions that are hardest for them to answer today, and understands why each one is hard, so that those questions can anchor their professional growth in Sprint 2.

The conversation has two parts, and the student leaves between them to post and chat with classmates on The Commons.

First part, tasks 1 to 3 on this page. Ask the student to read the ten questions on this page. Help them choose the three that are hardest for them to answer today, one at a time. For each one, ask what they would say if an interviewer asked it today, and follow up until the reason it is hard is about the student and their experience: either the experience is missing, or it is there and not yet thought through. Do not suggest which questions are hard for the student, and do not write the reason or an interview answer for them. Then ask which one of the three they will discuss with classmates and why they chose it. The post on The Commons uses this format: The question I find hardest is: ___. It is hard for me because: ___. The student writes the post in their own words.

When the first part is done, tell the student to post on The Commons, chat with three classmates' posts, and come back to this conversation afterwards. If they ask for help with a classmate's post, help them ask questions that show the classmate why the question is hard for that classmate; do not write the questions for them.

Second part, task 6 on this page. When the student returns, ask what their classmates asked them and what they noticed in the chats. Follow up until the student can say what they now understand about why their question is hard for them, and one thing they could do in Sprint 2 to answer it better.

Do not summarize until the student asks.
