CST349 · Fall 2026 · Sprint 2 · Week 8 · Set your goal

# My Sprint 2 goal

Graded item · Due Date: Monday 19 October · 100 pts

This graded item will affect your final grade, and the late penalty applies.

## Purpose

Turn the interview question you found hardest into a goal you can act on this semester, by imagining the answer you want to give and working backwards to what you will do this week.

## How this form works

Start from the end. Write the answer you would be proud to give to your question, then ask what would have to be true for that answer to be real, and keep asking until you reach something you can do this week. Planning this way focuses your time and energy on the experiences that will matter, instead of taking whatever challenge comes next. You start it in class on Wednesday 14 October, and the worked examples beside the form show what each box looks like filled in.

☑ **I would like to use the AI Dojo to work out my goal.** With this ticked, the Dojo steps appear below with a starter prompt; your Dojo asks you questions until each box is precise, and you copy its final version into the boxes. Untick it to work on your own with the AI feedback button under each box. (Page note: ticked when the page opens; the tick is remembered on this browser.)

**Before you start with the AI Dojo:** this route works only if the course Google Doc is linked in your AI Dojo project. Check that it is linked, so that your Dojo works from the latest information about the course. (Page note: shown as a bright reminder while the box is ticked.)

## The form

(Page note: the form takes the left two thirds of the page; the worked examples sit beside it on the right and move below it on a narrow screen. Each box has a short label above it and a one-line starter inside the label, never a placeholder.)

### Part 1 · The answer I want to be able to give

1. **My question.** The question you chose in the week-7 graded item as the one that gives you the biggest opportunity for growth. Copy it here in full.

   **My question is ...** The behavioral interview question, in full.

2. **The answer I want to be true by the end of this semester.** Write it the way you would say it in an interview, in STAR form: the situation, what you had to do, what you did, and what came of it. If you do not have this experience yet, write the one you want to have. That is what the rest of the form is for.

   **Situation ...** Where I was and what was going on.

   **Task ...** What I had to do, and why it mattered to someone other than me.

   **Action ...** What I did, step by step.

   **Result ...** What came of it, and what I know now that I did not know before.

   ☐ **This answer is imagined. The experience in it has not happened yet, and my plan below is how I will make it real.** (Page note: a tick under the four STAR boxes; when ticked, the copied text carries the line "This answer is imagined." under the answer.)

### Part 2 · What I will go deeper in

The answer above rests on something you can do well. Name the one technical thing you will go deeper in so that the answer holds up when the interviewer asks a follow-up question. One skill, tool or topic, narrow enough that you could say how you will know you have gone deeper.

   **I will go deeper in ...** The one technical skill, tool or topic, and what "deeper" looks like.

### Part 3 · What I will reach across to

Every answer the interviewers like in the week-7 samples has a second part: the candidate went past their own area. They explained something to a person who did not share their background, took on a role nobody had assigned, or learned a domain so they could build for it. Name the one thing you will reach across to: a kind of person, a kind of communication, a domain or a role.

   **I will reach across to ...** The people, the communication, the domain or the role, and what reaching across looks like in my answer.

The test: if you could learn it from the same textbook, course or tutorial as Part 2, it belongs in Part 2. Reaching across is a different kind of thing.

(Page note: these three short examples sit beside Part 3, in a small panel of their own.)

- *Deeper:* writing SQL queries that run fast on a large table. *Across:* the nurse who will use the report, so that the query answers the question she actually asks.
- *Deeper:* building a React front end. *Across:* presenting the demo to the client and taking their questions without a teammate beside me.
- *Deeper:* reading network traffic in Wireshark. *Across:* explaining a security risk to a manager who has to decide whether to spend money on it.

### Part 4 · Working backwards

Start from the answer in Part 1 and work back to this week. Each box is one experience you will build, written so that someone else could tell whether it happened.

1. **In the next 2 years.** The experience that makes the whole answer real: an internship, a shipped project, a role in a team, a person you worked with.

   **In the next 2 years I will have ...** The experience, and where it would happen.

2. **In the next 6 months.** By the end of spring. The part of that experience that has to be under way by then.

   **In the next 6 months I will have ...** What will be real by the end of spring.

3. **In the next 3 months.** By the end of this semester. Two of these are already on the Sprint 2 calendar: on Wednesday 21 October you give your answer aloud to a classmate and take their questions, and by Monday 2 November you report on the first step you took. Say what you want to have done by December, beyond those two.

   **By the end of this semester I will have ...** What will be real by December, with the Sprint 2 milestones in it.

4. **This week.** The first step, before Wednesday 21 October. Something you can do in the next seven days that moves the 3-month box: a message sent, a meeting booked, a repository created, a first chapter read. In the week-9 graded item you write one line about this step, taken or not, and what happened.

   **This week I will ...** The one step, and the day I will do it.

## Worked examples (shown beside the form)

Each example is written in the form's own shape, with the parts in the order above. They are built from the kinds of goals this class wrote in Sprint 1, one for each of the four week-7 questions. Every answer in them is imagined, the way yours may be.

**Example A · From a goal about talking to people**

- *My question:* Tell me about a time you took on work outside your role or your area because the team needed it.
- *The answer I want to be true (imagined):* Situation: our capstone team had built a working app for a campus food pantry, and the pantry coordinator asked for a demo at a staff meeting. Task: the teammate who presented got a job interview the same morning; someone had to give the demo and take questions from eight staff who do not write code. Action: I volunteered, because I knew the app best. I asked the coordinator which two things the staff worried about, rewrote the demo around those, and practiced twice with a teammate interrupting me with questions. Result: the staff asked to start using the app the following week, and one of them asked for a change we had not thought of. I found out that presenting is preparation, not personality.
- *I will go deeper in:* the full stack of my own capstone project, so that I can answer any question about how it works without looking.
- *I will reach across to:* non-technical users. Running a demo and a question session with people who will use what I built, not with classmates.
- *In the next 2 years I will have:* presented a working project to the people who use it, at least twice, once in an internship or a job.
- *In the next 6 months I will have:* given the capstone demo to a real partner, and run one user interview on my own.
- *By the end of this semester I will have:* given my STAR answer aloud on 21 October, taken my first step by 2 November, and presented one project I already built to three people outside my major, with their questions written down.
- *This week I will:* ask two people outside my major, by Friday, if I can show them my CST 338 project for fifteen minutes next week.

**Example B · From a goal about explaining technical work**

- *My question:* Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone who may have had more knowledge about the subject than you did.
- *The answer I want to be true (imagined):* Situation: as an IT intern, I was asked to roll out a password policy that the senior administrator had written. Task: I noticed from the help-desk log that most lockouts came from one group of staff, and I thought the policy would make it worse. Action: before I said anything, I asked the administrator why the policy was written that way and listened. Then I pulled three months of lockout data, showed which group it came from, and proposed a change for that group only, with a way to check after a month. Result: she kept the policy for everyone else, took my change for that group, and lockouts for them dropped by half. I learned to disagree with data and a question, not an opinion.
- *I will go deeper in:* reading and analyzing logs with Python and a spreadsheet, so that I can find a pattern and show it in one chart.
- *I will reach across to:* people with authority over a decision. Asking why first, then presenting evidence to someone senior to me and proposing a test instead of a verdict.
- *In the next 2 years I will have:* changed one decision at an internship or a job by bringing evidence to someone senior.
- *In the next 6 months I will have:* presented a finding from real data to someone who did not ask for it, at a job, a club or a lab, and proposed a change.
- *By the end of this semester I will have:* given my STAR answer aloud on 21 October, taken my first step by 2 November, and analyzed one real log or dataset from my job or a club and shown the pattern to the person who owns it.
- *This week I will:* ask my supervisor at the help desk, on Tuesday, whether I can look at the ticket log for the last three months.

**Example C · From a goal about building a project**

- *My question:* Tell me about a time you had to learn something unfamiliar to finish work that other people were depending on.
- *The answer I want to be true (imagined):* Situation: a student club that tutors high-school students asked our team to build a tool that answers tutors' questions from the club's own guides. Task: I took the part that finds the right passage before the AI model answers, which I had never built, and three teammates' parts depended on it being ready in three weeks. Action: I built the smallest version first, with ten of the club's real questions as my test, and wrote down the answer each one should find. When half of them found the wrong passage, I read about how documents are split and chunked, changed the split, and re-ran the ten. Result: nine of ten found the right passage, the team connected their parts on time, and the tutors used it during the fall. I learned to start from a test I can run, not from a tutorial.
- *I will go deeper in:* building a retrieval step in Python that finds the right passage before the model answers, tested with questions whose right answer I know.
- *I will reach across to:* the people whose questions the tool answers. Collecting their real questions and their judgement of what a correct answer is, before I build.
- *In the next 2 years I will have:* shipped a retrieval-based tool that a group outside the class uses, with a test set that came from them.
- *In the next 6 months I will have:* built a working version for one real group and had them judge twenty answers.
- *By the end of this semester I will have:* given my STAR answer aloud on 21 October, taken my first step by 2 November, and built the smallest version with ten real questions and a score.
- *This week I will:* message the tutoring club's lead on Wednesday to ask for ten questions tutors actually get asked.

**Example D · From a goal about a technical skill**

- *My question:* Tell me about a time you noticed that the project was not going in the right direction and you had to go above and beyond to get it on the right track.
- *The answer I want to be true (imagined):* Situation: in a course project, four of us were building a database-backed inventory app for a small store. Task: two weeks before the deadline, every page worked alone but the reports took a minute to load, and nobody owned the database. Action: I took it on. I learned how to read the query plan, found that two reports scanned the whole table, and added the right index for each, measuring the time before and after. Then I went to the store owner with the three reports and asked which one she opened every morning, and made that one load first. Result: the reports loaded in under a second, we delivered on time, and the owner started using the morning report. I learned that the right fix comes from asking who is waiting for it.
- *I will go deeper in:* SQL on a real database, far enough to read a query plan and know which index to add and why.
- *I will reach across to:* the owner of the business or process the data describes. Asking them which question matters most before I decide what to make fast.
- *In the next 2 years I will have:* taken ownership of the data layer in a team project at a job or an internship, with a measured improvement I can name.
- *In the next 6 months I will have:* improved one slow query in a project that someone else depends on, with the time before and after.
- *By the end of this semester I will have:* given my STAR answer aloud on 21 October, taken my first step by 2 November, and built one database with real data from a club or a job where I can measure a query.
- *This week I will:* set up PostgreSQL on my laptop on Saturday and load one public dataset of at least a hundred thousand rows.

## AI feedback (page note; the criteria the button under each box checks when the Dojo box is unticked)

- My question: one of the four week-7 questions, or a behavioral question in the same form, in full.
- Situation, Task, Action, Result: each box carries its own part; Task says why it mattered to someone other than the student; Action is what the student did, in steps; Result says what came of it and what the student knows now.
- I will go deeper in: one skill, tool or topic, not a field; a sentence on what deeper looks like.
- I will reach across to: a kind of person, communication, domain or role; if the text is a second skill or tool of the same kind as Part 2, ask who the answer is for or who the student would have to talk to.
- 2 years and 6 months: one experience each, written so that someone else could tell whether it happened.
- 3 months: the two Sprint 2 milestones and one thing beyond them.
- This week: one action, with a day, that can be done before Wednesday 21 October.

The reply is two or three sentences: what the box already does, then the one thing that would make it precise. No rewriting of the student's box.
