Sprint 1 Exam — the paired half

Week 6 · Wednesday 30 September, in class · 100 points · Sprint 1 Exam

Purpose

Questioning someone closely about work they did themselves, and writing down what they actually understand, is the same skill you are graded on when it is your turn to be questioned.

Tasks

You have your partner's paper in front of you. Work down this page, then press Copy my answers at the bottom and paste the text into the Canvas box.

  • Swap and type. Exchange papers with a partner and type their handwritten answers into the AI form. Both students do this at the same time.
  • Then the conversation. Question your partner about what they wrote — at least two follow-up questions — and write a summary of how they understand their own work. Take turns, so each of you is interviewed once.
  1. 1.Write down who wrote the paper and who is typing it.
  2. 2.Say which second question your partner chose. Question 2 is on every paper; each student then chose question 4 or question 7.
    Which question did your partner choose?
  3. 3.Type your partner's handwritten answer to question 2. Type what they wrote, not your version of it. If a word is unreadable, ask them.
    Question 2, as it was set in Demonstrate growth in your Sprint 1 goalDemonstrate what you learned and how it helped you grow. What can you do now that you could not in week 3? Show it against the evidence you said in week 3 would count — the data, or what the person outside this class said when they saw it.
  4. 4.Type your partner's handwritten answer to the question they chose.
  5. 5.Question your partner about what they wrote. Ask at least two follow-up questions, and write down each question and the short version of what they said back.

    The suggestions are a starting point for your own questions, not a list to read out. You can ask something better. Type both answers above first.

    Ask your own — what a follow-up question is doing
    • Pick one claim in their answer and ask how they know it.
    • Ask what they would have to see for the answer to be wrong.
    • Ask them to say the same thing again without the words they used the first time.
    • Ask what they tried that is not written on the paper.
  6. 6.Write a summary of how your partner understands their own work: what they can account for, what they were less sure about, and what the follow-up questions showed that the paper did not.

Criteria

Check it before you submit — the same list your partner runs on yours
  • Both names are there, and the question your partner chose is marked.
  • Their answer to question 2 is typed as they wrote it, not summarised.
  • Their answer to question 4 or question 7 is typed as they wrote it.
  • At least two follow-up questions are written down, each with what your partner said back.
  • The follow-up questions ask about something in their answer, rather than starting a new topic.
  • The summary says what your partner can account for and what they were less sure about.
  • The summary reports what they said rather than judging whether they are right.

What makes it strong — the follow-up questions in 5 go at the part of the answer in 3 or 4 that carries the most weight, and the summary in 6 is traceable to what your partner actually said when you asked, rather than repeating the paper back.

Grading — 100 points, the paired half of the Sprint 1 Exam. You will be asked to explain this to a peer, the TA and/or the instructor, so make sure you are comfortable with what you have written here.
