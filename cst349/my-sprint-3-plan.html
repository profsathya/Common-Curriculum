Before you write your plan

The Power of Small Wins

Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer · Harvard Business Review, 2011

The authors read nearly 12,000 diary entries written by people at work at the end of each day. On the days people felt most motivated, the most common reason was that they had made progress in work that mattered to them, even when the progress was small. A quality assurance plan helps you see that progress, because it names in advance what your work should produce and what it should change.

  1. 2.Write three or four lines that describe quality work toward your goal, each with its points, adding to 100. For each line, think about what you will do, what it will produce, and what it changes for someone, including you. At least one line must show what it changes. (Write each line so that your partner can say yes or no to it from what your report will show them. "I worked hard" cannot be checked; "I gave the demo to three people and have their questions written down" can. A change for you counts when your partner can see it: "I can now answer the question that stumped me in the first demo, and my report gives my answer" can be checked; "I feel more confident" cannot.)
