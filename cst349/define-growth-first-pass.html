Define what growth means for you — first pass

Week 3 · due Monday 14 September, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded assignments

Purpose

Practice choosing what you need to improve and deciding how you will check your progress. Choose one growth goal for this sprint, set a milestone for week 5, and identify resources that will help you work toward it.

Tasks

If you would like to work on the assignment in a Google doc without the guidance from this assignment, you can paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it. The guidance structure provided in this page is the recommended approach to ensure you are being guided and challenged along the way.

Answer the six questions in your own words. Each + opens guidance and examples to help you work through the question, along with a box for your answer and a reflection question.

  1. 1.What will you do differently in Sprint 1 because of something you learned in Sprint 0? Identify the activity where you learned it: What Are My Priorities, Read the Market, Know What Makes Me Tick, or Find Your Gap. Explain what happened in that activity and the change you will make.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Reread what you submitted for the four Sprint 0 activities. Choose an observation that gives you a reason to change how you work. Write a change you can begin this week.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    In Read the Market, I found that 11 of my 15 postings mentioned working with people outside engineering. I will record those responsibilities alongside the technical skills when I read postings.

    My time record showed that I spent the hour before work switching between apps. I will use that hour for one chosen task and keep my phone in another room.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • The Sprint 0 activity is identified — What Are My Priorities, Read the Market, Know What Makes Me Tick, or Find Your Gap.
    • What happened in that activity is explained.
    • The Sprint 1 change follows from something recorded in that activity.

    Reflection

    What did the activity confirm or change about your understanding of how you work? What remains uncertain?

  2. 2.Which growth opportunity will you work on first this sprint? List two or three possibilities and describe what you would do differently for each. Choose one and explain how it would challenge you to move beyond your comfort zone. Explain how developing this ability could help your application stand out among 100 applications.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    For each possibility, describe an action you would practice. Compare those actions with how you work now, then choose the opportunity you want to pursue. Explain what you could show in an application if you developed that ability.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    I could build another project alone, explain unfinished work to people outside my team, or contact people in roles I want to understand. I choose explaining unfinished work because I usually wait until everything is finished before asking for feedback. A recorded explanation and the changes I make after questions could show how I communicate my work.

    I could practice data structures, ask for help sooner, or write about my projects. I choose asking for help sooner because I sometimes spend days stuck before talking to someone. Keeping a record of how I asked for help and used it would give me a concrete collaboration example to discuss in an application.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Two or three growth opportunities are compared, with an action for each.
    • The chosen opportunity includes an explanation of the challenge.
    • The chosen opportunity includes its possible value in an application.

    Reflection

    What mattered most to you when choosing between the possibilities?

  3. 3.Why is your chosen growth opportunity the highest priority for you? Use findings from Read the Market and Find Your Gap to support your choice. Quote a relevant phrase from the postings you read and report how often you found it. Explain what improving this ability would change for you personally.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Open Read the Market and Find Your Gap. Find the posting evidence and the observation about your own work that support this choice. Then explain what you would be able to do differently if you improved.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    Eleven of the 15 postings I reviewed included the phrase “communicate with non-technical stakeholders.” In Find Your Gap, I recorded that I had only presented to classmates who knew my project. I want to explain my work to someone unfamiliar with it and answer their questions myself.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • A phrase from the postings is quoted, with how often it appeared.
    • Findings from Find Your Gap support the choice.
    • Its personal value is explained.

    Reflection

    Would you still choose this goal if the postings you read did not mention it? Explain why.

  4. 4.What can you already show about your chosen growth opportunity, and what are you unsure about? Describe work or an experience that shows your current ability. If you have nothing to show yet, say so. Explain any specific uncertainty about what you can do.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Choose work or an experience relevant to your goal. Describe what it shows about your current ability. Then identify what you have not yet checked or cannot tell from that example.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    I presented a class project to people who already knew it. I have not checked whether I can explain the project to someone unfamiliar with it or respond when someone disagrees.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It includes what can be shown, including if there is nothing yet.
    • It includes any specific uncertainty.

    Reflection

    What does your example show about your ability? What does it leave uncertain?

  5. 5.What will you be able to show or do by the end of week 5? Describe a milestone and identify the data or a person outside this class you will use to check whether you reached it.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Choose something you can check by the end of week 5. Decide what you will record or whom you will ask. Explain how that information will help you judge your progress, including if you have not reached the milestone.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    By week 5, I will explain an unfinished project to two people outside my team and answer their questions myself. I will ask each person to describe what they understood and compare their descriptions with what I intended to explain.

    When I get stuck, I will ask for help within one day on three occasions. I will record when I became stuck and when I asked, then use the record to check whether I met that target.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • The week-5 milestone is stated.
    • It has a way to check progress using data or a person outside the class.

    Reflection

    How will your check distinguish progress from something you still need to work on? What might it leave uncertain?

  6. 6.Which three resources will you learn from? Identify each resource so another person could find it. For each, write one sentence explaining how it will help you work toward your goal. The AI Dojo is available to everyone and does not count as one of the three.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Open each resource or learn enough about the person or group to see how it could help with your goal. Identify something you could learn or practice with it, and use that connection in your explanation.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    Examples of resource choices: a specific chapter with speaking exercises you can practice; a person who has agreed to listen to an explanation and ask questions; a group that offers short practice presentations. In your answer, include the actual chapter, person, or group you have found.

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Three resources are included, each identified so another person could find it.
    • Each has a reason it will help.
    • The AI Dojo is additional to those three.

    Reflection

    If you had less time than expected, which resource would you stop using first, and why?

Criteria

Check it before you submit
  • The Sprint 1 change follows from something recorded in one of the four Sprint 0 activities.
  • Two or three growth opportunities are compared, with an action for each. The chosen opportunity includes an explanation of the challenge and its possible value in an application.
  • The priority is supported by posting evidence, how often it appeared, and findings from Find Your Gap. Its personal value is explained.
  • The starting point includes what can be shown, including if there is nothing yet, and any specific uncertainty.
  • The week-5 milestone has a way to check progress using data or a person outside the class.
  • Three identifiable resources are included, each with a reason it will help. The AI Dojo is additional to those three.

What makes it strong — the change in 1 comes from something that actually happened in the activity, not a general resolution; the possibilities in 2 are genuinely different from each other, so there was a real choice to make; the phrase in 3 is quoted from postings you read yourself rather than a general claim about the market; task 4 is honest about what you cannot show yet — this is week 3, and having little to show is the starting point, not a failing; the data or the person in 5 could show that you have not reached the milestone; the resources in 6 are ones you have actually opened.

Grading — This assignment is worth 100 points in Graded assignments. Your goal should be your own choice, and your milestone should make it possible to check your progress. This is a first version that you will test and revise. Be prepared to explain your responses to a peer, the TA, or the instructor.

What that looks like — what he decided, what AI built, and how it is checked

What he decided

  • The purpose and the six tasks are his design from 8 September 2026; the wording was revised on 18 September 2026 so that each question carries everything an answer needs, and he approved the revision. Task 1 is the sprint-opening look back — every sprint’s first graded item starts with one. Nothing on this page reorders or adds to the six.
  • The two layers. The tasks stand on their own; everything under a + is optional. A student who copies the tasks into a document and answers them there has done the whole assignment.
  • No categories for growth. Task 2 never splits “technical” from “soft” skills. Naming the categories would do the choosing for you and tell you which answer is expected; asking for two or three candidates and then choosing on the comfort zone leaves the choice yours.
  • Uncertainty is the starting point. Task 4 asks what you are not sure about because that is what the sprint goes after. Having nothing to show in week 3 is a starting point, never a failing.
  • The check-before-you-submit list is one list, two uses — you run it on your own draft, and your partner runs the same list on theirs in the week-4 session.

What AI built

  • The guidance under each + — a process for thinking the question through, rather than more explanation of it.
  • The examples — more than one per task, so that no single one becomes the thing to copy. They are illustrations, not answers.
  • The reflection questions and the criteria notes the AI feedback button reads from.

How it is checked

  • Every page is written against the course’s own writing rules and then read by him; where a line misses, he edits it directly.
  • The AI feedback button is held to the observable criteria for each box. It never judges you, never rewrites your answer, and never hands you wording.
  • The Check it yourself list under each box is the same criteria without the AI, so the button is never the only route.
