Define what growth means for you — first pass

Week 3 · due Monday 14 September, 11:59 p.m. · 100 points · Graded assignments

Purpose

Recognize what is the highest priority growth opportunity for you, set an initial milestone for that, develop a plan and work towards it. Being able to do this without it being provided by others is the engine for your continuous growth. You will write the first version of it in this sprint and test it.

Tasks

If you would like to work on the assignment in a Google doc without the guidance from this assignment, you can paste this into a Google doc and answer each task under it. The guidance structure provided in this page is the recommended approach to ensure you are being guided and challenged along the way.

Each + opens guidance, examples, a reflection question and a box to write in. The guidance and reflection are provided to support you to do a high quality job of answering the questions.

  1. 1.Look back: identify one thing you are going to do differently in Sprint 1 because of something you learned in Sprint 0.
    1. Which activity it came from — What Are My Priorities, Read the Market, Know What Makes Me Tick, or Find Your Gap.
    2. What you are going to do differently.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Open the four Sprint 0 activities and read what you actually submitted. Look for the moment in one of them where something did not match what you expected — the hours that went somewhere you could not name, the phrase that kept repeating in the postings, the pattern in the work that holds your attention, the blank in your audit. Pick one. Then write what you will do differently in the next four weeks because of it. One thing is enough, and it should be something you could start this week.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    From Read the Market — Eleven of the fifteen postings I read asked for something about working with people outside engineering, which I had not counted as part of the job at all. Going forward I am reading postings for what the work is like day to day, not only for the tech stack.

    From What Are My Priorities — Nearly three hours a day went to things I could not name afterwards. Going forward I am giving the hour before my shift to one thing, with the phone in another room, because that is the block I actually control.

    Reflection

    What did you assume about yourself, or about the work, that turned out not to be true?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It names which of the four Sprint 0 activities it came from.
    • It names one thing you will do differently, specific enough to start this week.
    • The change comes from something that actually happened in that activity, not a general resolution.
  2. 2.Identify the highest priority growth opportunity for you — the one you will work on first this sprint.
    1. Write down two or three things you could grow in, and for each, what you would actually have to do differently.
    2. Pick the one that will challenge you to move out of your comfort zone, and how it will help you stand out among 100 other applications.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Write two or three candidates before you choose any of them, so there is a real choice to make. For each one, write what you would actually have to do differently — not what you would know, but what you would be doing on a Tuesday. That is what turns a vague area into something you can work on and something you can be seen doing. Then choose. The one you already know how to start is rarely the one worth this sprint; the one that makes you hesitate usually is. And test the choice against the market you read in week 2: if a hiring manager had a hundred applications on the desk, what would this one give them a reason to notice?

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    Three candidates, with what I would do differently — (1) React: build two more projects on my own. (2) Explaining my work: present something unfinished to people who will push back on it. (3) Cold outreach: message five people doing the work I want and ask for twenty minutes.
    The one I picked: explaining my work. Building alone is what I already do, so it costs me nothing. Standing in front of people with something half-finished is the part I avoid, and every posting I read asked for someone who can work with people outside engineering — out of a hundred applicants with similar projects, the one who can walk a room through their thinking is the one they remember.

    Another — (1) Data structures. (2) Asking for help early. (3) Writing about what I build.
    The one I picked: asking for help early. I lose days to being stuck rather than looking like I do not know something, and the postings kept using the word collaborative. It would show up as fewer weeks lost and as a manager who can see where I am, which most junior applicants cannot demonstrate at all.

    Reflection

    Which candidate did you most want to pick, and what does that tell you?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Two or three candidates are written down.
    • Each candidate says what you would actually have to do differently.
    • The one you chose is argued as a move out of your comfort zone.
    • It says how the choice would help you stand out among 100 other applications.
  3. 3.Explain why this one is the highest priority. What in your market read and your gap points at it — with the data — and what would being better at it change for you personally?
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Two sides, and both are needed. Open your Read the Market summary and your Find Your Gap audit and pull out what actually points at this: the phrase you counted, how many postings it appeared in, the blank in your audit. Quote it rather than summarising it. Then the other side: what would being better at this change for you — something you would do, decide or stop avoiding. If the market points one way and nothing in you responds to it, that is worth knowing now, and worth writing down.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    “Communicates technical work to non-technical stakeholders” or something close to it appeared in 11 of the 15 postings I read, and my audit had a blank next to it — I have never done it outside a class. What it would change for me: I have twice let someone else present work I did most of, because I did not want to be the one answering questions. I would rather be the one answering.

    Reflection

    If the market had not pointed at this, would you still have chosen it?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It quotes something from your market read, with how often you saw it.
    • It points at the blank or the weakness in your gap audit.
    • It says what being better at this would change for you personally.
  4. 4.Describe your current understanding of where you stand. What can you already show on this? What are you not sure about?
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Two halves. First, what you can show today: a piece of work, a time you did this, something someone else saw. If the honest answer is nothing, write nothing — this is week 3, and starting from nothing is a starting point, not a failing. Second, what you are not sure about: whether you can do it under pressure, whether what you did once would hold up again, whether you would know a good version if you saw one. That second list is what the next four weeks go after.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    What I can show: one class presentation last spring, to twelve people who already knew the project. That is it.

    What I am not sure about: whether I can do it with people who will interrupt and disagree; whether I can explain something I have not finished, which is the situation I actually keep avoiding; and whether what I think is a clear explanation is clear to anyone else.

    Reflection

    What is the difference between what you can show and what you can do?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It says what you can already show, or that there is nothing yet.
    • It says what you are not sure about, specifically.
  5. 5.Set your initial milestone: write down what will count as evidence that you moved.
    1. What you will be able to show or do by week 5.
    2. What data, or which person outside this class, would confirm it.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Set the bar now, before the work starts, so at the end of the sprint you are measured against your own standard rather than a feeling. Picture the last day of week 5: what do you show, and to whom? Make it something that either happened or did not — a conversation you had, a thing you sent, a recording, a number. Then name who or what would confirm it, and choose someone who could say no: a person outside this class whose reaction you cannot predict, or data you have not seen yet. If there is no way for it to come back against you, it is not evidence yet.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    a. By week 5 I will have presented an unfinished piece of work to two people outside my team and answered their questions without deferring to anyone.
    b. Priya, who runs the meetup and has never seen my work, will tell me what she took away from it — and if what she says back is not what I meant, that is the answer.

    a. By week 5 I will have asked for help within a day of getting stuck, three separate times, with a note each time on how long I waited.
    b. The notes are the data — if the waits are still three days, I did not move.

    Reflection

    Could your evidence come back against you? If not, what would you change?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • It says what you will be able to show or do by week 5.
    • It names the data, or a person outside this class, who would confirm it.
    • There is a way for the evidence to come back against you.
  6. 6.List the three resources you will learn from, with one line each on why you think it will help you learn. The AI Dojo is a default resource for everyone and does not count as one of the three.
    Guidance, examples, a question, and a place to write

    Guidance

    Start from what you actually have to do differently, not from a search box. Look for one of each kind: something structured you can work through — a course, a book, documentation; a person who already does this and would talk to you; and a place where the work itself happens, where you can watch it done or do it — a meetup, a community, a team, a club. Spend ten minutes on each: who made it, who it was made for, and does anyone you trust point to it? Then try it for twenty minutes against your own goal before you keep it. Write one line per resource on why you think it will help you learn — not what it is.

    Examples, to shape your thinking — not models to copy

    Speaking Up Without Freaking Out (the book) — it is about the physical part of speaking, which is the part that actually stops me, rather than about slide design.

    Priya at the Salinas dev meetup — she runs lightning talks, so she can give me somewhere real to practise and tell me what landed.

    The Thursday meetup itself — five minutes in front of strangers every fortnight is the only way I will get repetitions, and I cannot get those alone.

    Reflection

    Which of the three would you drop first if you ran out of time?

    Check it yourself — the same criteria, without the AI
    • Three resources are listed, named specifically enough for someone else to find them.
    • None of them is the AI Dojo.
    • Each has one line on why you think it will help you learn.

Criteria

Check it before you submit
  • It names one Sprint 0 activity and one thing you will do differently in Sprint 1.
  • Two or three growth candidates are written down, each with what you would actually have to do differently.
  • The one you chose is argued both ways — as a move out of your comfort zone, and as something that would help you stand out among 100 applications.
  • The reason it is the highest priority points at your market read and your gap, with the data, and says what being better at it would change for you.
  • It says what you can already show on this, and what you are not sure about.
  • The milestone says what you will be able to show or do by week 5, and names the data or the person outside this class who would confirm it.
  • Three resources are listed, none of them the AI Dojo, each with a line on why you think it will help you learn.

What makes it strong — the change in 1 comes from something that actually happened in the activity, not a general resolution; the candidates in 2 are genuinely different from each other, so there was a real choice to make, and the one you picked is one you would not have picked a week ago; the data in 3 is quoted from postings you read yourself rather than a general claim about the market; task 4 is honest about what you cannot show yet — this is week 3, and having little to show is the starting point, not a failing; the person in 5b is someone who could say no; the resources in 6 are ones you have actually opened.

Grading — 100 points, in Graded assignments. Graded on whether the growth opportunity is yours and specific enough to be wrong, not on whether the picture is complete — this is a first pass and it is expected to change. You will be asked to explain this to a peer, the TA and/or the instructor, so make sure you are comfortable with the responses you have developed for this assignment.

How this page was made

The purpose and the six tasks are Prof. Sathya’s, word for word, settled on 8 September 2026. The guidance, the examples, the reflection questions and the notes behind the AI feedback button were drafted by AI from that design; he reads them and edits by hand where they miss.

What that looks like — what he decided, what AI built, and how it is checked

What he decided

  • The purpose and the six tasks are his wording from 8 September 2026. Task 1 is the sprint-opening look back — every sprint’s first graded item starts with one. Nothing on this page rewrites, reorders or adds to them.
  • The two layers. The tasks stand on their own; everything under a + is optional. A student who copies the tasks into a document and answers them there has done the whole assignment.
  • No categories for growth. Task 2 never splits “technical” from “soft” skills. Naming the categories would do the choosing for you and tell you which answer is expected; asking for two or three candidates and then choosing on the comfort zone leaves the choice yours.
  • Uncertainty is the starting point. Task 4 asks what you are not sure about because that is what the sprint goes after. Having nothing to show in week 3 is a starting point, never a failing.
  • The check-before-you-submit list is one list, two uses — you run it on your own draft, and your partner runs the same list on theirs in the week-4 session.

What AI built

  • The guidance under each + — a process for thinking the question through, rather than more explanation of it.
  • The examples — more than one per task, so that no single one becomes the thing to copy. They are illustrations, not answers.
  • The reflection questions and the criteria notes the AI feedback button reads from.

How it is checked

  • Every page is written against the course’s own writing rules and then read by him; where a line misses, he edits it directly.
  • The AI feedback button is held to the observable criteria for each box. It never judges you, never rewrites your answer, and never hands you wording.
  • The Check it yourself list under each box is the same criteria without the AI, so the button is never the only route.
