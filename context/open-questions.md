## OQ-3: Dojo as required format, or the structured prompt as the real mechanism

**Tension:** Students do Dojo-assigned work in ChatGPT and get equivalent — sometimes
better — results. The structured prompt, not the Dojo platform, appears to be what drives
depth. Requiring Dojo-format submissions adds friction without a clear quality benefit.

**Options:**

- **Require Dojo** — keeps data consistency, Dojo-specific features (constructs,
  partners), and institutional control over the AI interaction.
- **Accept any AI tool with the structured prompt** — lower friction, higher compliance,
  loses Dojo metadata.
- **Dojo preferred, alternatives accepted** — best of both, or just confusing?

**Current leaning (Feb 2026, unchanged):** the structured prompt is the intervention; the
platform is delivery. Probably not worth fighting students on format when the thinking is
what matters.

**Why it's still live for Fall:** all three Fall courses set up a Dojo in their opening
sprint, and the check-in conversations are meant to draw on what students did with AI. If
the Dojo isn't required, what the conversation samples needs another answer.

## OQ-4: How to give students moves for going deeper without adding a framework to memorize

**Tension:** Students need specific techniques for going deeper — not "go deeper" but
"ask what would have to be structurally different." Yet each new named technique compounds
the conceptual overload that spring documented.

**Options:**

- **Embed moves in assignments, not as named techniques** — "before submitting, check:
  does each why introduce a new type of cause?" rather than "use the Level Change
  Technique."
- **Teach one meta-move** — "when stuck, ask: am I repeating, or advancing?" One
  diagnostic question, applicable anywhere.
- **Reduce existing frameworks first**, then add targeted moves.

**Current leaning (Feb 2026, unchanged):** embedded, assignment-specific revision checks
at the point of submission. No new framework names.

**Why it's still live for Fall:** CF-3 on the vision page commits to introducing the
thinking frameworks gradually rather than piling them into the first sprint, which is the
"reduce first" half. It doesn't yet say what a student does when they're stuck mid-dig —
and in CST286 going deeper is the whole course, so this question lands harder there than
it did in spring.

## Settled since February

Kept short, so the reasoning stays available without inviting a re-run.

- **OQ-1 · Handwritten reflections, keep or replace.** Handwriting stays where it blocks
  AI-generated answers: the Fall courses hand-write the Sprint Exams. The visibility cost
  that made this a question is absorbed differently — depth is now verified in the
  check-in conversation rather than read off submitted text.
- **OQ-2 · Analytical depth as gate or gradient.** Gradient. The Fall mechanism grades
  distance travelled from a student's own baseline, splits the grade 50/50 between
  submission and verification in conversation, and applies verification per window. No
  student is blocked from progressing.
- **OQ-6 · Group-averaged grading.** Moot. Fall grades the individual — their own
  submission, their own check-in.
- **OQ-7 · Blueprint/Studio/Sprint metaphor in CST395.** Standardized on "Sprint," and
  CST395 is not a Fall course.
- **OQ-8 · Progressive disclosure of SDL/IS/AB.** Superseded. Fall reads the six qualities
  per activity as the course goes, rather than presenting a capability architecture up
  front — which is the phased-reveal answer, arrived at from a different direction.

**OQ-5** (what "external stakeholder" should mean in a Sprint 3) was CST395-specific and
never had options formulated; retired with the spring trackers.
